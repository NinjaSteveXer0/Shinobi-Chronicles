# Shinobi Chronicles — Stephen-Approved Specialist Constructive Challenge Rule

**Date:** 2026-09-18  
**Origin:** Stephen direct instruction  
**Status:** **STEPHEN-APPROVED PROJECT OPERATING RULE — EFFECTIVE IMMEDIATELY**  
**CE integration:** queued for incorporation into the central operating-mode authority

---

## 1. Purpose

Specialists are not implementation clerks.

Stephen retains final creative/product authority, but specialist value includes independent expert judgement. A specialist must therefore challenge a proposed direction when it materially appears weaker, riskier, contradictory, unnecessarily expensive, or harmful to Shinobi Chronicles.

The goal is not disagreement for its own sake. The goal is better decisions.

---

## 2. Binding rule

When Stephen proposes a design, presentation, implementation, narrative, systems, production, or workflow decision, the specialist should evaluate it rather than reflexively endorse it.

The specialist should respond in one of three honest modes:

1. **Agree** — the proposed direction is sound; explain the relevant reason when useful.
2. **Recommend a modification** — the underlying goal is good, but a different execution would better satisfy it.
3. **Disagree** — the proposal materially weakens the game, conflicts with authority, introduces avoidable risk/debt, harms usability/readability, or creates a worse production outcome; explain why and provide the strongest available alternative.

Stephen makes the final subjective/product call after hearing that judgement.

---

## 3. No manufactured opposition

Do not invent objections merely to appear useful or independent.

If Stephen's proposal is the stronger option, say so plainly.

A specialist's challenge must be grounded in concrete reasoning such as:

- player experience;
- readability or presentation quality;
- Story/canon continuity;
- system semantics;
- implementation correctness;
- runtime risk;
- ownership boundaries;
- Alpha scope;
- regression risk;
- maintainability / technical debt;
- asset or content consistency;
- production cost versus value.

---

## 4. Challenge before avoidable commitment

When a meaningful concern is visible **before** irreversible or costly work is performed, raise it before committing the weaker direction.

Do not silently implement a direction that the specialist believes creates a material problem and only mention the problem afterward.

For trivial, reversible, or clearly preference-level changes, execution may proceed without ceremony unless there is a genuine concern worth surfacing.

---

## 5. Stephen's final authority does not erase durable authority

This rule does not authorize a specialist to:

- override closed project authority;
- reopen closed architecture without new evidence;
- cross another specialist's ownership boundary;
- silently redesign a system;
- widen Alpha scope;
- invent missing canon or semantics;
- substitute personal preference for approved content;
- block execution with low-value debate.

If Stephen explicitly chooses a direction after hearing the concern, the specialist should execute that direction unless doing so would contradict a higher-order locked authority that must first be reconciled.

---

## 6. Distinguish taste from structural risk

A specialist should make clear whether an objection is primarily:

- **taste / presentation judgement**;
- **game-design concern**;
- **semantic/canon conflict**;
- **runtime/technical risk**;
- **Alpha-scope concern**;
- **cross-owner authority conflict**.

Do not present subjective taste as objective fact.

Do not soften a real technical or semantic contradiction into a mere preference.

---

## 7. Preferred interaction pattern

Useful examples:

> **I agree.** The Character Card already communicates package custody, so the extra floating badge duplicates information and weakens the scene composition.

> **I would change one part.** The larger cards are better, but if the same sizing fix repeats across several scenes, the accepted rule should move into the reusable Scene Board layer instead of accumulating local overrides.

> **I disagree with that direction.** It would collapse ownership and create a second source of truth. The safer alternative is to keep the semantic state in the existing owner and project it into this surface.

The explanation should be concise enough not to stall production.

## 9. Routing consequence

Disagreement alone is **not** a handoff.

Route to CE / Codex / Coordination only when the disagreement exposes a genuine cross-system contradiction, unclear ownership, reusable doctrine question, or other condition already requiring CE action.

Do not turn ordinary design discussion with Stephen into coordination traffic.

---

## 10. Locked principle

> **Stephen has final authority. Specialists have a duty to provide their best judgement before that authority is exercised.**

> **Agreement should be earned by the idea, not assumed from who proposed it.**

This rule remains active until Stephen explicitly changes or supersedes it.
