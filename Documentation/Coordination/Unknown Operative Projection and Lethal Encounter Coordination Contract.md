`observer_projection_unknown_operative`

## 3. Projection behavior

`observer_projection_unknown_operative` may be consumed by any factually human/concealed opponent or participant when the observing character lacks legitimate identity/affiliation knowledge.

The projection may expose only observer-legitimate information, for example:

- generic human operative presentation;
- currently observable clothing/equipment silhouette if separately authored;
- currently observable injuries;
- generic display label `UNKNOWN OPERATIVE`;
- no hidden faction badge;
- no true name;
- no concealed rank/title;
- no hidden Registry/source ID in player-facing presentation.

When an observer later legitimately learns the participant's identity or affiliation, presentation may change for that observer without changing the stable participant/source identity or rewriting prior Chronicle history.

Different observers may therefore legitimately see different presentation labels for the same stable participant at the same world time.

A prior Chronicle entry that records what the observer knew at the time may remain `Unknown Operative` even after later identification; later Knowledge may link that historical referent to the now-known identity rather than retroactively pretending the observer always knew.

## 7. Participant role and motive separation — CLOSED

For the major-contact confrontation, distinguish:

- **causal presence** — who is physically/socially present in the occurrence;
- **Battle participation** — who actually enters the Combat resolver as an active participant;
- **side/alignment for this resolver** — who attacks whom in this occurrence;
- **motive/relationship interpretation** — why each person acted, supported, abstained or objected.

Examples from the supplied playthrough remain valid as examples only:

Preserve:

**presence ≠ offensive participation**

**participation ≠ agreement**

**support ≠ identical motive**

**team majority ≠ automatic action authority**

The Story Scene must provide the exact active participant set for the chosen confrontation. Combat must not infer attackers from team membership alone.

## 8. Kill / Detain / Release intent semantics — CLOSED

The scene-level player choice expresses an intended resolution path. It does not itself commit the outcome.

### Kill

Choosing `Kill` means the player/active side deliberately initiates a lethal confrontation **if the Story Scene establishes a legal Battle transition**.

Preserve:

**kill intent ≠ successful kill**

For the specifically authored lethal encounter requested by issue #4:

- if the active kill side legitimately wins the Battle, the operative dies;
- that death is committed as real Chronicle history against the stable operative identity;
- no automatic post-defeat escape may silently replace the lethal victory;
- no later same-Chronicle recurrence is eligible after committed death, except non-present historical references/flashback-like presentation that does not assert survival;
- any resurrection/revival mechanism would require separate explicit future authority and cannot be inferred.

If the operative wins, escapes before lethal defeat, or the confrontation ends without the active side satisfying the lethal victory condition, he survives.

### Detain

`Detain` is not a synonym for `Kill` with a different label.

A detention route requires a Combat/resolver outcome that leaves the operative alive and legitimately captured/restrained under the authored encounter contract.

Capture success, escape, failed restraint, surrender or rescue/intervention must be resolved factually by the authored Combat/Story outcome rather than assumed from Battle victory alone.

### Release

`Release` does not launch a Battle merely because a Battle package exists elsewhere. It leaves the operative alive unless another independent occurrence changes that state.

## 9. Death / survival / injury persistence — CLOSED

The operative's life state belongs to the stable participant, not to the `Unknown Operative` projection.

Required Chronicle semantics:

- committed death is permanent historical fact for this Chronicle;
- survival is not erased merely because the operative disappears from the current scene;
- escape is an outcome/state transition, not a synonym for survival after defeat;
- capture preserves life unless a later separate occurrence changes it;
- injuries, scars, maiming or disability may persist only when the resolver emits those factual outcomes;
- Writing/UI must not add permanent injuries merely for drama if Combat did not support them;
- later opportunities involving the same operative must evaluate the persisted life/custody state before becoming eligible.

The exact Coding field names are not prescribed here. What is required is a stable causal record sufficient to answer:

- Is this operative alive?
- If dead, which occurrence killed him?
- If alive, was he captured, released, escaped or otherwise displaced?
- Which factual injuries persist?
- May a later opportunity legally include the same stable participant?
