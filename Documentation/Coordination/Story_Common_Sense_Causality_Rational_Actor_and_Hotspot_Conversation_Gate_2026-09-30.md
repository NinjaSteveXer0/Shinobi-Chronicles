# Shinobi Chronicles — Story Common-Sense Causality, Rational-Actor + Hotspot Conversation Gate

**Date:** 2026-09-30  
**Owner:** Stephen / CE / Codex / Coordination / Writing  
**Status:** **BINDING PROJECT-WIDE STORY-LOGIC GATE — ACTIVE**  
**Applies to:** authored Writing, CE-generated hotspot conversations, Story continuations, Battle return Story, Mission/Event dialogue, debriefs and dynamic participant interactions  
**Primary priority:** Finish Shinobi Chronicles Alpha without logically broken Story.

## 1. Why this gate exists

Shinobi Chronicles already has strong authority for:

- World Truth;
- observer Knowledge;
- participant autonomy;
- intelligent Story continuation;
- player agency;
- character voice;
- dialogue naturalism;
- consequence;
- causal reincorporation;
- Battle / Story seams.

Those rules can still be misapplied in a way that is technically careful but narratively absurd.

Recurring failure pattern:

```text
writer protects hidden truth
-> writer prevents character from reacting to obvious evidence
-> scene behaves as though the obvious contradiction did not occur
-> character becomes irrational
-> later reveal is technically Knowledge-safe but dramatically stupid
```

Canonical correction:

> **Knowledge safety is not permission for irrational ignorance.**

And:

> **Hide the answer when required. Do not hide the obvious problem from a character who just witnessed it.**

## 2. Mandatory causal spine

Before Writing or CE produces a consequential conversation/scene, derive this chain:

```text
TRIGGER
-> WHO PERCEIVES IT
-> WHAT THEY CAN REASONABLY INFER
-> WHAT THEY NOW WANT / FEAR / NEED
-> WHAT THEY DO
-> RESOLVER / FACTUAL RESULT
-> VISIBLE CONSEQUENCE
-> CHANGED NEXT SITUATION
```

If any arrow has no sensible explanation, the scene is not ready for prose.

Do not use dialogue to conceal a missing causal link.

## 3. Rational-actor floor

Characters do not need to make optimal decisions.

They may be:
- frightened;
- angry;
- impulsive;
- proud;
- inexperienced;
- distracted;
- mistaken;
- manipulated;
- exhausted.

But their action must still make sense from:

`personality + current Knowledge + relationship + capability + pressure + immediate evidence`.

A character may make a bad decision.

They may not make a nonsensical decision merely because the author needs the next scene.

Canonical distinction:

> **imperfect judgement != missing common sense**

## 4. Obvious-observation rule

When something materially abnormal happens directly in front of a participant, the participant must normally register it.

Examples:

- the person you are escorting attacks you;
- a prisoner suddenly escapes;
- an ally turns a weapon on you;
- the package you were carrying is visibly gone;
- somebody who was just defeated stands up and leaves;
- the person speaking uses information they should not know;
- a supposed civilian performs obvious shinobi combat;
- a route physically becomes blocked;
- a person believed dead walks into the room.

The observer does not automatically know **why**.

But they cannot behave as though nothing happened.

Preserve:

`observation != full explanation`

and:

`obvious contradiction -> reaction / suspicion / reassessment`.

## 5. Mystery vs stupidity

Mystery is created when:

- the character sees something strange;
- understands that it is strange;
- lacks enough evidence to know the full truth;
- acts from that uncertainty.

Stupidity is created when:

- the character sees something strange;
- the Story suppresses their obvious reaction;
- they continue the old behavior unchanged;
- the author later calls the hidden answer a reveal.

Canonical rule:

> **A mystery may withhold the answer. It may not require everyone to ignore the question.**

## 6. Premise-invalidation rule

Every ongoing activity has a premise.

Examples:

- escort: this person is the person I am assigned to protect and is participating as an escortee;
- interrogation: this participant is available and under control;
- pursuit: the target remains at large/reachable;
- delivery: the object/person remains in the expected custody chain;
- negotiation: both sides remain willing/able to negotiate;
- Battle: the encounter remains unresolved;
- rescue: somebody still needs rescuing.

When an event materially invalidates that premise, Story must reassess before continuing.

Do not default to:

`unexpected event -> old activity resumes unchanged`.

Required:

`unexpected event -> premise check -> continuation / revision / termination`.

Example:

> Escortee attacks Escort.

The Story must not automatically continue the escort afterward merely because the route graph previously expected an escort scene.

## 7. Trigger != outcome

Writing and CE must explicitly distinguish:

- **what event triggers a Story change**; and
- **what the result of that event changes**.

Examples:

```text
Battle occurrence may trigger reveal.
Battle victory/defeat may change aftermath.
```

```text
Attempted theft may trigger suspicion.
Success/failure changes custody.
```

```text
A participant speaks a forbidden detail may trigger doubt.
Whether the detail is true changes later evidence.
```

Do not accidentally make a Story trigger depend on success/failure when the **occurrence itself** is what logically causes the reaction.

Canonical lock:

> **Trigger decides whether the situation changes. Outcome decides how it changes.**

## 8. Why-now test

For every major deliberate action, ask:

> **Why does this person do this NOW?**

If the answer is only:
- because the route requires it;
- because the next hotspot needs it;
- because the Battle must start;
- because the reveal is scheduled;
- because the writer needs exposition;

the action fails.

The answer must come from current Story state.

## 9. Why-continue test

After every disruptive event, ask:

> **Why would these people continue doing what they were doing before?**

If the answer cannot be stated from participant Knowledge/motive, do not continue the old activity.

This is especially mandatory after:

- Battle;
- betrayal;
- reveal;
- attack;
- defeat;
- escape;
- death;
- custody change;
- major lie exposed;
- objective completion/failure;
- location access change.

## 10. Immediate-memory rule

Characters remember what happened five seconds ago.

Do not allow:

- Battle -> normal conversation as though no fight occurred;
- betrayal -> friendly routine;
- defeat -> continue task without acknowledgement;
- reveal -> old identity labels remain socially active;
- escape -> discussion assuming custody;
- destruction -> scene pretending environment unchanged.

Short-term continuity is mandatory unless a specific effect legitimately alters memory/perception.

## 11. No explanation-before-reaction

Do not protect a reveal by explaining away the contradiction before the character gets to react.

Bad pattern:

```text
strange actor explains why strange thing is authorised
-> protagonist accepts explanation
-> strange event happens
-> no genuine surprise/suspicion remains
```

When the contradiction itself is dramatically important:

```text
contradiction happens
-> character reacts
-> uncertainty / conflict
-> later explanation/reveal
```

Do not use exposition to sterilise the scene.

## 12. Knowledge boundary — correct use

Knowledge authority limits conclusions.

It does not erase perception.

Correct:

```text
Mirai sees apparent Traveller attack her
-> knows something is wrong
-> does NOT yet know "this is my female instructor in disguise"
```

Incorrect:

```text
Mirai may not know hidden identity
-> therefore Mirai cannot find the attack suspicious
```

Preserve:

`World Truth != observer Knowledge`

while also preserving:

`directly observed event -> observer Knowledge of that event`.

## 13. Participant-intention update

When a participant legitimately perceives a material change, reevaluate their current intention.

Required CE/Writing loop:

```text
perceived material event
-> belief / Knowledge update
-> existing intention check
-> retain / revise / abandon
-> next action
```

Do not keep an intention alive merely because a previously authored scene expected it.

## 14. No plot-preservation stupidity

Story may not make a character less observant, less competent or less rational than their established characterization simply to preserve:

- a reveal;
- a Battle;
- a route;
- a future choice;
- a planned hotspot;
- a debrief;
- a twist.

If preserving the planned beat requires obvious character stupidity:

> **the planned beat is wrong.**

Repair the continuation rather than damaging the character.

## 15. No semantic-safety overfitting

If a semantic safeguard appears to force unnatural Story behavior, Writing must stop.

Do not invent an absurd scene to prove:

- Knowledge was not granted;
- ownership was not implied;
- Battle did not reveal identity;
- route remained technically available;
- state did not automatically change.

Instead route the exact conflict to the owning authority.

Canonical rule:

> **Safeguards constrain facts. They do not require bad human behavior.**

## 16. Hotspot Conversation causal skeleton — mandatory CE gate

Before CE creates or selects a hotspot conversation, it must have an internal causal skeleton with:

1. **Current place**
2. **Current participants**
3. **Immediate prior event**
4. **What each participant legitimately knows**
5. **What each participant currently wants**
6. **What changed / why conversation happens now**
7. **What can actually change because of this conversation**

A hotspot conversation must not spawn merely because:

- two characters are colocated;
- the engine has dialogue available;
- a relationship exists;
- the author wants flavour;
- the hotspot has not fired recently.

Conversation eligibility requires a reason to talk **now**.

## 17. Hotspot conversation must have a pressure source

At least one meaningful pressure should shape a substantive hotspot exchange:

- conflicting wants;
- unresolved history;
- new information;
- embarrassment;
- secrecy;
- urgency;
- hierarchy;
- debt;
- affection;
- suspicion;
- fear;
- rivalry;
- recent consequence;
- opportunity;
- disagreement;
- practical task.

This does not require hostility.

It prevents:

`hello -> generic personality banter -> lore -> goodbye`.

## 18. Hotspot no-reset rule

A hotspot conversation must consume recent relevant Chronicle history where that history changes meaning.

Do not write a fresh generic version of two characters meeting when:

- they just fought;
- one betrayed the other;
- one saved the other;
- they learned a secret;
- trust changed;
- somebody was humiliated;
- somebody was promoted/demoted;
- an earlier promise remains unresolved.

Recent causal state outranks generic relationship templates.

## 19. Conversation purpose test

Before authoring a hotspot, answer:

> **Why is this conversation happening?**

Acceptable answers include:
- one participant wants information;
- somebody confronts a contradiction;
- somebody asks for help;
- somebody tries to repair damage;
- somebody warns the other;
- somebody tests loyalty;
- somebody needs a decision;
- somebody cannot avoid addressing what just happened.

Weak answer:
- “to show their personality.”

Personality should emerge while participants pursue something.

## 20. The "what the fuck?" test

Before approving a scene, ask:

> **Would a reasonable player immediately say, "Why the fuck did they do that?"**

If yes, determine whether the confusion is:

### Intended mystery
The character also notices the contradiction and the Story is deliberately withholding the answer.

This can pass.

### Unintended logic hole
The character ignores the contradiction or behaves as though it did not matter.

This fails.

The player may be confused about **why** something happened.

The player should not be confused about **why nobody noticed it happened**.

## 21. Counterfactual sanity test

For each major action, temporarily remove the writer's explanatory narration.

Ask:

> **Would the action still make sense from the visible situation and character state?**

If no, the explanation may be patching a causal hole.

Repair the cause rather than adding explanation.

## 22. Objective sanity test

At each major transition:

- What is the current objective?
- Is it still possible?
- Is it still necessary?
- Does the protagonist still believe it is valid?
- Did a more urgent objective replace it?

Do not continue an obsolete objective because its completion scene already exists.

## 23. Reveal sanity test

For reveals:

1. What visible contradiction exists before reveal?
2. Who notices it?
3. What can they infer safely?
4. What remains hidden?
5. What event makes continued concealment no longer useful/plausible?
6. Why does reveal happen now?

If the reveal only occurs because the script says “now reveal,” fail.

## 24. Battle return sanity test

Every Story <- Battle return must answer:

- What did the participants just experience?
- Who won/lost/withdrew?
- What physically/socially changed?
- Is the pre-Battle objective still valid?
- Does anybody need to react before moving on?
- Did the Battle itself trigger a reveal, escape, custody change, assessment end or relationship change?

Battle victory != automatic Story continuation.

Battle defeat != automatic mission failure.

But Battle occurrence/result must not be ignored.

## 25. CE-generated conversation restrictions

CE may intelligently realise conversations from authorised state.

CE must not invent new:

- World Truth;
- secret relationship;
- Knowledge;
- ownership;
- capability;
- political authority;
- romance state;
- betrayal;
- permanent consequence;

merely to make conversation interesting.

Common sense operates **inside authority**, not instead of authority.

If logical continuation requires a fact that is not authorised:

> fail closed and route the missing fact.

## 26. Writing preflight addition

Every Writing preflight for a consequential scene/hotspot must now include a compact:

### CAUSAL SPINE
- Trigger:
- Direct observers:
- Safe inference:
- Current participant wants:
- Premise invalidated? YES/NO + why:
- Why this action/conversation happens now:
- What changes afterward:

The preflight may not report GREEN if this causal spine is incoherent.

## 27. Acceptance status

Story logic has its own status:

- **CAUSAL GREEN** — event/reaction/intention/continuation chain is coherent.
- **KNOWLEDGE GREEN** — no illegitimate hidden truth granted.
- **VOICE GREEN** — characters remain distinct.
- **CONVERSATION GREEN** — exchange has lived social shape.
- **SEMANTIC GREEN** — owning facts preserved.
- **WRITING GOLDEN** — all required layers approved.

Do not collapse:

`KNOWLEDGE GREEN != CAUSAL GREEN`

A scene can preserve Knowledge perfectly and still be stupid.

## 28. Mirai benchmark correction

The 2026-09-30 Mirai defect is the benchmark example.

Wrong reasoning:

```text
Mirai must not know Traveller is instructor
-> therefore pre-explain the attack as an authorised assessment role
-> victory can preserve disguise
```

Correct reasoning:

```text
apparent Traveller attacks Mirai
-> Mirai directly knows the escortee is attacking her
-> she knows something is wrong
-> she does not yet know full hidden identity
-> PL Battle occurs
-> Battle occurrence ends usefulness of disguise
-> Story reveal follows
-> victory/defeat changes aftermath
```

This is the model for using Knowledge constraints without destroying common sense.

## 29. Final lock

> **Shinobi Chronicles Story must obey both authoritative state and ordinary human causality. Characters may be mistaken, emotional or imperfect, but they must react to material events they actually perceive. Knowledge limits may withhold hidden answers; they may not erase obvious observations. Every consequential scene and CE-generated hotspot conversation must have a coherent trigger -> perception -> inference -> intention -> action -> consequence -> changed-next-situation chain. If preserving a planned route requires a character to ignore the obvious, the route continuation is wrong — not the character.**
