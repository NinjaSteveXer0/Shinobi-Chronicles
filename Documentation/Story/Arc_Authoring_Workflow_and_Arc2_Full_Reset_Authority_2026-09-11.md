## 1. Why this exists

Live Arc 2 authoring drifted into mission-level improvisation before the full Arc had been planned and approved. That produced local scenes and mechanics without a stable whole-Arc mission spine, then encouraged successive patching of the live branch rather than returning to the intended Arc authoring workflow.

This document closes that failure mode.

The active Writing workflow for Shinobi Chronicles is **Arc-first**.

## 2. Arc-first authoring workflow — binding

At the beginning of each Story Arc:

### Phase A — Plan the entire Arc before writing playable story

Writing first produces a complete **11–12 mission Arc plan**.

The plan covers the whole Arc from opening through climax/transition. It is a mission spine / blueprint, not live prose and not a developer playthrough.

Each proposed mission should establish, at minimum where relevant:

- mission number and working title;
- mission dramatic function inside the Arc;
- core situation/conflict;
- required authored invariant / historical anchor;
- expected geography / major location movement;
- Chronicle-relative causal-carrier space where Structured Story Autonomy applies;
- major NPC/faction pressures without pre-owning autonomous decisions;
- likely investigation / traversal / infiltration / pursuit / protection / Battle / consequence role;
- material PL Battle opportunities where the mission needs tactical conflict;
- durable outputs that can matter later in the Arc / next Arc;
- how the mission advances escalation rather than merely filling space;
- bridge into the next mission.

The plan should show the Arc's pacing across all 11–12 missions: opening, escalation, reversals, outward/world expansion where authored, major tactical peaks, political/relationship consequences, climax, and Arc transition.

**Do not begin full playable Mission 1 prose while the Arc plan is still being invented.**

### Phase B — Stephen reviews / mutates / approves the Arc plan

The planned 11–12 mission spine is presented for Stephen's review.

Stephen may approve, reject, reorder, merge, split, replace or mutate missions and major beats.

Writing revises the Arc plan until Stephen approves the whole spine.

Approval of a mission idea in isolation is not approval to start live prose if the overall Arc spine is not yet accepted.

### Phase C — Write the playable Story after Arc approval

Only after the Arc mission spine is approved does Writing author the actual Story.

Playable authoring proceeds mission by mission and scene by scene with **breathing space for player decisions**.

The player-facing rhythm remains:

`scene beat -> character reactions -> autonomous NPC intent where perceivable -> meaningful player decision -> STOP -> legitimate resolution -> consequence / next beat`

Writing must not convert the approved mission outline into one uninterrupted long-form story dump.

The Arc plan tells us where the Story is going. The playable Story still gives the player room to decide **how their Chronicle gets there** within legitimate authority.

## 3. Breathing-space / choice rule

The binding interactive authority remains:

- the Origin Character belongs to the player;
- NPCs choose themselves;
- CE resolves what the world permits;
- player intent is not guaranteed occurrence;
- Story should stop at meaningful protagonist decisions;
- NPC intent should be surfaced first where causally appropriate and perceivable;
- choices should be meaningful rather than inserted mechanically;
- do not let long exposition/prose run past points where the player could reasonably decide, question, prioritise or act;
- Story must move toward action, investigation, traversal, opportunity, Battle and consequence rather than trapping the player in dialogue walls.

Authoring may be cinematic and characterful. **Breathing space means frequent meaningful control, not flavourless summaries.**

## 4. Relationship to Structured Story Autonomy

This workflow does not replace Structured Story Autonomy.

Binding doctrine remains:

> **Story says what the campaign must confront. CE proves why this Chronicle reaches it this way.**

The approved Arc plan authors the campaign mission spine, functions, mandatory macro destinations/anchors and dramatic escalation.

CE/Chronicle state supplies legitimate causal carriers, bounded participant Knowledge, autonomous actor decisions and Chronicle-relative realisation where Story has intentionally left those open.

Therefore:

- full Arc plan != fixed every-scene outcome;
- mission invariant != protagonist-specific hard-code;
- authored destination != fabricated causal path;
- player choice != party command;
- approved Arc spine != permission to pre-write the player's meaningful decisions.
