## 1. Core clarification

CE must not only determine the **next logical choice set** from committed Chronicle state.

It must also allow the **direction of the Story itself** to move because of accumulated player choices and factual outcomes.

Canonical rule:

> **Choice changes state. State changes Story eligibility. Story eligibility changes direction.**

Therefore this is insufficient:

`choice -> resolver -> changed local state -> smarter next menu -> eventual forced return to same underlying Story path`

Required:

`choice intent -> factual resolver result -> committed Chronicle history -> changed local situation -> changed future Story eligibility/direction -> later scenes/missions/routes inherit that history`

## 2. What Story direction means

Story direction is broader than the next dialogue or next button.

Committed history may legitimately change:

- which scene occurs next;
- which scenes become eligible at all;
- scene ordering where authored constraints permit it;
- which NPCs are present, absent, willing, hostile, unavailable or already gone;
- which objectives remain unresolved;
- which evidence survives;
- which World opportunities appear/disappear;
- which locations become relevant/accessible;
- which relationships or obligations create later calls for help, refusal, trust or betrayal;
- which Mission-local branches exist;
- which later Mission situations are seeded;
- which Arc-local pressures strengthen or disappear;
- which route opportunities become plausible or impossible;
- which route gates are eventually satisfied.

A prior choice does not need to create a totally separate campaign immediately. It does need to remain capable of changing future Story where the committed facts matter.

## 3. Immediate continuation != full directional consequence

CE must evaluate choices at multiple timescales.

### Immediate

What happens in the next seconds/minutes?

- who reacts;
- who moves;
- what changes hands;
- whether Battle begins;
- who escapes;
- what the protagonist learns.

### Scene / Mission local

What changes later in this scene or Mission because of that result?

- new objective;
- missing participant;
- different route through the location;
- altered assistance;
- different confrontation;
- different information available at debrief/closure.

### Arc trajectory

What accumulated history should alter later Arc situations?

- trust;
- faction standing;
- institutional obligation;
- evidence held;
- grudges;
- alliances;
- unresolved enemies;
- prior rescues/betrayals/refusals;
- Knowledge;
- legal/political status;
- ownership/access;
- recurring NPC interpretation of the protagonist.

### Route trajectory

What later route directions become more or less eligible because of actual Chronicle history?

Examples may include:
- Canon;
- Alt;
- Rogue;
- Non-Conformance;
- Leader / Take-the-Throne;
- future village-specific Canon/Alt;
- All-Route convergence prerequisites.

## 4. Route trajectory != one-choice route lock

Preserve:

> **one choice != route lock**

> **route affinity / trajectory != personality or alignment score**

> **repeated factual history may create route eligibility without becoming an invisible morality meter**

CE must not invent a universal `Good/Evil`, `Canon/Alt`, `Rogue`, `Leader`, `NonConformance` scalar merely to steer the campaign.

Route movement should arise from actual facts such as:

- the protagonist left or remained within an institution;
- authority was obeyed, challenged or replaced;
- a faction now trusts or opposes them;
- a relationship exists or has broken;
- an NPC is alive/dead/injured/absent;
- evidence is possessed, destroyed or unknown;
- the protagonist holds legitimate office/authority or does not;
- prior agreements/obligations exist;
- capability/access/ownership legitimately opens a route;
- repeated decisions establish a pattern that relevant observers can legitimately know and react to;
- authored route gates are factually satisfied.

This may create **directional pressure** or **eligibility**, but CE must not treat a single stylistic choice as metaphysical destiny.

## 5. Story must inherit the world the player created

If prior choices make a later planned scene factually impossible, Story must not stage that scene unchanged merely because it exists in an outline.

Examples:

- an NPC who escaped earlier cannot appear as though captured;
- a destroyed piece of evidence cannot be handed over later;
- an NPC who never met the protagonist cannot reference Shared History that never happened;
- an institution the protagonist abandoned cannot casually issue routine internal orders unless a legitimate communication/authority path exists;
- a faction the protagonist openly opposed may react differently from one they supported;
- a package already secured/lost cannot remain simultaneously unresolved;
- a discovered identity cannot later be treated as unknown to the same observer without a legitimate reason.

Canonical rule:

> **Later Story consumes Recorded History, not Expected History.**

## 6. Mandatory authored beats and flexible direction

Choice-driven direction does not mean every authored campaign beat becomes optional.

A mandatory set-piece may remain mandatory when its eligibility conditions are still true.

Examples:
- a major world event happens independently of the protagonist;
- a political crisis occurs regardless of which local option was selected;
- a founder returns because world history caused it;
- Madara's endgame plan progresses from his own agency.

But the protagonist's entry point, allies, Knowledge, objectives, relationships, available actions, consequences and aftermath may differ because of prior Chronicle history.

Preserve:

> **mandatory event != identical player experience**

> **world event autonomy != protagonist history erased**

## 7. CE directional reasoning questions

At meaningful Story checkpoints, CE should ask not only `what happens next?`, but also:

1. What unresolved facts from prior scenes still exert pressure?
2. Which actors now have changed goals because of the protagonist's history?
3. Which future scenes are now newly eligible?
4. Which previously planned scenes are no longer factually legal?
5. Which optional opportunities should appear because of trust, Knowledge, access, obligation or prior contact?
6. Which opportunities should disappear because of death, hostility, absence, lost evidence, failed access or route history?
7. Has accumulated history satisfied an authored route gate?
8. Has accumulated history made a route gate impossible or unlikely?
9. Is the current Mission/Arc still pursuing the same objective, or has the actual Chronicle created a new dominant pressure?
10. If convergence occurs, which factual differences must remain visible and consumable later?

## 8. Kakashi benchmark

For Academy Kakashi, the first choice family includes:

- Observe;
- Get Closer;
- Attack;
- Attempt to Pickpocket.

CE should not merely produce four different immediate reactions and then force all four back into an identical exchange state if the actual results differ.

Examples of directional consequences that CE must be capable of preserving where factual resolution supports them:

- Kakashi learns more but allows the exchange to progress;
- Kakashi is detected earlier;
- the package changes custody under pressure;
- one participant flees sooner;
- the Rogue Chūnin enters against a different local state;
- Kakashi reaches the Rogue Chūnin sequence with different Knowledge, position, custody or assistance;
- Pakkun's involvement may occur under different circumstances depending on how the situation develops;
- later final interpretation choices should consume the history Kakashi actually created.

Stephen's recovered route remains one authoritative realised history. CE must allow other legitimate Kakashi histories to develop directionally from their own state.

## 9. Arc 1–3 application

Arc 1–3 must consume this rule.

- Arc 1 may change later Mission-local and Arc-local presentation/opportunities because of actual choices and outcomes.
- Arc 2 and Arc 3 closed historical facts remain closed for already-committed developer histories, but runtime/player-facing route realization must preserve the direction created by the player's own Chronicle history.
- retrospective implementation must not flatten different legitimate histories into one expected-history screenplay merely because the macro Arc endpoint is shared.

Shared macro endpoints are permitted where already authored, but the path into them and the state carried out of them must remain Chronicle-relative.

## 10. Canon / Alt / Rogue / Non-Conformance / Leader

These route families must emerge from legitimate historical conditions rather than a single explicit `Choose Route` morality button unless a later authored political/strategic choice deliberately formalises the route.

CE should allow cumulative choices/outcomes to:

- strengthen or weaken route eligibility;
- create route-specific NPC support/opposition;
- open/close route-specific opportunities;
- change what the protagonist is asked to do;
- change which institutions still recognise them;
- change what compromises remain possible;
- change which conflicts become central.

Preserve:

**route trajectory != deterministic personality**  
**route eligibility != ownership**  
**route eligibility != mandatory route selection**  
**one earned outcome != universal campaign canon**

## 11. Final shorthand

> **CE does not merely choose the next branch. CE carries the consequences of the branch forward until they stop mattering.**

> **The Story should move toward the world the player actually created, not continually pull the player back toward the outline they were expected to create.**

> **Macro structure may converge. Recorded History must not be erased by convergence.**
