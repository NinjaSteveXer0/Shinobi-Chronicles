# Shinobi Chronicles — Writing Anti-Flattening Character Voice + Conversation Hard Gate

**Date:** 2026-09-30  
**Owner:** Stephen / Writing / CE-Codex-Coordination  
**Status:** **BINDING ADDITIVE PLAYER-FACING WRITING QUALITY GATE — ACTIVE**  
**Parent protocol:** #333  
**Primary priority:** Finish Shinobi Chronicles Alpha with characters who sound and behave like themselves.

## 1. Why this gate exists

Existing Writing authority already protects:

- semantic correctness;
- Knowledge;
- player agency;
- scene causality;
- dialogue naturalism;
- character voice;
- group-scene liveliness;
- diction / cadence / personality / emotion;
- Story-vs-system separation.

That is necessary but has not been sufficient.

Repeated owner review has exposed a successor failure mode:

```text
Writing avoids obvious system prose
-> prose becomes cleaner
-> dialogue becomes safe
-> everyone becomes reasonable / competent / lightly sarcastic
-> conversations become short neutral exchanges
-> personalities lose force
-> scenes become technically natural but dull
```

This gate exists to block that failure.

Canonical rule:

> **Natural dialogue is not enough. It must be natural dialogue spoken by THESE people.**

And:

> **Clear is required. Plain is not the target.**

## 2. HARD FAIL — neutral default voice

A named character fails if their dialogue could be transferred to another named character with only pronoun/name changes and still feel equally correct.

Writing must not fall back to a house voice built from:

- short competent answers;
- mild dry humour;
- calm observation;
- generic scepticism;
- tidy emotional restraint;
- polite disagreement;
- sensible instructor guidance;
- interchangeable "looks at X / looks back" staging.

If the cast sounds like one intelligent writer wearing several names, the scene is RED.

## 3. Mandatory character voice fingerprint

Before substantial player-facing drafting, Writing must recover the current character-specific authority and hold an internal **voice fingerprint** for every major named participant.

For established characters, the fingerprint must cover at least:

- sentence rhythm / usual length;
- directness vs evasion;
- vocabulary/formality;
- humour style or absence of humour;
- emotional leakage;
- conflict posture;
- what they notice first;
- what embarrasses/angers/softens them;
- what they refuse to say;
- characteristic physical/social behaviour.

This is author-side guidance only. Do not print the fingerprint into player-facing Story.

A one-scene NPC does not need a dossier, but must still have a **micro-anchor**:
- immediate want;
- social posture;
- one specific behavioural/voice trait.

## 4. HARD FAIL — personality absent from the first contact

When a named character enters a scene, the player should not need several generic exchanges before that character becomes recognisable.

Within the character's early participation, the scene should naturally expose something specific through:

- wording;
- reaction;
- interruption;
- refusal;
- assumption;
- humour;
- action;
- attention;
- mistake;
- emotional tell.

This is not a quota for a catchphrase.

It is a ban on opening a character as a neutral exposition device.

## 5. Character want / friction gate

Every materially present participant must have a current position in the scene.

That position may be:

- wanting something;
- avoiding something;
- protecting something;
- proving something;
- hiding something;
- hurrying;
- testing;
- teasing;
- doubting;
- resenting;
- obeying reluctantly;
- misunderstanding;
- trying to end the conversation.

A conversation where everyone cooperatively exchanges the information Writing needs is RED unless the exact situation genuinely requires formal information exchange.

Even friendly scenes need social shape.

> **Conversation requires pressure, not necessarily conflict.**

Pressure may come from:
- status;
- urgency;
- embarrassment;
- affection;
- mistrust;
- competing priorities;
- history;
- fear;
- pride;
- fatigue;
- secrecy;
- unequal Knowledge.

## 6. HARD FAIL — fact-delivery dialogue

A line must not exist only because the writer needs to transfer a fact.

Fact delivery should be carried through character behaviour whenever possible.

Ask of each important line:

> **What is this speaker doing socially by saying this?**

Examples:
- challenging;
- deflecting;
- warning;
- testing;
- provoking;
- reassuring;
- withholding;
- bargaining;
- showing off;
- covering fear;
- changing subject;
- asserting status;
- asking for help without admitting it;
- trying to leave.

If the answer is only "telling the player information," rewrite.

## 7. HARD FAIL — therapist / teacher-theme voice

Instructors, mentors, leaders and adults must not become Writing's mouthpiece for the lesson of the Origin.

Reject dialogue shaped like:

- "You need to understand...";
- "The important thing is...";
- "You are good at X, but you need Y...";
- "Both things can be true...";
- "Don't forget that...";
- "What matters is...";
- tidy summaries of the protagonist's strengths/weaknesses;
- emotionally perfect advice that resolves the scene's theme.

An instructor may teach.

But they must teach **as that person**, under current pressure, with their own patience, bias, humour, frustration, limits and relationship to the student.

A lesson that the scene has already demonstrated should usually not be spoken again.

## 8. HARD FAIL — universal dry wit

Do not give every competent or intelligent character the same:

```text
dry observation
-> understated reply
-> mild sarcasm
-> small reaction
```

Humour must be character-specific.

Some characters:
- joke loudly;
- tease;
- brag;
- deadpan;
- misread;
- get defensive;
- make bad jokes;
- do not joke at all.

If several unrelated characters share the same dry-smart cadence, rewrite.

## 9. HARD FAIL — tidy conversational symmetry

Reject repeated exchange shapes such as:

```text
A asks
B answers
A replies
B gives sensible closer
```

or:

```text
A notices
B explains
A accepts
Narration confirms lesson
```

or:

```text
NPC prompts
protagonist gives one-line personality answer
NPC validates
```

Real conversations may:
- interrupt;
- ignore;
- answer late;
- answer the wrong part;
- change topic;
- overlap concerns;
- leave something unresolved;
- end because somebody moves, arrives, refuses or gets distracted.

The shape should emerge from the people and circumstance, not from Writing's preferred rhythm.

## 10. Anti-house-tic lint

The following are already forbidden or high-risk fallback constructions when used as generic pacing devices:

- `A beat.`
- `A pause.`
- `Silence.`
- `For a moment.` as repeated filler;
- `Something changes in...`;
- `The silence changes.`;
- repeated `looks at X / then at Y` chains;
- repeated small nod / slight smile / tiny shrug staging;
- repeated "that answer is enough" / "no speech follows" narrator closure;
- repeated "irritatingly reasonable" / "annoyingly true" type thematic narrator commentary;
- repeated "does not chase / does not answer / does not move" as default dramatic emphasis.

These phrases are not all banned from English.

They are banned as **house tics**.

If the same construction appears repeatedly across one scene family, one Origin, or several adjacent Origin drafts, Writing must replace the repeated generic staging with character-specific action or remove it.

`A beat.` remains specifically prohibited in player-facing Story.

## 11. Physicality must be character-specific

Physical performance is not satisfied by attaching generic gestures to dialogue.

Weak:
- looks;
- nods;
- folds arms;
- sighs;
- glances away.

Those may be used, but they do not by themselves create personality.

Prefer behaviour with character meaning:

- Obito talks with his whole body and commits before thinking;
- Iwabee plants himself, bristles, dismisses, pushes through;
- Kushina points, interrupts, moves first, gets louder when challenged;
- Kakashi becomes more still and precise;
- Hinata's confidence/pressure expression follows her current SC voice authority;
- Kurenai watches patterns and lets other people expose themselves;
- Menma's pride/ambition changes how he responds to control and restriction.

These are examples of specificity, not mandatory repeated animation cues.

Do not turn a character-specific tell into a new repetitive tic.

## 12. Character capability must alter scene language

Current #333 capability authority remains binding.

A character's real techniques, strengths and habits should change:

- what they notice;
- what options feel natural;
- how they solve a problem;
- what they fear failing at;
- what another person expects from them.

Do not write the same generic decision and merely swap the protagonist name.

Capability does not replace personality. It intersects with it.

## 13. Relationship-specific voice

A character must not speak identically to:

- teacher;
- peer;
- rival;
- stranger;
- authority figure;
- friend;
- family;
- subordinate;
- enemy.

The same core personality expresses differently across relationships.

If a line can be moved between relationship contexts with no adjustment, run the relationship-swap test again.

## 14. Scene-memory gate

Before calling a scene family candidate-ready, ask:

> **What will the player remember about THIS exchange besides the information delivered?**

Acceptable memory anchors may be:

- a specific reaction;
- a strange interruption;
- a joke only that character would make;
- an argument;
- a refusal;
- a risky action;
- a quiet kindness;
- an embarrassment;
- a power imbalance;
- a character-specific choice.

If the answer is only:
- "the player learned X";
- "the instructor explained Y";
- "they discussed Z";

the scene is not ready.

## 15. Three substitution tests

### Speaker swap

Swap two named speakers.

If most lines still work, RED.

### Protagonist swap

Replace the protagonist with another Academy Origin protagonist without changing the scene.

If the same scene/choices remain plausible with only cosmetic edits, RED.

### Franchise-neutral swap

Remove Naruto/Shinobi terminology.

If the scene becomes generic YA/tutorial dialogue with no character/world identity left, RED.

## 16. Read-aloud test

Player-facing dialogue must be read internally as speech, not inspected only as prose.

Reject:
- lines nobody would naturally say aloud;
- sentences built to explain theme;
- consecutive characters using the same rhythm;
- overly complete grammar under pressure;
- repeated polished quotable lines;
- dialogue that sounds written for a trailer rather than spoken in the moment.

Characters are allowed to:
- stumble;
- restart;
- cut themselves off;
- speak badly;
- be unfair;
- be childish;
- be wrong;
- say something awkward.

Polish must not erase humanity.

## 17. Personality intensity is not sameness

Do not solve flattening by making everyone loud, sarcastic or quirky.

Distinctive may mean:

- reserved;
- awkward;
- intense;
- formal;
- aggressive;
- soft;
- eccentric;
- restrained;
- impatient;
- warm;
- strange.

The requirement is **specificity**, not maximum volume.

## 18. Review escalation after owner rejection

When Stephen rejects a Writing candidate for dullness, generic personality, unnatural speech or repeated cadence:

### First rejection
- do not bulk-rewrite the whole Origin;
- repair one coherent scene family;
- return actual player-facing prose for review.

### Second rejection on the same voice problem
Writing enters **VOICE QUARANTINE**:
- stop large-scale drafting;
- produce one short representative scene/sample from the actual Story;
- prove the protagonist + key NPC voices are distinct;
- await Stephen approval before propagating that voice treatment.

### Third repeat failure
Do not keep generating more prose.
Re-read:
- #333;
- this gate;
- exact character voice anchors;
- latest Stephen-approved benchmark for that exact character if one exists.

Then restart from the smallest scene family.

This prevents hours of bulk prose being generated in the wrong voice.

## 19. Candidate status cannot self-upgrade on cleanliness

Writing may not mark a rewrite "benchmark-quality", "natural voice", or Writing-GOLDEN solely because it:

- removed CE language;
- shortened sentences;
- removed exposition;
- passed route semantics;
- used correct backdrops;
- avoided scroll overflow.

Those are necessary.

They do not prove personality.

After a known expression rejection:

> **Stephen player-facing approval remains the quality gate.**

## 20. Benchmark anti-mimicry

The approved Kakashi/Uchiha Police exemplar remains a quality benchmark for:

- specificity;
- subtext;
- relationship;
- action;
- character separation.

It is **not a prose template**.

Do not copy into other characters:
- Kakashi's clipped cadence;
- Pakkun's dryness;
- ANBU stillness;
- Minato's restrained reaction;
- the exemplar's sentence-fragment rhythm;
- its gesture vocabulary.

A benchmark is evidence of the required quality level, not house style.

## 21. Whole-Origin anti-flattening audit

Before Writing-complete status, scan the entire Origin for:

- one humour style infecting multiple characters;
- one cadence infecting multiple characters;
- protagonist personality fading after Scene 1;
- NPCs converging into reasonable mentor voice;
- repeated "look / nod / silence" staging;
- final scenes becoming theme summaries;
- choices becoming generic across protagonists;
- dialogue becoming more formal/polished as the document continues;
- relationship-specific speech disappearing;
- scene-specific wants disappearing.

A strong opening does not excuse a generic second half.

## 22. Controlled failure vocabulary

Use these statuses explicitly:

- **VOICE GREEN** — characters are specific/non-interchangeable in actual prose.
- **CONVERSATION GREEN** — social pressure/shape feels lived rather than information-routed.
- **NARRATION GREEN** — narration stages experience without generic tics/theme explanation.
- **SEMANTIC GREEN** — closed Story facts preserved.
- **WRITING GOLDEN** — all required quality layers approved.

Do not collapse:

`SEMANTIC GREEN != VOICE GREEN != CONVERSATION GREEN != WRITING GOLDEN`.

## 23. Final lock

> **Shinobi Chronicles Writing must not settle for dialogue that is merely clear, short, grammatical and plausible. Named characters must remain recognisable through diction, cadence, wants, reactions, relationships, capability and behaviour. Generic competent dialogue, universal dry wit, therapist-like instructor speeches, tidy information exchange, repeated house tics and interchangeable scene rhythm are hard failures. When Stephen rejects voice quality, Writing must shrink the next attempt rather than generating more bulk prose.**
