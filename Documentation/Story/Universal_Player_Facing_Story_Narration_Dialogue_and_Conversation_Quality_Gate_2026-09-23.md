---

## 1. Purpose

This document does **not** create a competing Writing philosophy.

It makes the existing Writing standards universally applicable and adds an execution gate for the recurring failure Stephen has identified:

```text
correct semantics
-> author becomes over-focused on proving the semantics
-> system safeguards are narrated directly to the player
-> characters become examples of the rules
-> dialogue repeats the same conversational algorithm
-> scene feels like documentation wearing character names
```

This standard extends and operationalises:

- `Documentation/Story/Writing_Group_Conversation_Character_Interaction_and_Scene_Liveliness_Standard_2026-09-22.md`;
- `Documentation/Story/Writing_Character_Voice_Personality_Anchor_and_NPC_Behaviour_Standard_2026-09-22.md`;
- `Documentation/Coordination/Canon Research First Specialist Production Protocol 2026-09-12.md`;
- current Writing production workflow / one-pass Origin preflight authorities;
- current Storywide interactive Scene Board presentation direction.

Canonical rule:

> **The player should experience the Story, not be told how the Story system works.**

And:

> **Writing must obey semantic safeguards in the construction of the scene; it must not narrate those safeguards back to the player.**

# 3. Hard separation: Story vs author notes

Every Writing deliverable must distinguish:

## Player-facing Story

What the player is actually allowed to read/hear/experience:

- narration;
- action prose;
- dialogue;
- silence;
- physical reaction;
- environmental description;
- player choice text;
- diegetic labels where authorised.

## Author / implementation notes

Non-player-facing material such as:

- Knowledge constraints;
- sourceOccurrence IDs;
- route predicates;
- resolver conditions;
- semantic safeguards;
- Battle seams;
- state writes;
- reward seams;
- continuity notes;
- “this does not imply X” protections;
- branch intent;
- player-agency explanation;
- evidence-channel explanation;
- implementation TODOs.

These two layers must not be blended.

Canonical rule:

> **If a sentence exists to explain why the scene is semantically legal, it probably belongs in author notes rather than player-facing prose.**

---

# 4. Forbidden player-facing system prose

Player-facing narration/dialogue must not read like CE / QA / design documentation.

Unless a term is genuinely diegetic in the world, avoid prose such as:

- “the player decides...”;
- “this does not establish...”;
- “this does not automatically mean...”;
- “this route remains available...”;
- “the evidence channel...”;
- “the Knowledge state...”;
- “this is a legitimate decision...”;
- “the player is allowed to...”;
- “this does not manufacture confirmation...”;
- “the Chronicle records...”;
- “the branch now...”;
- “the resolver...”;
- “the semantic meaning...”;
- “the player connects the clues...”;
- “this choice represents...”;
- “the system distinguishes...”.

Writing must encode those truths through what actually happens.

Bad:

```text
Mirai does not receive an automatic conclusion.
The player decides what the mismatch means.
```

Correct execution:

```text
The answer is different.

Mirai keeps walking.

She says nothing.
```

The player now owns the inference because the scene leaves room for it.

---

# 5. Trust the player

Do not explain a conclusion the player can reasonably infer from:

- previous dialogue;
- visual staging;
- actor reaction;
- silence;
- contradiction;
- repeated behaviour;
- absence;
- changed tone;
- environmental change;
- known prior context.

Canonical rule:

> **If the player can understand it from what happened, do not explain it again.**

Do not end scenes with a paragraph translating the Story into its lesson.

Do not use narration to certify what the player should think.

Do not make every clue announce its significance.

Ambiguity is allowed when the factual Story supports ambiguity.

---

# 6. Story first, safeguards second

Before drafting, Writing should know:

- World Truth;
- participant Knowledge;
- allowed inference;
- branch/outcome rails;
- Battle seam;
- reward/progression boundaries;
- continuity requirements.

Once those are known, **stop writing the rules and write the scene**.

The drafting order is:

```text
semantic rails understood
-> participant motives/relationships understood
-> scene written as lived experience
-> semantic audit afterward
```

Not:

```text
semantic rule
-> sentence explaining semantic rule
-> dialogue illustrating semantic rule
-> narrator explains why dialogue was legal
```

---

# 7. Canon research is prerequisite, not deliverable

Canon Research First remains binding.

But once the current character anchor is sufficient for the scene:

> **Stop researching and write.**

Do not repeatedly search canon as a substitute for producing player-facing Story.

Additional research is justified when a **new specific canon uncertainty** blocks an exact characterization/history decision.

Research notes do not count as Story progress.

A personality anchor does not count as Story.

A semantic analysis does not count as Story.

The deliverable is the scene.

---

# 8. Character must emerge through behaviour

Do not repeatedly tell the player a character is:

- serious;
- awkward;
- angry;
- suspicious;
- kind;
- guarded;
- observant;
- overthinking;
- protective.

Make it legible through:

- what they notice;
- what they miss;
- what they say;
- what they refuse to say;
- how quickly they answer;
- what they interrupt;
- what embarrasses them;
- what they physically do;
- what they assume;
- who they defer to;
- who they challenge;
- what changes their behaviour.

Other characters should not repeatedly exist to announce the protagonist's personality back to them.

---

# 9. No conversation algorithm

A scene fails if it repeatedly uses one exchange template as a substitute for personality.

Examples of overused patterns include:

```text
precise question
-> light joke
-> literal answer
-> teasing comment
-> look / silence
```

or:

```text
NPC exposition
-> protagonist dry remark
-> NPC dry remark
-> narration says tension changed
```

or:

```text
question
-> answer
-> next required question
-> answer
-> next required question
```

Character consistency does **not** mean identical conversational rhythm.

Across a Story, vary naturally:

- sentence length;
- who initiates;
- who changes subject;
- whether someone answers;
- interruptions;
- overlapping concerns;
- humour;
- awkward pauses;
- incomplete thoughts;
- disagreement;
- distraction;
- body language;
- silence;
- misunderstandings;
- emotional escalation/de-escalation.

Canonical rule:

> **A personality is not a reusable dialogue algorithm.**

---

# 10. NPC autonomy

Every materially present person is a person, not a delivery mechanism.

An NPC should normally have some combination of:

- a current want;
- an opinion;
- a worry;
- impatience;
- humour;
- embarrassment;
- a relationship posture;
- a reason to speak;
- a reason not to speak.

Do not reduce NPCs to:

- clue dispenser;
- exposition terminal;
- quest marker;
- grading interface;
- villain information faucet;
- joke generator;
- protagonist-validation device.

A one-scene NPC does not need a biography.

They do need enough human specificity to behave like somebody.

---

# 11. Narration job

Narration should primarily do work dialogue cannot efficiently do:

- movement;
- space;
- timing;
- physical action;
- facial/body reaction;
- environment;
- atmosphere;
- sensory information;
- silence;
- observer-safe internal texture where appropriate.

Narration must not become an instruction manual for:

- what the player should infer;
- why a choice is legitimate;
- what a clue means;
- what a route means;
- what the engine is tracking;
- what consequence category has been satisfied.

Use narration to **show the event**, not grade the event.

---

# 12. Dialogue job

Dialogue should arise from:

```text
personality
+ relationship
+ current Knowledge
+ immediate want
+ pressure
+ what the previous person just said/did
```

Dialogue is not a queue of facts Writing needs to deliver.

Every line should have a social/action context.

Characters may:

- misunderstand;
- evade;
- answer only part of a question;
- interrupt;
- change subject;
- lie;
- refuse;
- joke badly;
- say too much;
- say too little;
- realise something late;
- react physically instead of verbally.

Not every factual point needs spoken explanation.

---

# 13. Group conversation

Existing group-conversation authority remains fully binding.

Additionally:

> **A group scene should have a conversational shape, not a speaker rotation.**

Avoid:

```text
A speaks
B speaks
C speaks
player choice
A speaks
B speaks
C speaks
```

unless the exact situation naturally produces that structure.

NPCs may talk to each other without routing every line through the protagonist.

Silence can be meaningful.

Do **not** write generic screenplay-pause filler such as:

`A beat.`

into player-facing Story.

If silence matters, show the specific physical reaction, interruption, hesitation or change in attention that makes that silence belong to this character and this scene. If nothing meaningful happens in the pause, omit it.

Do not replace `A beat.` with another repeated house tic such as `For a moment.`, `A pause.`, or `Silence.`.

One person may dominate.

Another may interrupt.

Another may leave.

Another may refuse to participate.

The scene should reflect relationships, not seating order.

## 13.1 Verbal turn completion is not required

Conversation is not a mandatory alternating sequence of spoken replies.

A character does **not** always need to speak in order to:

- begin an interaction;
- answer;
- acknowledge;
- refuse;
- continue;
- redirect;
- de-escalate;
- close a conversational beat;
- end the scene.

A meaningful response may instead be:

- laughter / a chuckle;
- a sigh or changed breath;
- a look / eye-line shift;
- a gesture;
- posture or physical distance changing;
- turning away;
- returning to a task;
- handing over / taking an object;
- leaving;
- staying;
- touch;
- silence;
- another character noticing the non-verbal reaction;
- environmental/action continuation that makes the social meaning legible.

Canonical rules:

> **Spoken line != required response.**

> **Last spoken line != required final beat.**

> **Conversation closure != verbal closure.**

> **Non-verbal response may carry the interaction forward or finish it.**

Do not append another spoken line merely to make dialogue feel symmetrical, to give every present character a final turn, or to verbally restate meaning already carried by performance.

Example shape:

```text
A says something that changes the emotional/social state.
B reacts physically or non-verbally.
Scene ends.
```

That may be more natural than:

```text
A speaks.
B reacts.
A explains/acknowledges the reaction.
B gives a closing reply.
Scene ends.
```

If the extra line adds no new intent, relationship movement, Knowledge, action, pressure or character texture, it may actively weaken the scene.

Likewise, a conversation may **start** before anybody speaks. Blocking, eye contact, avoidance, entering/leaving space, an offered object, a visible injury, resumed work or another action may establish the interaction before the first line.

Canonical acceptance test:

> **Do not ask whether every line received a reply. Ask whether the interaction has naturally completed its current beat.**

This does not require every scene to end silently or non-verbally. Spoken closure remains valid when it belongs to the people and situation.

The rule exists to prevent **forced conversational bookkeeping**.

---

# 14. Player agency

Player agency is expressed by genuine decisions, not by narration explaining that agency exists.

Do not write:

```text
The player may decide whether to...
```

Present the actual situation and actual choice when the decision point arrives.

Do not insert choices merely to prove interactivity.

Do not let Writing spend the player's inference automatically.

Do not remove legitimate authored protagonist continuity by turning every emotional beat into a menu.

Preserve:

```text
meaningful choice != constant choice
character personality != forced player outcome
player choice != NPC command
```

---

# 15. Scenes must change

A conversation should normally produce some change:

- new Knowledge;
- changed trust;
- changed mood;
- changed plan;
- changed relationship pressure;
- a decision;
- a refusal;
- a reveal;
- a misunderstanding;
- a physical action;
- a silence that matters.

Avoid scenes that merely restate the same fact with different sentences.

Avoid debriefs that re-narrate everything the player already saw unless the act of reporting itself creates new character/relationship/institutional drama.

---

# 16. Scene continuity

Characters remember what just happened.

After:

- Battle;
- death;
- escape;
- betrayal;
- humiliation;
- injury;
- revelation;
- argument;
- affection;
- fear;
- failure;

do not emotionally reset everyone merely because a new scene begins.

The next conversation must feel causally downstream of the previous one.

---

# 17. Debrief / ending rule

Debriefs are especially vulnerable to turning into admin forms.

Do not structure them as:

```text
What happened?
What did you see?
What did you learn?
What do you think it means?
```

unless that interrogation style is genuinely character/institution appropriate and dramatically active.

A debrief should still contain:

- people;
- relationships;
- reaction;
- withholding;
- judgement;
- disagreement;
- concern;
- relief;
- humour;
- institutional pressure;
- consequence.

Do not use terminal scenes to explain the lesson of the route.

Trust the route to have already communicated most of its meaning.

---

# 18. Choice wording

Choice text should sound like an actual decision by the current protagonist.

Avoid:

- QA labels;
- database labels;
- abstract morality labels;
- system categories;
- “success/failure” language;
- meta Story terminology.

Choice labels may remain concise, but should preserve character/situation voice where appropriate.

Semantic IDs remain stable separately.

---

# 19. Player-facing Story acceptance gate

Before any scene is called player-facing complete, remove all author notes and read only what the player receives.

Ask:

1. Does this feel like people living through an event?
2. Or does it feel like a test specification speaking through characters?
3. Can the player understand at least one important thing without narration explaining its significance?
4. Does each exchange actually respond to the previous line/action?
5. Are voices distinguishable without name tags?
6. Is any character serving only as a clue/exposition/joke/grading device?
7. Is the scene repeating a conversational pattern already used repeatedly nearby?
8. Does narration describe physical/emotional experience rather than system logic?
9. Does the scene trust the player?
10. Did the scene change something?

If the answer exposes a documentation-like scene, rewrite it.

---

# 20. Speaker-blind / relationship-swap test

Existing voice tests remain binding.

Additionally:

### Speaker-blind
Remove speaker names.

If multiple named characters still sound interchangeable, rewrite.

### Relationship-swap
Imagine the same lines spoken to a different relationship.

If nothing materially changes, the relationship is not affecting the scene enough where it should.

### System-strip test
Remove every sentence that explains:

- player agency;
- Knowledge;
- inference;
- route legality;
- evidence categories;
- semantic meaning.

If the scene stops making sense, the scene is relying on system explanation instead of dramatization.

Rewrite the scene so the event itself carries the meaning.

- relationship reset;
- exposition loops;
- over-explained consequences;
- narration explaining themes;
- debrief repetition;
- scenes that do not change anything;

- every Origin;
- Arc 1;
- Arc 2;
- Arc 3;
- Canon Route 4–8;
- Alternate Route 4–8;

# 22. Research / planning ceiling

Writing preparation is allowed.

But preparation must terminate.

Once:

- current authority is known;
- participant Knowledge is known;
- character anchors are sufficient;
- the scene's purpose and rails are known;

the next step is **drafting player-facing Story**.

Do not continue:

- GitHub archaeology;
- web research;
- personality-note expansion;
- semantic restatement;

unless a specific unresolved fact genuinely blocks the scene.

Canonical shorthand:

> **Research until you know who is in the scene and what is true. Then write the scene.**

# 23. Stephen review / self-certification boundary

Checklists are not a substitute for player-facing quality.

Writing may prove:

- semantics preserved;
- Knowledge preserved;
- voice anchors consumed;
- route facts preserved.

Writing may **not** self-certify subjective Story quality merely because the checklist passes.

For active production rewrites where Stephen has already rejected the quality:

```text
Writing drafts coherent scene family
-> Stephen reviews actual Story
-> Writing revises
-> approved player-facing scene becomes durable
-> Coding consumes exact approved content
```

Do not bulk-author several large scene families after Stephen has identified a repeated style failure.

---

# 24. Coding boundary

Coding must consume approved player-facing Story as content/presentation authority.

Coding should not:

- rewrite awkward dialogue;
- add semantic explanatory prose;
- paraphrase approved lines;
- “clarify” inference by adding narrator text;
- restore retired older prose.

Writing owns prose.

Coding owns faithful runtime delivery.

If runtime constraints force material prose changes, return the exact constraint to Writing rather than improvise new Story.

# 26. What this rule does NOT require

This standard does not require:

- every scene to be funny;
- every scene to be dialogue-heavy;
- every character to speak;
- every ambiguity to be resolved;
- every NPC to have a full personality dossier;
- every scene to branch;
- every scene to contain a choice;
- every line to be subtle;
- every canon character to reproduce canon dialogue verbatim.

It requires Story to feel authored as **experience**, not as documentation.

> **All Shinobi Chronicles player-facing Story—every Origin, Arc, Canon route, Alternate route, Mission, Event, debrief, ending, narration, dialogue and conversation, including future village content—must dramatize semantic truth rather than explain the underlying rules. ## 28. Mandatory Writing startup preflight

Before any current or future Writing workspace drafts, rewrites, expands, compresses or materially edits player-facing Shinobi Chronicles Story, it must complete a source-first **WRITING PREFLIGHT**.

This preflight is mandatory whenever:
- a new Writing chat/workspace begins substantial work;
- the task switches to a different Origin / Arc / character family;
- Stephen says Writing has drifted;
- a candidate is reopened for voice/personality repair;
- the chat is uncertain which Story/voice authority is current.

Writing must not draft first and audit later.

### 28.1 Required live authority fetch

At minimum, Writing must fetch and read the current live versions of:

1. this universal quality gate;
2. `Documentation/Story/Writing_Anti_Flattening_Character_Voice_and_Conversation_Hard_Gate_2026-09-30.md`;
3. `Documentation/Coordination/Story_Common_Sense_Causality_Rational_Actor_and_Hotspot_Conversation_Gate_2026-09-30.md`;
4. the exact current Story authority for the active Origin/Arc/scene;
5. the current character-specific voice/personality anchor(s) for every major named participant;
6. any current scene-specific / benchmark / owner correction that directly governs the task.

Do not rely on Project memory or an old summary when a live durable source exists.

### 28.2 Required preflight receipt

Before drafting, Writing must return a compact receipt containing:

- **CURRENT STORY AUTHORITY:** exact path + current SHA;
- **VOICE ANCHORS:** exact path(s) + current SHA(s);
- **QUALITY GATES:** exact path(s) + current SHA(s);
- **5 NON-NEGOTIABLE RULES FOR THIS TASK:** concrete rules derived from those sources;
- **VOICE DIFFERENTIATORS:** one short line per major named participant stating what makes their speech/behaviour distinct in this scene;
- **DRIFT RISKS:** the 2–4 most likely failure modes for this exact task;
- **CAUSAL SPINE:**
  - Trigger;
  - Direct observers;
  - Safe inference;
  - Current participant wants;
  - Premise invalidated? YES/NO + why;
  - Why this action/conversation happens now;
  - What changes afterward;
- final lines:
  - **CAUSAL PREFLIGHT: GREEN**
  - **WRITING PREFLIGHT: GREEN**.

If a required source cannot be found or conflicts with another current source, Writing must stop and resolve that before drafting.

### 28.3 No performative acknowledgements

The following do NOT count as preflight:

- “I’ve read the rules.”
- “I’ll follow #333.”
- “Understood.”
- generic paraphrase of Project instructions;
- quoting only issue titles;
- relying only on memory;
- listing rules without exact live paths/SHAs.

The receipt exists to prove actual live-source consumption.

A preflight may not be GREEN when its causal spine contains an unexplained jump.

If Writing cannot answer why a participant acts now, why the prior activity continues after a disruptive event, or what directly observed fact justified a reaction, it must stop before drafting.

### 28.4 Stephen gating phrase

Stephen may enforce this at any time with:

> **WRITING PREFLIGHT FIRST. DO NOT DRAFT.**

On receiving that instruction, Writing must return only the preflight receipt and must not author player-facing Story until Stephen continues the task.

### 28.5 Preflight is not bureaucracy

The receipt should be short.

It is not permission for:
- another archaeology sweep;
- canon over-research;
- long design analysis;
- restating every Writing rule.

The purpose is to force the active workspace to load the right rules and voices before prose generation.

Canonical rule:

> **No live authority receipt -> no draft.**

And:

> **If Writing cannot prove what rules and voices it is using, it is not ready to write.**
