## 1. Start boundary

M11 begins after M10 establishes:

- Minato remains lawful Hokage with a functioning but distrusted central verification/command model;
- Sasuke now leads a credible independent accountability/security coalition with demonstrated operational value;
- Menma has publicly favoured independent verification + source protection rather than exclusive truth custody by either camp;
- hostile information actors have proven they can coordinate true/false/edited pressure windows around Konoha reactions.

The next escalation is not another forged order pretending to be legitimate.

It is **two real, competing priority structures** trying to protect different things at the same time.

---

## 2. Section 1 — simultaneous emergencies

Two real emergencies erupt inside the same response window.

### Emergency A — central command/communications attack

A coordinated sabotage operation targets Konoha's emergency communications / dispatch continuity.

If successful, the village loses the ability to verify and coordinate orders while hostile packets are still circulating.

### Emergency B — protected witness/evidence site attack

A separate strike targets a site holding protected participants and independently preserved evidence tied to M10's forged-notice operation.

If successful:

- witnesses may be killed/taken;
- independent evidence may disappear;
- Minato's central record becomes the only surviving version, which would itself become politically explosive.

Both emergencies are real.

Konoha cannot fully concentrate its strongest response on both at once.

---

## 3. Section 2 — the Hokage order

Minato issues the formal priority:

> **Hold command and communications first. Keep the village capable of verifying orders. Secondary forces protect the witness/evidence site until command stabilises.**

His reasoning is legitimate:

- if central communications collapse, every other operation becomes easier to manipulate;
- preserving command allows reinforcements to be redirected as facts change;
- hostile actors have repeatedly weaponised confusion about what orders are real.

---

## 4. Section 3 — the second order

Sasuke refuses the priority allocation.

His coalition issues/organises a competing emergency response:

> **Move enough force to the witness/evidence site now. If the people and independent proof are erased while everyone protects the command centre, restoring communications only restores the same monopoly everyone is already fighting about.**

This is not a forged Hokage order.

It is an openly competing operational directive from a coalition that now has enough support to act.

Sasuke does not claim to be Hokage.

He claims Minato's priority is wrong.

---

Menma does not obey Minato's priority or join Sasuke's protection column.

## 8. Section 8 — the tradeoff becomes public

By the time the immediate attacks are contained, Konoha can see exactly what each choice preserved and cost.

### Minato's priority preserved

- central command continuity;
- ability to invalidate follow-up false orders;
- coordinated emergency response capacity.

Cost:

- witness/evidence site initially received fewer formal resources;
- one independent evidence cache was lost;
- people at that site know they were not the Hokage's first priority.

### Sasuke's priority preserved

- protected witnesses;
- enough independent evidence/provenance to prevent the site from being politically erased;
- confidence among people who believed central command would sacrifice them for institutional continuity.

Cost:

- Sasuke pulled people/resources outside the formal allocation;
- the existence of an openly competing command response further weakened Minato's ability to claim singular emergency authority;
- the coalition could not protect every piece of evidence/infrastructure.

### Menma's independent route preserved

- proof of synchronised manipulation;
- disruption of the second wave;
- evidence that both attacks were intended to expose/strain Konoha's command split.

Cost:

- Menma personally reinforced neither threatened front;
- his choice depended on other people surviving the immediate attacks without him;
- the coordinator escaped.
