# Shinobi Chronicles — Interactive Scene Performance + Branch Realization Doctrine

**Date:** 2026-09-14  
**Owner:** Writing / Story — Konoha  
**Status:** **BINDING STORYWIDE AUTHORING + IMPLEMENTATION ACCEPTANCE LAW**

## 1. Purpose

Shinobi Chronicles Story must be authored and projected as **lived scenes**, not as semantic summaries, exposition cards, dialogue transcripts without physical action, or button-driven branches that immediately reconverge onto identical visible text.

This doctrine locks the relationship between:

- narration;
- actor dialogue;
- body language / physical reaction;
- environment / movement;
- meaningful player choice;
- factual resolver outcome;
- visible consequence;
- changed subsequent situation.

It also defines how to author **alternative branches that Stephen did not personally play/recover**, without inventing arbitrary outcomes or treating one recovered route as universal canon.

This doctrine applies to:

- all ten Academy Origins;
- Arc 1, Arc 2 and Arc 3 player-facing Story implementation;
- Arc 4–8 Canon route;
- Alt routes;
- Rogue route;
- Non-Conformance route;
- Leader / Take-the-Throne route;
- Great Nation travel and route-specific foreign deployments;
- hidden-area Story introductions and trials;
- Infinite Tsukuyomi Story framing;
- future Beta village Canon + Alt campaigns;
- future All-Route / Ōtsutsuki convergence;
- later Shinobi Chronicles Story unless explicitly superseded.

It does **not** reopen already-closed factual history merely to make a scene more cinematic.

# 2. Core storytelling grammar

The default scene rhythm is:

> **narration → actor dialogue → visible reaction/body language → environmental or spatial change → further dialogue where natural → meaningful player choice → narrated player action → actor/world reaction → factual resolver → visible consequence → changed next situation**

Not every scene requires every element, and not every line needs a gesture.

But the player-facing scene should normally combine enough of them that the player experiences an event rather than reads a report.

## Narration

Narration owns:

- what the camera/player can perceive;
- movement;
- posture;
- eye-line;
- hands;
- breath;
- facial reaction where visible;
- spatial relationships;
- environmental motion;
- physical consequences;
- transitions between positions/locations;
- selective attention appropriate to the protagonist's legitimate observation.

Narration should often let the player **notice what the protagonist notices** rather than stating that the protagonist is clever, suspicious, frightened, confident, etc.

Preferred principle:

> **Show the observation; do not explain the intelligence.**

Example pattern:

Bad:
> Kakashi notices that the man is suspicious.

Better:
> The man checks the rooftops twice. Kakashi stops watching his face and starts watching his hands.

Narration may be brief. It is not permission for another wall of prose.

## Actor dialogue

Dialogue owns:

- what a character actually says;
- character-specific cadence;
- interruption;
- disagreement;
- withholding;
- bluffing;
- humour;
- threat;
- uncertainty;
- emotional reaction;
- relationship-specific phrasing;
- information that actor legitimately knows and chooses to communicate.

Dialogue must not become a disguised design document.

Named characters must remain recognisably distinct. If lines can be exchanged among named speakers without meaningful loss of identity, the writing has failed.

## Player choice

A player choice owns **protagonist intent / attempted action**, not guaranteed success.

Choice buttons should appear at genuine decision points, not after every sentence.

Canonical rule:

> **Player agency requires meaningful decisions, not constant decisions.**

## Resolver

The correct owning system resolves factual success, partial success, failure, custody, injury, Battle outcome, Knowledge, capability use, ownership, access, opportunity, etc.

Story must not pre-win an outcome belonging to Battle, World, Skills, Acquisition, Progression, Registry, or another owner.

## Visible consequence

Where the result can reasonably be observed, the scene should show it:

- somebody reacts;
- somebody moves;
- an object changes hands;
- a door closes;
- a person escapes;
- a character is injured;
- evidence is damaged/revealed;
- an NPC refuses or assists;
- trust/hostility changes visibly;
- the environment changes;
- the next problem becomes different.

Do not hide all meaningful consequence in invisible ledger state when Story can dramatize it.

# 3. Recovered route != universal canon route

When Stephen recovers or approves one complete playthrough route through a Story unit, that route becomes an **authoritative realised branch**, not a mandate that all other choices secretly collapse back into it.

For Kakashi specifically, the recovered eight-scene path is a legitimate realised Kakashi Chronicle route and a benchmark for tone, pacing and scene depth.

It is **not** evidence that:

- every player must make the same choices;
- alternative choices must produce the same visible sequence;
- the same actors must react identically;
- the same information must be learned;
- the same Battle must occur;
- the same object custody must result;
- the same relationship evidence must exist;
- the same ending interpretation must be selected.

Canonical rule:

> **Recovered path = one realised Chronicle history.**  
> **Choice architecture defines possible histories.**  
> **The resolver commits which history actually happened.**

# 4. How to author an unplayed alternative choice intelligently

Alternative branches must be derived from the same factual starting state, not invented as flavour variants.

For every meaningful choice, author the branch using this sequence.

## A. Freeze the pre-choice factual state

Record only what is true immediately before the decision:

- who is present;
- where everyone is;
- object custody;
- what each actor legitimately knows;
- active threats/opportunities;
- protagonist capabilities/access;
- unresolved World facts;
- current relationships/obligations;
- environmental constraints.

Do not import knowledge from another branch.

## B. Define the protagonist's intent

Ask:

> **What is the player actually trying to do?**

Examples:

- observe longer;
- get closer;
- attack;
- steal the package;
- protect somebody;
- pursue a fleeing actor;
- ask for help;
- withhold information;
- negotiate;
- abandon an objective;
- trust another person with part of the problem.

Do not translate this automatically into success.

## C. Apply real capability + environmental constraints

Ask:

- Can this protagonist legitimately attempt it?
- What makes success easier or harder?
- What can they perceive from this position?
- What physical route exists?
- Which Skill/Technique/Stat/Knowledge is actually available?
- Is a Battle, World, capability or social resolver required?

Superior capability should remain genuinely superior. Do not secretly nerf a strong protagonist merely to force the preferred lesson.

Likewise, capability does not make the world freeze or guarantee clean success.

## D. Let NPCs act autonomously

Every materially involved NPC should react from:

- their own Knowledge;
- personality;
- goal;
- fear/confidence;
- obligations;
- capability;
- prior relationships/history;
- what they actually observed.

The player choosing `Attack` does not command every NPC to stand still and receive the attack.

The player choosing `Trust them` does not force the other character to cooperate.

NPC autonomy is part of the branch.

## E. Resolve the factual outcome

Use the correct owning resolver.

Possible results may include:

- success;
- partial success;
- failure;
- success at one objective but loss at another;
- new information at increased risk;
- objective secured but suspect escapes;
- Battle won but package lost;
- package recovered but intelligence missed;
- ally helps but relationship interpretation differs;
- attempted intervention fails but still creates real history.

Do not collapse multi-dimensional outcomes into one generic `success/failure` bit when later Story cares about those distinctions.

## F. Dramatize the immediate branch result

Every meaningful choice must receive an **immediate visible realization** before any convergence.

Minimum expectation:

> **player action → specific narration/physical action → actor/world reaction → changed local state**

Bad:
> Choose Attack → same package-transfer paragraph as Observe.

Good:
> Choose Attack → Kakashi moves early → both men react → the handoff changes under pressure → the next factual state reflects that intervention.

## G. Carry the altered state forward

Later beats must inherit what actually happened:

- Knowledge;
- custody;
- injury;
- position;
- escape;
- trust/hostility;
- evidence;
- Battle participation;
- Shared History;
- missed opportunities;
- object state;
- who witnessed what.

Convergence is allowed only where the factual states are genuinely compatible.

Canonical rule:

> **Convergence may simplify future presentation; it may not erase meaningful Chronicle difference.**

---

# 5. Equal legitimacy != equal length, reward, or outcome

Alternative branches do not need identical scene counts.

They do not need equal rewards.

They do not need equal success probability.

They do not need to reach the same Battle.

They do not need to expose the same information.

They **do** need to be:

- causally coherent;
- character/world truthful;
- worth choosing for a legitimate intent;
- visibly realised;
- historically preserved where meaningful.

A choice may be tactically poor without being narratively fake.

A failure may be a valid Chronicle history rather than a reset condition.

A protagonist may make the correct call and still suffer from incomplete information or another actor's independent decision.

# 6. Branch-writing worksheet — required for major choices

For each major Story decision, Writing should be able to answer:

1. **Pre-choice truth:** what is factually true now?
2. **Player intent:** what is this option trying to accomplish?
3. **Immediate action:** what does the protagonist physically/s verbally do?
4. **NPC response:** how do involved actors independently react?
5. **Resolver:** which system decides factual outcome?
6. **Visible result:** what does the player actually see happen?
7. **State delta:** what Knowledge/custody/injury/position/history/etc. changes?
8. **Next pressure:** how is the next situation different because of this branch?
9. **Convergence test:** can this branch legitimately rejoin another path? If yes, what differences remain preserved?
10. **Long-tail hooks:** what later Story/World/Relationship/Recognition material may legitimately remember this occurrence?

If these cannot be answered, the branch is not ready for production.

---

# 7. Environment and scene continuity

A new dialogue beat does **not** require a new backdrop.

A backdrop changes when the meaningful physical space changes, not merely because the Story advanced one beat.

Canonical rule:

> **new dialogue beat != new backdrop**  
> **new meaningful physical space ≈ new backdrop**

Within one location, staging may change through:

- closer framing;
- actor cards/portraits moving;
- object appearance/custody;
- character entrance/exit;
- light environmental state;
- damage/evidence overlays where supported;
- dialogue focus.

Scene continuity should help the player understand where events are happening.

Canonical boundary:

> **Same history, better lived realization.**

Where an existing player choice already has different semantic outcomes, those differences must be visibly dramatized rather than hidden behind one shared paragraph.

# 10. Canon / Alt / Rogue / Non-Conformance / Leader application

All major route families consume this doctrine.

## Canon

Canon route is not permission to turn source/canon events into an on-screen novel.

The protagonist must inhabit events, make legitimate decisions, experience NPC autonomy and see consequences.

## Alt

Alt route must not be `same exposition, different final paragraph`.

Divergence must be experienced through changed scenes, actor behaviour, opportunities, consequences and later inherited state.

## Rogue

Rogue Story must make institutional absence/exclusion/freedom materially lived through location, access, trust, resources, pursuit, allies and World reaction.

Do not express Rogue status only through labels.

## Non-Conformance

Non-Conformance must be experienced through situations where existing institutional categories genuinely fail to describe the protagonist's relationships/position.

Do not reduce it to a route badge or philosophy speech.

## Leader / Take-the-Throne

Leadership decisions must produce visible political and human consequences.

Choosing policy/authority does not command universal agreement. NPCs/factions retain autonomy, Knowledge, interests and capability to resist, comply, negotiate or exploit.

---

# 11. Acceptance test

For every substantial Story unit, ask:

> **Could the player describe what happened as an event they participated in, including what they chose and how people/the world reacted?**

If the answer is instead:

> `I clicked a choice and read another paragraph explaining the state`,

then the scene is not finished.

For every major branch, also ask:

> **If the player chose another legitimate option, would the immediate scene visibly behave differently for causal reasons?**

If no, the choice is probably decorative or under-authored.

---

# 12. Final canonical shorthand

> **Narration is the camera and legitimate perception.**  
> **Dialogue is actor agency.**  
> **Choice is protagonist intent.**  
> **Resolver is factual truth.**  
> **Aftermath is visible consequence.**  
> **Chronicle state is what remains changed.**

And:

> **One recovered playthrough teaches us how one history happened. It does not decide every other history.**

> **Alternative branches are authored by causality, capability, NPC autonomy and committed state — not by guessing flavour text around the same destination.**

> **Shinobi Chronicles Story is something the player inhabits, not a summary they click through.**
