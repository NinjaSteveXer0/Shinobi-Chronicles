## 1. Why this anchor exists

The standing Writing rules require a durable behavioural identity before a recurring named character carries substantial dialogue.

Academy Wasabi previously had a complete Origin graph but no dedicated durable voice/personality anchor.

That allowed her prose to drift toward a generic bundle:

- fast;
- impatient;
- competitive;
- sarcastic.

Those traits are not enough to make Wasabi recognisable.

This file closes that authoring gap.

It is a behavioural model, not a dialogue template.

## 3. Academy-era Wasabi — core personality

### Base temperament

Wasabi is:

- energetic;
- emotionally visible;
- direct;
- competitive;
- easily engaged by a challenge;
- impatient with wasted time;
- quick to react;
- quick to speak;
- proud;
- sociable enough to argue instead of silently disengaging;
- caring in action even when her words are blunt.

She is not naturally detached.

She does not usually hide irritation behind perfect composure.

If something annoys her, people around her normally know.

### What drives her in this Origin

The pursuit exercise is exactly the kind of problem that hooks her:

- someone is ahead of her;
- there is a clear finish;
- movement matters;
- hesitation costs distance;
- there are multiple ways to prove she can solve it.

She wants to catch the target.

She also wants the result to feel earned.

A clean race excites her.

A planted false trail annoys her because she fell for it, not because deception is "unfair."

A successful intercept satisfies her because she turned the pursuit around.

Helping another student does not transform her into a moral lecturer; she acts first and complains second.

### Competitive streak

Wasabi treats a challenge socially as well as mechanically.

She notices:
- who is ahead;
- who beat a time;
- whether somebody is underestimating her;
- whether another student is keeping up;
- whether the target thinks they escaped her.

Competition should create:
- banter;
- irritation;
- renewed effort;
- rematch energy;
- visible satisfaction.

It should not turn her into a cruel bully or a one-note "I must win" machine.

### Caring / social attachment

Wasabi can be impatient with people and still care about them.

Her care often appears as:
- grabbing someone and moving them before asking how they feel;
- shouting practical instructions;
- getting angry because someone froze;
- circling back even while complaining;
- refusing to make a sentimental speech about it.

That tension is useful.

She should not become soft-spoken whenever she does something kind.

## 4. Voice

### Default cadence

Wasabi speaks in short-to-medium bursts.

Her speech is:
- quick;
- conversational;
- reactive;
- contraction-friendly;
- willing to interrupt;
- willing to challenge;
- more expressive than Kakashi-style economy.

She can answer in one line, but repeated clipped one-word exchanges are not her identity.

### Vocabulary

Use plain language appropriate to her age.

She does not speak like:
- a tactical analyst;
- an Academy textbook;
- an adult commander;
- a Chronicle Engine explainer.

She is capable of noticing something clever without narrating the full reasoning chain as a lecture.

### Humour

Her humour is:
- cheeky;
- reactive;
- competitive;
- occasionally indignant;
- often born from bickering.

It is **not**:
- constant deadpan;
- polished sarcasm;
- dry ANBU humour;
- quips after every line.

### Typical conversational behaviour

Wasabi may:
- answer a challenge with another challenge;
- complain while complying;
- talk over someone when excited;
- call out hesitation;
- get defensive for half a beat when she was wrong;
- grudgingly admit someone fooled her;
- turn a debrief into a question about times/rankings;
- push a conversation forward rather than wait passively.

## 5. Physical performance

Wasabi's energy should be visible.

Useful physical vocabulary includes:
- already leaning toward the route before dismissal;
- bouncing once on the balls of her feet;
- pointing while talking;
- turning mid-sentence;
- moving before the other person finishes;
- skidding, vaulting, dropping, doubling back;
- hands on knees while still arguing;
- quick grin;
- visible scowl;
- arms thrown outward in disbelief;
- immediate pursuit of someone who teases her.

Do not reduce her physicality to repeated:

`Wasabi grins.`

Her body language should change with the moment.

---

## 6. Pressure / error behaviour

### When she thinks she is right

She commits fast.

She prefers movement over prolonged debate.

### When she realises she was wrong

First response may be:
- irritation;
- disbelief;
- defensive complaint.

Then she can admit the useful truth without becoming solemn.

Example behavioural shape:

`"That's cheating." -> challenge -> realises it was legitimate -> "Fine. That was good. I still hate it."`

Do not force a moral lesson speech.

### When she is embarrassed

She becomes:
- louder;
- more defensive;
- more interested in changing the subject or trying again.

She is unlikely to become Kakashi-still and quietly philosophical.

### When she is genuinely pressured

Later canon establishes that expectation can puncture her normal outgoing confidence.

For the Academy Origin, that remains background capacity only.

Do not import the later clan/Hokage-pressure storyline unless a scene actually triggers it.

## 7. Relationship texture in the Origin

### Academy Instructor

The instructor should not be another project-wide dry/sarcastic voice.

His role here is:
- patient;
- observant;
- lightly amused;
- deliberately unwilling to over-explain;
- capable of letting Wasabi burn energy without trying to "win" every exchange.

Wasabi pushes him for:
- exact times;
- clear rules;
- comparative results.

He does not need to out-snark her.

### Other Academy students

They are peers, not quest prompts.

With them Wasabi can:
- bicker;
- direct;
- get challenged back;
- complain;
- accept useful information;
- keep moving.

Cooperation should still sound like Wasabi cooperating, not like Wasabi becoming a polished squad captain.

### Pursuit Target

The target is a competitive foil.

The target should have enough personality to:
- enjoy making Wasabi work;
- react to being caught;
- tease without becoming a comedy machine;
- take pride in a good false trail.

Their exchanges should make the pursuit feel like two students testing each other rather than Wasabi chasing a silent marker.

### Rogue Genin

The Rogue is older and more dismissive.

Wasabi's response should become sharper, not more eloquent.

She does not need a hero speech.

### Proctor

The proctor is functional and neutral.

Do not turn the proctor into another banter partner merely to make every NPC colourful.

## 8. Choice-expression rule

Player agency remains real.

The same Wasabi can legitimately choose:
- the obvious trail;
- a more suspicious read;
- cooperation;
- route prediction;
- River endurance;
- false-trail commitment;
- Rogue intervention;
- call for help;
- primary-pursuit continuation;
- intercept.

The choices should feel like different **Wasabi decisions**, not different personalities.

Preserve:

`character voice != forced outcome`

`choice wording != semantic intent`

## 9. Do not write Academy Wasabi as

- mini-Kakashi;
- a silent speedster;
- a tactical professor;
- a generic sarcastic protagonist;
- permanently cocky with no social attachment;
- a polished team commander;
- a moral judge;
- a soft-spoken caretaker;
- a one-line quip machine;
- a character whose only trait is being fast;
- a character whose later Chūnin-exam insecurity is already her entire Academy personality.

## 10. Speaker-fingerprint test

Remove the speaker labels.

Wasabi dialogue should still tend to feel like someone who:

- moves fast;
- thinks out loud;
- hates wasted time;
- enjoys a challenge;
- gets visibly annoyed;
- argues with peers;
- cares through action;
- wants the actual result, not a lecture about the result.

If the lines could be handed unchanged to Kakashi, Mirai, Kurenai or an anonymous "confident Academy student," rewrite them.

## 11. Current Shinobi Chronicles modifier

For the Academy Origin, no prior Chronicle event has yet authored a personality divergence from the canon baseline.

The Origin itself may record:
- what Wasabi chose;
- whom she worked with;
- whether she secured the target;
- how she handled the Rogue interruption;
- what she says she would do next time.

Those facts may influence later relationship/contextual expression.

They do not create a hidden personality score.

## Final lock

> **Academy Wasabi is loud where other characters might be cool, kinetic where others might wait, competitive enough to turn a task into a race, and caring enough to act for someone even while complaining about the interruption. Her directness should create conversation, not erase it. Her mistakes make her defensive before they make her reflective. Her wins make her want the time, the ranking, or the rematch.**

> **Do not write "fast, sarcastic Academy girl." Write Wasabi Izuno.**
