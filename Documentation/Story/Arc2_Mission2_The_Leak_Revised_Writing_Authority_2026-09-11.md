## 2. Mission identity

# ARC 2 — MISSION 2: THE LEAK

### Universal mission purpose

Mission 2 proves one major Arc-2 truth:

> **The protagonist discovers that hostile or competing actors are not merely leaking information; they are deliberately shaping Konoha's response and exploiting the movement that response creates.**

The mission is therefore not fundamentally about discovering one secret, one trait, one Echo, one operative or one mastermind.

It is about learning the operational grammar of Arc 2:

> **information enters Konoha → different people/institutions react → their reaction reveals priorities, procedures, loyalties and vulnerabilities → someone exploits what Konoha just revealed by moving.**

The title **THE LEAK** is intentionally double-edged. Information is leaking out, but Konoha's **response pattern** is also leaking information about Konoha.

---

## 3. Core premise

Shortly after the Arc-1 wind-down, Konoha begins receiving several apparently separate information events tied to genuine unresolved Arc-1 consequences.

The information streams are not identical and need not share one factual source. Across the mission they should include a combination of:

- truth stripped of context;
- partial truth;
- forged or compromised official-looking authority;
- selectively released classified detail;
- a false or altered destination / transfer / protection order;
- a rumour that is accurate enough to force institutional attention;
- one protagonist-specific pressure carrier derived from legitimate committed Arc-1 history.

The adversarial purpose is not simply to make Konoha believe a lie.

The purpose is to make Konoha **move**.

The adversarial field wants to learn or exploit things such as:

- who responds to which kind of threat;
- which office can command which team;
- which people are considered protectable, expendable, sensitive or politically dangerous;
- how quickly Konoha validates an order;
- which channels bypass normal scrutiny under pressure;
- who trusts whom;
- which records or locations become active when particular information appears;
- what the protagonist prioritises when several legitimate pressures compete.

This is the first mission in which the player should feel that **Konoha itself is being studied through its reactions**.

---

## 4. The three-stream structure

Mission 2 should begin with **multiple imperfect information streams**, not one omniscient briefing that already explains the conspiracy.

The exact details may vary by Chronicle, but the authored structure requires three pressure classes.

### Stream A — institutional / operational pressure

A legitimate-looking order, alert, transfer request, protection instruction or records action causes a Konoha institution/team to move.

At least one important part of it is forged, compromised, selectively altered or being interpreted from incomplete information.

This demonstrates that **authority itself can be weaponised**.

### Stream B — human / political pressure

A person, witness, survivor, family, clan, office, civilian group or other legitimate stakeholder reacts to information that is true, partly true or plausibly true.

This stream demonstrates that Konoha is not one mind. People act from their own bounded Knowledge, duties, fears, interests and relationships.

### Stream C — Chronicle-specific pressure

One stream deliberately activates a pressure point that exists because of this protagonist's actual Arc-1 Chronicle history.

Possible families include, only where legitimate:

- survivor history;
- sensitive evidence;
- protected person;
- compromised institution;
- reputation;
- mission disobedience/compliance history;
- foreign contact;
- exposed secret;
- custody/release/escape consequence;
- unresolved investigation lineage;
- relationship / obligation / Shared History;
- capability whose existence is legitimately known;
- Menma/Echo/White-Thread consequences in a Chronicle where an adversary can legitimately know enough to exploit them.

### Hard Menma rule

Menma having an Echo does **not** automatically make Echo the Mission-2 carrier.

The adversarial field must possess a legitimate Knowledge/evidence basis for whichever Menma-specific pressure it uses.

If no actor can legitimately know enough about Echo, Mission 2 must use another real Arc-1 Menma pressure point instead of granting an enemy convenient omniscience.

## 5. Mission sequence

### PHASE 1 — RECALL

Team Anko's Arc-1 wind-down is interrupted by an emergency recall because information connected to unresolved Arc-1 consequences has begun producing contradictory movement inside Konoha.

The mission should not open by announcing that there is a mastermind, an Echo hunter or a solved conspiracy.

The immediate problem is simpler and more believable:

- too many things are moving at once;
- some orders appear legitimate;
- some information is demonstrably true;
- different responders possess different pieces;
- nobody can yet prove which movement is the trap.

The briefing should establish uncertainty rather than erase it.

### PHASE 2 — KONOHA MOVES

Different Konoha participants react independently from their own roles and Knowledge.

Team Anko must not behave as a player-controlled hive mind.

Examples of legitimate autonomous movement include:

- one participant prioritising the safety of a person;
- another tracing the authority/order chain;
- another protecting or securing evidence;
- another watching for counter-surveillance;
- another refusing to abandon an existing duty merely because the protagonist prefers a different route.

The protagonist is then confronted with a meaningful decision about **which pressure to follow or protect**.

For the developer Chronicle, Stephen chooses Menma's intent.

This is the first major player decision of the revised Mission 2.

Story must stop for that decision during live authoring.

### PHASE 3 — THE RESPONSE TRAP

Whichever legitimate route the protagonist follows, the route must gradually reveal that the adversarial field anticipated a Konoha response.

Material ninja tradecraft should make that fact physical rather than purely conversational.

Possible authorised ingredients include, where causally appropriate:

- chakra suppression;
- observation marks;
- Genjutsu/perception manipulation;
- false scent/blood/tracks;
- signal tags;
- burned or partially erased seals;
- false uniforms / courier presentation;
- deliberately exposed evidence;
- abandoned observation positions;
- timing devices or synchronized message drops;
- evidence-destruction teams;
- ambush geometry built around the expected responder path.

The protagonist should be able to realise, before or after Battle depending on legitimate resolution, that the route was not simply hidden from Konoha.

It was **built for Konoha to enter**.

### PHASE 4 — REAL PL BATTLE PRESSURE

Mission 2 must contain real tactical danger.

Where the chosen route legitimately reaches an encounter, Story calls Battle with objectives and participants defined by current state.

Potential objective families include:

- protect a target;
- prevent evidence destruction;
- stop an observer escaping;
- survive an ambush;
- capture a courier/operative;
- recover a response-tracking device/object;
- prevent a false order from completing its intended movement.

Writing does not pre-author detailed Combat choreography or guarantee victory/capture/death.

Battle returns authoritative participant and consequence state.

Preserve:

- Battle victory ≠ mission completion;
- kill intent ≠ death;
- detain intent ≠ custody;
- Battle result ≠ identity Knowledge;
- Battle result ≠ proof of mastermind or political allegiance.

### PHASE 5 — THE REAL REVELATION

The mission's major discovery is **not** `someone knows Menma has an Echo`.

The major discovery is that evidence from multiple response routes can be reconciled into a more dangerous conclusion:

> **somebody was measuring Konoha's reaction.**

The evidence need not be a convenient single villain ledger.

A stronger version uses several bounded facts that only become meaningful together, for example:

- identical timing notation on otherwise unrelated drops;
- matching observation tags at separate response locations;
- a courier route that only makes sense if the sender expected a particular Konoha office to mobilise;
- suppression or ambush preparation positioned before the responders arrived;
- a destroyed message whose surviving portion records response time rather than secret content;
- evidence that the attackers changed behaviour after seeing which Konoha people moved.

The protagonist and Konoha therefore prove:

> **the information was bait, and the response was the intelligence target.**

This is Mission 2's required Arc proof.

### PHASE 6 — POLITICAL AFTERMATH

Mission 2 must leave Konoha with a political/institutional problem rather than a solved mystery.

The immediate consequences should include some combination of:

- reduced confidence in official-looking orders;
- argument over compartmentalisation vs disclosure;
- pressure to centralise validation/security;
- pressure to expose compromised systems rather than hide them;
- damaged trust between people/offices whose movements were manipulated;
- a protected/exposed person whose future relationship to Konoha has changed;
- questions about who had enough legitimate access to make the bait convincing;
- proof that different Konoha actors can sincerely draw different conclusions from the same failure.

Do **not** label these reactions Reform, Dominion, Rogue, Take the Throne or Non-Conformance.

Record the actual history and let later route affinity emerge from provenance-bearing choices, relationships and consequences.

Mission 2 should create doubt about process and competence without declaring Minato wrong, Sasuke right, or vice versa.

## 6. Connection to the wider Arc-2 campaign

Mission 2 is an **early Konoha / Land-of-Fire information-war mission**.

It must not consume so much narrative weight on one personal mystery that the Arc loses its required expansion.

The mission should leave at least one legitimate indication that the pressure network is not necessarily contained inside one Konoha office or one local operative.

That indication may be:

- an external relay;
- financing;
- courier infrastructure;
- foreign contact;
- trade/smuggling route;
- imported seal/material;
- escaped participant route;
- another authorised external connection.

However Mission 2 does **not** automatically identify Kusa or Ame from convenient evidence unless World/Story authority legitimately establishes that connection.

Binding macro future remains:

- early Arc 2 — Konoha / Land of Fire destabilisation;
- mid/late Arc 2 — both **Kusagakure / Land of Grass** and **Amegakure / Land of Rain**;
- exact mission order/caller into those destinations remains later Writing authority until deliberately locked.

Mission 2's job is to make the player understand why Konoha-only assumptions will eventually become insufficient.

## 7. Developer-Menma realisation boundary

For the current developer Menma Chronicle:

- Menma's Arc-1 history remains unusually explosive;
- Echo/False Identity/White Thread/carrier consequences may legitimately matter;
- they are available as candidate pressure only when the exploiting actor's Knowledge is legitimate;
- Menma's personal history may change which bait he finds most urgent;
- Anko, Hinata, Mikoto and other participants remain autonomous and may choose different priorities;
- protagonist choice does not command the whole team;
- Menma's personal pressure may create unique dialogue, danger, relationships and later opportunities without becoming the campaign's universal explanation.

Simple test:

> **Replace Menma's personal pressure with another protagonist's legitimate Arc-1 pressure. Does Mission 2 still work?**

Required answer: **yes**.

If no, Writing has drifted back into protagonist-specific hard-coding.

## 9. Character and presentation rule

Preserve the existing voice differentiation authority.

Especially:

- Menma = analytical literalism, inconvenient questions, technical curiosity, dry humour;
- Anko = sharper, rougher, instinctive, physical, less explanatory;
- Shizune = controlled, practical, compassionate, medically/professionally focused;
- other participants retain their own voice/Knowledge rather than becoming Menma-style banter machines.

Not every line needs a joke.

Mission 2 should feel like **shinobi counter-intelligence under political pressure**, not a succession of quippy clue conversations.
