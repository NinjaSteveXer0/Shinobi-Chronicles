# ARC 3 / MISSION 1 — THE FIRST SHOT

## Sections 1–3 — contested custody becomes real

Arc 3 opens within days of the Arc-2 mirrored-order crisis.

The living operator captured at the M12 distribution node remains one of the few people who can authenticate part of the packet infrastructure firsthand.

Minato's command issues a lawful transfer order moving the operator into a hardened ANBU-controlled debrief site.

Sasuke's coalition formally objects.

Its position is not that Minato forged the order. Its position is that **exclusive Hokage custody of the witness recreates the exact evidence-control problem Arc 2 exposed**.

Sasuke's people demand continued mirrored custody / joint observation.

Minato refuses to let an unelected parallel command veto prisoner movement.

Both claims are genuine.

Menma immediately recognises the problem:

> whoever physically owns the witness will be accused of owning the truth.

## Section 4 — Menma priority

Writing-selected Menma intent:

> **Keep the witness and the authentication record from becoming either side's fucking trophy.**

Menma supports neither exclusive transfer.

He pushes to keep the witness inside a temporary jointly observed review site long enough to complete the already-started provenance record.

This is not accepted quickly enough.

## Sections 5–7 — first blood

ANBU escort personnel arrive to execute Minato's transfer.

Sasuke-aligned security personnel block the route and insist the transfer is politically invalid without mirrored observation.

Neither group begins with an order to kill.

The standoff becomes physical during attempted disarm/separation.

A shinobi is badly wounded.

Another is killed during the escalation.

Exact lethal technique / combat actor attribution is not invented by Writing here; the factual civil-war consequence is that **Konoha personnel have now killed Konoha personnel while executing genuinely competing authority claims**.

The first death is not exposed as another foreign forgery.

The war is real.

## Sections 8–10 — Menma refuses the story

Menma helps preserve:

- the witness alive;
- the provenance copies;
- the fact that both genuine orders existed;
- the fact that neither side entered with a proven premeditated murder order.

Both camps immediately tell selective versions.

Menma refuses to endorse either simplified account.

Kurama observes internally:

**“They have stopped needing outsiders to make them fight.”**

Menma:

*Yeah. Saves everyone time.*

## Sections 11–12 — mobilisation

Minato activates formal emergency mobilisation.

Sasuke orders his coalition to secure people/sites it believes central command may absorb before joint review can occur.

District watches, gates and supply points begin choosing operational command relationships.

# ARC 3 / MISSION 2 — WHO YOU STAND WITH

## Sections 1–3 — everybody asks

The village begins recalling shinobi into explicit chains of command.

Menma receives pressure from both directions.

### Minato

Minato does not ask Menma for ideological loyalty.

He orders Menma and Naruto placed under heightened protected-movement rules because:

- both are his sons;
- both are Jinchuriki;
- the village is now at war with itself;
- kidnapping/coercion/control attempts would be catastrophic.

The rationale is strategically defensible.

To Menma it is also exactly the stronger leash he predicted.

### Sasuke

Sasuke does not promise freedom for free.

His coalition offers Menma independent operational standing and protection from unilateral Hokage custody **if Menma shares enough of his Arc-2 evidence/provenance network to stop Minato from monopolising the record**.

That is also a claim on Menma's leverage.

## Section 4 — Menma refuses both claims

Menma refuses protected confinement/escort as a standing condition.

He also refuses Sasuke's evidence price.

His position is practical:

> **If either of you gets to decide where I can go because I'm useful, I'm not joining you.**

Kurama approves the freedom logic, not the politics.

## Sections 5–6 — family/team autonomy becomes visible

NPCs do not become Menma's party.

- Naruto independently rejects being treated as a strategic object. He agrees to defend people if attacked but does not accept that `Jinchuriki` means another person decides his political allegiance.
- Kushina backs her sons' personhood/autonomy against compulsory strategic ownership while **not** declaring Sasuke correct and **not** abandoning Minato as family.
- Minato does not withdraw the security concern merely because his family is angry with him.
- Anko returns to Hokage-linked field duty because current emergency command and her own service history make that operationally credible; she does not become a Minato propagandist.
- Mikoto does not join Sasuke merely because she is Uchiha; she prioritises Uchiha/Konoha civilians and family safety from her own position.
- Hinata's current contribution centres observation/evacuation/civilian safety rather than automatic allegiance to Menma.

The prior social unit is now operationally split without requiring personal hatred.

## Sections 7–9 — cross-line help

Menma learns a wounded shinobi connected to Sasuke's coalition cannot reach treatment without crossing a Hokage-controlled route.

He uses his mirrored-evidence relationships to arrange a **one-time medically supervised passage** rather than smuggling a combat unit.

Minato-side personnel permit the transfer only after identity/weapon restrictions.

Sasuke-side personnel accept the restrictions because the wounded person otherwise may die.

Menma gains no faction loyalty credit.

He instead gains a precedent:

**bounded neutral passage is possible when both sides believe cheating will be documented.**

## Sections 10–12 — no safe home

Both camps begin asking whether Menma intends to make that passage model repeatable.

Menma refuses to answer yet.

His operational status remains independent and increasingly distrusted by both sides.

**ARC 3 / MISSION 2 COMPLETE.**

Movement restrictions and fractured supply chains begin hitting:

- Academy families;
- mission income;

Menma chooses the corridor over an offered intelligence target.

Not because he has become charitable.

Because a route both sides need gives him leverage neither side can casually destroy.

Kurama:

**“There. A motive I recognise.”**

Menma:

*People staying alive is also useful.*

Kurama:

**“How noble.”**

*Fuck off.*

Menma helps construct a practical passage arrangement using things he already possesses legitimately:

- mirrored evidence/provenance practice from Arc 2;

## Sections 11–12 — independent infrastructure exists

# ARC 3 / MISSION 4 — THE LOCKED GATES

## Sections 1–3 — territorial control hardens

Minato command and Sasuke coalition each establish controlled movement zones.

Infiltration is real enough that checkpoint regimes are not merely political theatre.

Mistakes and abuses also occur.

Menma's Grey Line now crosses areas neither camp wants the other using freely.

## Section 4 — the Jinchuriki question becomes explicit

Minato formalises a limited strategic-security order for high-value targets, including his sons:

- movement outside secured areas should be escorted/registered;
- unscheduled departure during active operations can trigger retrieval.

He frames it as protection and village survival.

Menma hears ownership.

Sasuke publicly opposes exclusive Hokage control over Jinchuriki while privately making clear that **uncontrolled tailed-beast deployment would also be unacceptable to his coalition**.

Menma notices immediately that `freedom` from one side still comes with another side wanting a veto.

## Section 5 — Menma decision

He refuses both veto models.

He accepts specific safety coordination when he chooses it.

He does not accept standing escort/retrieval authority over his person.

He tells Minato, in substance:

> **If you put me on a stronger leash because you're scared someone else might use me, you've already decided I'm equipment.**

Minato does not agree.

The family argument remains unresolved.

## Sections 6–9 — crossing pressure

Menma continues operating the Grey Line using mutually witnessed passage rather than clandestinely moving troops.

A Minato-side checkpoint attempts to stop one transfer because Menma himself is not following the escort order.

A Sasuke-side patrol simultaneously offers passage if Menma moves the route under coalition protection.

Menma refuses both.

The transfer pauses until third-party witnesses from the two sides verify cargo/people separately.

The delay costs time and medicine but preserves the route's independence.

## Sections 10–12 — de facto map

By Mission end:

- Minato retains central Hokage command / major formal institutions;
- Sasuke coalition controls/supports enough eastern/northern security space to function as a real competing authority;
- several civilian/medical passages remain contested;
- the Grey Line is tolerated because destroying it would immediately harm both camps and expose the responsible side.

Menma's independence now has infrastructure—and enemies.

# ARC 3 / MISSION 5 — THE PRICE OF LOYALTY

## Sections 1–3 — both sides try to use the Grey Line

Minato command asks Menma to disclose the location of a wounded Sasuke-aligned intelligence carrier receiving treatment through the corridor.

Reason:

- the carrier is wanted for questioning about a real security incident;
- central command believes delay risks evidence disappearing.

Sasuke coalition separately asks Menma to move a sealed evidence packet through the corridor without Hokage inspection.

Reason:

- coalition analysts believe Minato will suppress it if he sees it first.

Both requests are strategically rational.

Both would destroy the Grey Line's credibility if Menma quietly complied.

## Section 4 — Menma answer

He refuses both.

To Minato's side:

- the wounded carrier remains medically protected until stable;
- questioning may occur under mutually witnessed terms afterward;
- Menma will not provide a hidden extraction opportunity.

To Sasuke's side:

- the packet can move only if it enters the mirrored-record process;
- the corridor is not a covert intelligence tunnel.

## Sections 5–8 — cost

Minato's command restricts Menma's access to several formal channels because he has now actively obstructed an authorised immediate transfer/questioning request.

Sasuke's coalition stops sharing some tactical warnings because Menma refused to privilege its secrecy.

The wounded carrier survives treatment.

The evidence packet is eventually moved under recorded conditions and proves materially useful—but later than Sasuke wanted.

Nobody is fully satisfied.

## Sections 9–12 — Menma doubles down

Menma does not repair either relationship by surrendering the principle.

He instead publishes/records the corridor rule more explicitly:

> **If you want to use it, you do not get to secretly turn it into your side's road.**

Kurama:

**“You have created rules.”**

Menma:

*Temporary rules.*

Kurama:

**“That is how rules introduce themselves.”**

Menma dislikes that answer.

# ARC 3 / MISSION 6 — THE UNCLAIMED

## Sections 1–3 — independence has to become real or die

Both camps now treat Menma's infrastructure as strategically relevant.

Each asks for formal terms.

The Grey Line cannot remain `some kid improvising passage` while armed authorities are fighting around it.

Current Menma history genuinely qualifies for a Non-Conformance route expression because he possesses:

- independently mirrored evidence credibility;
- Kusa/Ame relationships;
- repeated documented refusal to hide troop/evidence movement for either side;
- civilian/medical passage history;
- family ties that give him access but do not automatically control him;
- enough political visibility that neither side can quietly disappear the arrangement without cost.

No hidden route point is used.

## Section 4 — Menma operational commitment

Menma does **not** declare himself Hokage.

He does **not** pledge Sasuke.

He does **not** accept Minato's command.

He turns the Grey Line into a bounded wartime compact with three purposes only:

1. protected civilian/medical passage;
2. mirrored verification/provenance for evidence moved through the network;
3. negotiated exchanges of wounded/prisoners/documents where all involved sides explicitly accept the terms.

Menma refuses military command of faction troops.

His position:

> **It's a route. Not a fucking country.**

Other people continue calling it the Grey Line anyway.

## Sections 5–9 — reactions

### Minato

Tolerates the compact provisionally because it is saving lives and reducing false-order exploitation, but rejects any claim that it supersedes Hokage law.

### Sasuke

Accepts it tactically because mirrored records prevent Minato from owning all evidence, but rejects Menma's attempt to stand outside the legitimacy dispute forever.

### Naruto

Cooperates with evacuation/protection work when he chooses, but does not become Menma's subordinate or formal Grey-Line soldier.

### Kushina

Supports the autonomy/passage principle in concrete cases while retaining her own family/political judgement.

### Kurama

Likes that Menma has made himself harder to cage.

He also points out that becoming necessary is another kind of chain.

Menma cannot convincingly argue otherwise.

## Sections 10–12 — route state

Menma's current Arc-3 trajectory is now **earned Non-Conformance / independent compact**, with Rogue and Take-the-Throne still possible later if history changes.

This is not a permanent metaphysical route lock.

Sasuke's coalition moves to seize/hold an eastern administrative-security corridor linking records, communications and supply access.

Its stated objective is to prevent Minato from slowly isolating coalition districts through central control.

- Hokage central command;
- hospital/medical routes;
- Academy/civilian shelter;
- communications;
- records;
- eastern gate/supply access.

## Section 4 — Menma priority

Menma commits the Grey Line to **hospital + Academy/civilian passage**, not either leadership site.

His reasoning is brutally practical:

> If either Minato or Sasuke loses a building, the war continues. If the hospital corridor collapses, people die before anyone gets to win anything.

## Sections 5–7 — siege pressure

Fighting closes multiple streets.

Menma's people/witness network repeatedly reroutes evacuees rather than contesting military positions.

No Battle win is invented.

Menma cannot save every objective.

While he protects the civilian/medical passage:

- Sasuke's coalition secures a meaningful portion of the eastern administrative/security corridor;
- Minato retains Hokage Tower / central command and key formal communications;
- at least one secondary records/supply facility is damaged/evacuated;
- civilian displacement worsens.

Exact military Combat packages remain downstream implementation work.

## Sections 9–12 — stalemate changes shape

Neither side wins Konoha.

Both prove they can hold territory.

The Grey Line becomes materially harder for either side to absorb because it operated under siege and both camps used it for wounded/civilians.

**what level of power is each side willing to use to break the stalemate?**

# ARC 3 / MISSION 10 — THE CLAIM

## Sections 1–4 — what Minato and Sasuke actually want

War exhaustion makes slogans insufficient.

### Minato's programme

Minato's baseline proposal is:

- restore one lawful command structure;
- preserve mirrored/provenance protections proven useful during Arc 2/3;
- investigate war/security failures after active hostilities cease;
- disarm parallel armed command;
- protect lawful civilian/clan institutions from factional seizure.

He refuses to resign under armed pressure because he believes doing so would make force a legitimate succession mechanism.

### Sasuke's programme

Sasuke's baseline proposal is:

- Minato steps aside from exclusive emergency command pending independent adjudication/restructure;
- evidence/security review cannot return to sole Hokage custody;
- coalition-controlled security remains in place until enforceable power-sharing/accountability exists;
- no forced disarm before those guarantees.

He is willing to lead an interim/restructured command if the settlement requires it.

He does not present himself as a harmless advisor.

The disagreement is now explicit:

**who may legitimately command while the institutions deciding legitimacy are themselves disputed?**

## Section 5 — internal dissent

Neither side is monolithic.

- some Minato supporters want harsher suppression than Minato himself authorises;
- some want concessions/reform immediately;
- some Sasuke supporters want outright seizure;
- others want enforceable oversight without replacing Minato permanently.

The war cannot be reduced to two minds.

## Section 6 — Menma's claim

Menma does not claim Hokage office.

His concrete settlement demand is narrower and more self-interested:

- Grey-Line civilian/medical passage remains protected from faction control;
- decisive political evidence remains mirrored rather than exclusively held;
- neither command receives standing ownership over Menma/Naruto/Kurama or foreign source identities;
- prisoners/wounded exchanges use witnessed terms;
- future settlement must include an enforceable way to challenge emergency authority without creating permanent secret parallel command.

Menma's position is not `both sides are equally bad`.

It is:

> **Neither side has demonstrated that giving it everything is safe.**

## Sections 7–9 — live test

A district under mixed/unclear control suffers a security collapse when both camps initially refuse to let the other's responders enter.

Grey-Line witnesses broker a temporary cross-command response under documented terms.

The crisis is contained enough to prevent mass escalation.

This improves the credibility of bounded joint/third arrangements without proving they can govern all of Konoha.

## Sections 10–12 — stalemate cannot last

Minato refuses Sasuke's demand to step aside.

Sasuke refuses unilateral disarm.

Sasuke concludes central strategic pressure must be increased before negotiation can become real.

Final-offensive preparations begin.

**ARC 3 / MISSION 10 COMPLETE.**

# ARC 3 / MISSION 11 — THE LAST OFFENSIVE

## Sections 1–3 — Sasuke moves

In the current Menma Chronicle, Sasuke authorises a major targeted offensive intended to seize enough central communications/records access to force Minato into a negotiated restructuring.

He does not issue an order to massacre Hokage supporters or civilians.

Minato orders full defence of central command and counter-seizure of captured infrastructure.

Both sides understand this may decide the war's shape.

Menma's Grey Line prepares for mass casualty movement rather than joining either offensive.

## Section 4 — Menma priority

Menma commits to:

> **keep the hospital corridor, verification archive and cross-line medical exchange alive.**

He refuses requests from both sides to turn the corridor into a military flank.

## Sections 5–7 — breakthrough and direct confrontation

Sasuke's coalition achieves a real breakthrough into the central strategic zone.

Minato personally intervenes to prevent collapse of lawful command.

The Arc's headline confrontation becomes factual:

**Minato Namikaze and Sasuke Uchiha directly engage during the central confrontation.**

Writing does **not** declare a PL/combat winner or invent exact Battle mechanics.

The important Story facts are:

- neither man immediately yields;
- the direct clash draws elite attention/resources away from other fronts;
- neither side can treat the rest of the village as irrelevant while the leaders fight;
- the confrontation does not by itself settle political legitimacy.

## Section 8 — the unchosen front breaks

A damaged medical/utility district near the cross-line corridor begins collapsing under fire/sabotage/neglect from the broader battle.

Wounded from **both** camps are trapped.

Continuing the leadership confrontation at full intensity now risks mass preventable deaths and destruction of the last reliable cross-faction medical route.

## Section 9 — Menma creates war-ending leverage

Menma uses the assets he actually earned:

- Grey-Line cross-faction witnesses;
- Kusa/Ame authentication contacts;
- mirrored Arc-2/3 evidence practices;
- access to wounded/civilian movement data;
- current medical-route dependence.

He transmits a verified factual picture to both commands:

- casualty/collapse state;
- which route remains open;
- what will be lost if fighting continues through it;
- proof that neither side can blame the other for every current failure without falsifying the shared record.

Then Menma makes the threat explicit:

> **Use the Grey Line as a battlefield and it closes. No secret crossings. No wounded exchange. No foreign medicine route. Nothing.**

He cannot stop the whole war by himself.

He can make continuing **this** offensive significantly more expensive for both sides.

## Section 10 — autonomous leader decisions

Minato chooses not to sacrifice the remaining medical/civilian route for a chance at decisive local victory.

Sasuke chooses not to turn his breakthrough into an assault through the corridor that would destroy the very accountability/support base he claims to defend.

Their direct confrontation ends **without a declared Combat winner** as both sides disengage/reposition around the casualty/route crisis.

This is not reconciliation.

It is exhaustion, leverage and strategy.

## Sections 11–12 — armistice window

The offensive proves:

- Sasuke can threaten central command;
- Minato can prevent immediate seizure;
- neither can win cleanly without unacceptable village damage under current state;
- Grey-Line/neutral infrastructure now has bargaining power precisely because both sides need it.

A bounded armistice window opens.

**ARC 3 / MISSION 11 COMPLETE.**

# ARC 3 / MISSION 12 — AFTER THE FIRE

## Sections 1–3 — settlement window

Negotiations begin under ugly conditions:

- dead/wounded on both sides;
- damaged districts;
- disrupted Fire-country supply;
- Minato still holds lawful Hokage office/central command;
- Sasuke still commands substantial armed/political support and holds meaningful territory/infrastructure;
- neither side can claim clean victory;
- Grey-Line passages and mirrored evidence are among the few mechanisms still trusted across lines;
- foreign actors are watching.

Nobody calls this peace.

## Section 4 — Menma priority

Menma's priority is not who becomes his boss.

It is making sure the settlement does not let either camp immediately absorb everything he built.

He pushes four concrete conditions:

1. protected medical/civilian corridors remain non-military during the ceasefire;
2. decisive war/Arc-2 evidence remains mirrored and independently verifiable;
3. no forced use/seizure of Jinchuriki/hosted-entity power under ceasefire terms;
4. prisoner/wounded exchanges occur under documented mutual witnessing.

## Section 5 — spoiler pressure

Hardliners from **both** political camps reject compromise for different reasons.

A mixed set of defectors/refusers attempts to seize a gate/arms route and restart open conflict before terms harden.

They are not revealed as one secret organisation.

Some believe Minato must crush the rebellion now.

Some believe Sasuke must finish the seizure while central command is weak.

Some simply fear punishment under any settlement.

## Sections 6–7 — final crisis

Menma prioritises protecting the negotiation verification channel and civilian route rather than hunting every hardliner.

Minato and Sasuke each order their own loyal forces **not** to support the spoiler action.

Once both command structures publicly deny the seizure, its political support collapses.

Remaining armed holdouts are isolated/captured/withdraw through owning resolver channels; Writing does not invent a major Battle victory.

The ceasefire window survives.

## Sections 8–9 — terms

The current Menma Chronicle reaches a fragile settlement family with these Story-level facts:

### Minato

- remains Fourth Hokage;
- retains central lawful administration / core command;
- does not receive immediate unilateral disarm/control of Sasuke's coalition;
- accepts temporary mirrored evidence oversight and protected neutral-corridor terms because continuing war is worse.

### Sasuke

- does not become Hokage in Arc 3;
- retains a recognised armed/political coalition under ceasefire rather than surrendering as ordinary criminals;
- accepts no-expansion / no-first-strike conditions around coalition-held security areas;
- remains the established challenger and political dispute remains unresolved.

### Grey Line / Menma

- remains a bounded independent passage/provenance compact during the ceasefire;
- does not become a third government;
- receives explicit protection for its medical/civilian function so long as it does not move formed combat units or hide faction military operations;
- Menma remains outside both formal command structures.

### War accountability

A future adjudication/restructure process is required by the settlement but **not resolved inside Arc 3**.

That unresolved political problem is deliberate Arc-4 fuel, not missing Arc-3 completion.

## Section 10 — foreign consequence

Kusa/Ame and other observers now see:

- a weakened but surviving Konoha;
- multiple authority centres;
- damaged Fire-country routes;
- an independent cross-line network with foreign contacts;
- opportunities for diplomacy, leverage, infiltration, profit and retaliation.

Arc 4 inherits regional consequence rather than a reset village.

## Section 11 — family / personal consequence

### Minato and Menma

Their relationship is damaged but not erased.

Minato believes Menma repeatedly treated lawful command as merely another negotiable actor during an existential crisis.

Menma believes Minato proved that love does not stop institutions from putting a leash on useful people when fear gets high enough.

Neither apologises the disagreement away.
