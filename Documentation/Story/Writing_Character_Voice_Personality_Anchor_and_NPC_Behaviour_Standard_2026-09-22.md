# Shinobi Chronicles — Writing Character Voice, Personality Anchor and NPC Behaviour Standard

**Date:** 2026-09-22  
**Owner:** Writing / Story — Konoha  
**Status:** **BINDING PLAYER-FACING WRITING STANDARD — ACTIVE**  
**Primary priority:** **FINISH SHINOBI CHRONICLES ALPHA WITH DISTINCT PEOPLE, NOT ONE PROJECT-WIDE VOICE**

---

## 1. Purpose

Shinobi Chronicles uses many established Naruto/Boruto characters alongside original and role-based NPCs.

The project must not collapse those people into:

- one dry/sarcastic house voice;
- one generic "serious shinobi" voice;
- plot-delivery mouths;
- catchphrase caricatures;
- random personality gimmicks;
- dialogue that changes personality scene-by-scene because the plot needs a line.

Canonical rule:

> **Every materially speaking recurring person must have a stable behavioural identity before Writing asks them to carry substantial dialogue.**

And:

> **A personality anchor is a behavioural model, not a dialogue template.**

The anchor constrains and inspires behaviour. It does not prescribe one deterministic response to every situation.

---

# 2. Authority order

For an established canon-derived character:

```text
durable Shinobi Chronicles Recorded History / current Story authority
> deliberate SC divergence
> researched canon personality / era baseline
> model memory / inference
```

Canon establishes the starting person.

Shinobi Chronicles determines what has happened to that person here.

Do not use canon to overwrite closed SC history.

Do not use SC divergence as permission to discard the person's recognisable psychological continuity without causal reason.

For an original NPC:

```text
durable SC NPC/person authority
> current Story role/history/relationships
> newly authored personality anchor
> generic archetype/inference
```

---

# 3. Canon research requirement

Before substantial new dialogue or a major voice rewrite for a canon-derived named character, Writing must consume the project-wide Canon Research First protocol.

Research enough current canon to establish, where evidence allows:

- temperament;
- values;
- social behaviour;
- speech tendencies;
- emotional defences;
- response to pressure;
- relationship-specific behaviour;
- age/era development;
- meaningful contradictions in the character;
- behaviour when confident vs frightened vs angry vs embarrassed vs grieving.

Prefer primary/official sources where available.

Do not research merely to collect catchphrases.

The target is a behavioural model.

---

# 4. Required compact Voice / Personality Anchor

Every materially speaking named/recurring character should have a compact anchor using the following fields.

## Identity / context

- **characterRef / stable name**
- **representation / age / era**
- **role in current SC period**
- **canon-derived | original SC | role NPC**
- **source/provenance** — canon research refs or SC authority
- **SC divergence summary** — only where relevant

## Core personality

- **core temperament**
- **values and priorities**
- **primary emotional defences**
- **social / authority posture**
- **risk / conflict posture**
- **what they seek from other people**
- **what they fear losing / exposing**
- **what they tend to conceal**

## Voice

- **speech rhythm and vocabulary**
- **verbosity**
- **blunt vs indirect**
- **formal vs casual**
- **emotionally expressive vs guarded**
- **humour style**
- **use of profanity / euphemism / titles / honorific-style address where appropriate**
- **question style** — direct, leading, rhetorical, avoidant, etc.
- **silence style** — when they stop speaking rather than fill space
- **what they would almost never say**

## Pressure behaviour

- **response to authority**
- **response to threat**
- **response to uncertainty**
- **response to embarrassment / failure**
- **response to praise / affection**
- **response to grief / loss**
- **what makes them lose composure**
- **how loss of composure manifests**
- **how they recover control**

## Relationship behaviour

- **how they behave toward particular important people**
- **how they treat strangers**
- **how they treat subordinates / superiors**
- **how they treat rivals / enemies**
- **how they behave when protecting someone**
- **how they behave when they distrust someone**

## Continuity / development

- **age/era-specific differences**
- **SC history changes from baseline canon**
- **current relationship/history modifiers**
- **recent event pressure**
- **known personality evolution / arc**
- **protected traits that should not drift without causal Story change**

The anchor should remain compact enough to consult during drafting.

Do not turn every character into a psychological biography unless Story production actually needs one.

---

# 5. Behavioural model, not dialogue template

The anchor must describe tendencies, not deterministic scripts.

Bad:

```text
Kakashi always responds to fear with sarcasm.
Kushina always shouts when angry.
Naruto always jokes after being hurt.
```

Better:

```text
under pressure, this character tends to...
when emotionally cornered, they may...
with this specific person, their normal reserve weakens...
when protecting someone, they prioritise...
```

A person may behave atypically when the situation gives them a reason.

If Writing intentionally breaks an established tendency, the scene should make the reason legible through:

- relationship;
- pressure;
- recent history;
- fatigue;
- fear;
- grief;
- deception;
- role obligation;
- growth/regression.

Canonical rule:

> **Personality creates likelihood and texture, not fate.**

---

# 6. Same person, different relationship

A strong character does not speak identically to everyone.

Writing must consider relationship-specific expression.

The same person may be:

- deferential with a respected authority;
- teasing with a sibling;
- clipped with an enemy;
- gentler with a student;
- evasive with someone who knows too much;
- brutally direct with a trusted equal;
- formally controlled in public but emotionally open in private.

Therefore:

> **voice continuity != identical register with every listener.**

For recurring relationships, preserve compact relationship modifiers rather than reinventing chemistry each scene.

---

# 7. Age / era is mandatory

Do not write a canon character as one timeless composite personality.

Age and era matter.

Writing must ask:

- how old are they here?
- what have they experienced yet?
- what relationships exist yet?
- what responsibilities do they have?
- what emotional defences have or have not developed?
- which future canon experiences have not happened in this Chronicle?

Do not write a young representation with mature knowledge, confidence or social habits solely because the older canon version is more familiar.

Do not strip an older representation of development already earned.

---

# 8. SC divergence must be causal

If Shinobi Chronicles history changes a canon-derived character, record the change in the anchor.

Use:

```text
canon baseline
-> SC divergence/event
-> psychological/relationship consequence
-> current expression
```

Do not simply mark:

`AU personality = different`.

Examples of legitimate divergence effects may include:

- trust changing because a betrayal did/did not occur;
- confidence changing because a role was gained earlier;
- grief or attachment changing because a person survived/died differently;
- rivalry changing because team history differs;
- authority posture changing because institutional experience differs.

The exact divergence must come from durable SC authority.

---

# 9. Knowledge is not personality

Preserve permanently:

```text
personality != Knowledge
personality != motive proof
personality != World Truth
personality != deterministic action
```

A suspicious personality does not authorise hidden information.

An intelligent character does not automatically know the answer.

A confident character may still be factually wrong.

A guarded character may choose not to say something they legitimately know.

Writing must derive speech from:

```text
personality
+ bounded Knowledge
+ relationship/history
+ role/obligation
+ current circumstances
-> situational intent
-> speech/action
```

---

# 10. Original NPC personality construction

Original NPCs should not default to "generic shinobi".

Use proportionate personality depth.

## Tier A — major / recurring named NPC

Use the full compact anchor.

Preserve it durably before or alongside substantial recurring dialogue.

## Tier B — recurring supporting NPC

Minimum anchor:

- temperament;
- primary value;
- social posture;
- pressure response;
- speech register/rhythm;
- private concern;
- one relationship modifier;
- one contradiction or soft edge.

## Tier C — one-scene / functional NPC

Use a light identity:

- role;
- immediate motive;
- communication style;
- one distinguishing behavioural trait.

Do not spend major authoring effort on throwaway background people.

But do not let every guard, merchant, ANBU, rogue or civilian sound identical.

---

# 11. NPC personality recipe

When inventing a new recurring NPC, Writing should normally choose a coherent combination rather than random quirks.

A useful compact recipe:

```text
role / lived context
+ temperament
+ primary value
+ social posture
+ pressure response
+ speech register
+ private concern
+ contradiction
= behavioural identity
```

Example structure only:

```text
ANBU operative
+ patient
+ prioritises operational clarity
+ quietly authoritative
+ becomes more direct when evidence conflicts
+ short precise sentences
+ worries about avoidable civilian exposure
+ unexpectedly gentle with frightened juniors
```

This does not create canon by itself.

It creates a Writing-owned behavioural baseline for an SC original person.

---

# 12. Contradictions make people feel human

Do not make personalities one adjective deep.

Useful contradictions may include:

- brave in Battle, awkward with affection;
- cynical institutionally, loyal personally;
- verbally blunt, conflict-avoidant with family;
- cheerful under stress, privately fatalistic;
- disciplined in public, impulsive when a specific person is threatened;
- compassionate, but unforgiving of one category of betrayal.

Contradiction must be coherent, not random.

A contradiction should arise from values, history or relationships.

---

# 13. Anti-caricature rule

Canon research must not become imitation-by-catchphrase.

Do not reduce:

- Naruto to enthusiasm/catchphrases;
- Kakashi to lateness/sarcasm;
- Kushina to temper;
- Hinata to shyness;
- Sasuke to coldness;
- Anko to innuendo;
- any other named person to their most recognisable surface trait.

A recognisable voice comes from:

- priorities;
- reasoning;
- emotional defences;
- sentence choices;
- relationship behaviour;
- pressure behaviour;
- what they avoid saying.

Catchphrases, verbal tics or iconic traits should be used only where they naturally fit.

---

# 14. Anti-house-voice rule

Writing must actively detect project-wide voice drift.

Red flags:

- everyone becomes dry and sarcastic;
- everyone is eloquently self-aware;
- everyone uses the same sentence length;
- everyone responds to tension with humour;
- everyone speaks in compressed tactical language;
- everyone explains their own psychology;
- everyone gives perfect rebuttals;
- everyone speaks as though they know they are in a branching narrative.

If multiple characters could exchange dialogue unchanged, characterisation has failed.

---

# 15. Plot-mouthpiece test

Before finalising a line, ask:

> **Is this person saying this because they would say it, or because the outline needs the player to receive information?**

If the second answer dominates:

- move information to another speaker;
- turn it into a question/argument;
- split it across interaction;
- use narration if appropriate;
- let the player infer it;
- or preserve legitimate uncertainty.

Do not force a character to become an exposition terminal.

---

# 16. Speaker-blind fingerprint test

For important scenes, temporarily remove speaker labels and review the dialogue.

Ask:

- can several lines still be attributed plausibly?
- do characters use different reasoning, not merely different wording?
- do they react differently to the same event?
- are silences distributed according to personality?
- does humour belong to the speaker?
- does authority change register naturally?

Not every line must be uniquely identifiable in isolation.

The scene as a whole should preserve distinct people.

---

# 17. Relationship swap test

For recurring named characters, ask:

> **Would this line still make sense if the listener were replaced by a stranger, rival, superior or loved one?**

If nothing changes across relationships, the character is probably under-authored.

Relationship-specific expression should affect:

- openness;
- word choice;
- patience;
- humour;
- formality;
- willingness to admit uncertainty;
- willingness to challenge;
- physical distance/body language where authored.

---

# 18. Pressure ladder

For important recurring characters, understand behaviour at different pressure levels.

Suggested model:

```text
CALM
-> ALERT
-> PRESSURED
-> CORNERED
-> LOSS OF COMPOSURE
-> RECOVERY / AFTERMATH
```

Do not jump straight from calm baseline to screaming/violence merely because a scene needs intensity.

The path itself reveals personality.

Not every person reaches every stage the same way.

---

# 19. Silence is part of voice

Do not make every present character speak simply to prove they are present.

A person's voice includes:

- when they answer;
- when they refuse;
- when they let another person hang themselves with their own words;
- when they change the subject;
- when they communicate physically;
- when they leave.

Silence must still be intentional and scene-readable.

---

# 20. Personality continuity ledger

A recurring character anchor should be durable.

When Story events legitimately change the person:

- update the anchor or add a dated/history-sensitive modifier;
- preserve the old baseline/provenance;
- state what caused the change;
- do not silently rewrite past personality to match the present.

Canonical distinction:

```text
baseline personality
!= temporary mood
!= relationship-specific behaviour
!= history-driven development
!= current scene pressure
```

This prevents temporary anger in one Mission from becoming the character's new permanent voice.

---

# 21. Scene-local expression overlay

Before drafting a substantial scene, combine the stable anchor with current scene state.

Compact scene overlay:

- current objective;
- current mood/pressure;
- relevant recent event;
- current listener(s);
- current Knowledge;
- what the character wants from this interaction;
- what they refuse to reveal;
- what line they will not cross;
- whether their normal voice is currently strained.

Then draft.

Do not mutate the permanent anchor merely to fit one scene.

---

# 22. Player character / representation handling

Where the protagonist is an authored named representation:

- preserve that representation's personality/voice;
- player choice may legitimately alter immediate intent/attitude;
- do not make every selected option sound like a completely different person unless the Chronicle explicitly supports such divergence.

Choice variation should normally remain recognisable as:

`this character choosing differently`

rather than:

`a different personality spawned by the button`.

---

# 23. Personality and player agency

Personality should shape available expression without silently removing legitimate player agency.

Preserve:

```text
character voice != forced outcome
choice wording != semantic intent
player intent != resolver result
```

If a choice belongs to the player, Writing may phrase it character-appropriately while preserving the actual semantic distinction.

---

# 24. Full-scene character audit

Before player-facing approval, Writing should inspect:

- who dominates the conversation and why;
- who is silent and why;
- whether anyone became a plot mouthpiece;
- whether anyone's Knowledge leaked;
- whether humour belongs to the right person;
- whether every participant reacts from their own priorities;
- whether two characters collapsed into the same cadence;
- whether the scene changed someone's mood but not their underlying personality;
- whether relationship differences are legible;
- whether age/era remains correct.

---

# 25. Full-Origin / Arc voice audit

Before an Origin or Arc is treated as player-facing Writing complete:

- inventory materially speaking recurring characters;
- confirm each has a current anchor;
- confirm canon-derived anchors used current research where needed;
- confirm recurring original NPCs preserve their authored anchors;
- inspect for house-voice drift;
- inspect for personality reset between Missions;
- inspect for unearned personality change;
- inspect for future-era voice leaking backward;
- inspect for dialogue that could be reassigned freely between speakers;
- update durable anchors when Story actually develops a person.

---

---

# 27. Relationship mini-anchor template

For recurring important pairs:

```text
PAIR:
CURRENT RELATIONSHIP:
SHARED HISTORY:
ASYMMETRIC KNOWLEDGE:
TRUST:
OPEN CONFLICT:
UNSAID ISSUE:
REGISTER CHANGE:
HUMOUR / FAMILIARITY:
WHAT ONE CAN SAY TO THE OTHER THAT THEY WOULD NOT SAY ELSEWHERE:
CURRENT STORY MODIFIER:
```

Use only where the relationship materially affects scenes.

Do not create relationship bureaucracy for every pair of people who meet once.

---

# 28. NPC durability rule

Once a recurring original NPC has a meaningful personality:

> **Do not regenerate their personality from scratch next time they appear.**

Preserve the anchor durably.

A returning NPC should feel like the same person even if:

- their role changes;
- their mood changes;
- their loyalty changes;
- their relationship with the protagonist develops.

Development should have causes.

# 30. Final lock

> **Canon-derived characters begin from researched canon personality at the correct age/era, then diverge only through actual Shinobi Chronicles history. Original NPCs receive proportionate authored personalities instead of generic role voices. Every recurring person's anchor is a stable behavioural model: temperament, values, speech, pressure response, relationships, concealment, limits and development. It guides dialogue without turning personality into deterministic scripting.**

> **If the names are removed and everyone still sounds like the same writer, the scene is not finished.**
