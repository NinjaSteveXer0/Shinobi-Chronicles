# Shinobi Chronicles — Writing Group Conversation, Character Interaction and Scene Liveliness Standard

**Date:** 2026-09-22  
**Owner:** Writing / Story — Konoha  
**Status:** **BINDING PLAYER-FACING WRITING QUALITY STANDARD — ACTIVE**  
**Primary priority:** **FINISH SHINOBI CHRONICLES ALPHA WITHOUT FLAT OBSERVATION-ONLY SCENES**

## 1. Purpose

Shinobi Chronicles Story must read like people actually occupying a scene together, not like an observer describing everyone one-by-one.

A recurring failure mode has been:

```text
scene has several characters
-> narration explains what each person does/thinks
-> characters barely address each other
-> player receives observations instead of a conversation
-> scene feels robotic, staged or like an admin summary
```

Canonical rule:

> **When multiple speaking-capable characters are present, default to interaction between them. Narration supports the interaction; it does not replace it.**

This standard does not require every character to speak in every scene. Silence may be intentional. But silence must be a character/scene choice, not the default caused by weak drafting.

---

## 2. Scene prewrite brief

Before drafting a dialogue-bearing scene, Writing must establish:

- who is physically present;
- who can hear whom;
- what each participant currently knows;
- what each participant wants in this scene;
- what each participant is worried about / hiding / resisting;
- current relationship/attitude between participants;
- what changed immediately before the scene;
- what the scene must accomplish;
- where the player can meaningfully intervene.

Do not begin free-writing until these are clear.

---

## 3. Conversation must have exchange

A multi-character dialogue scene should normally contain at least some genuine exchange:

- question -> answer / refusal / evasion;
- statement -> reaction;
- accusation -> defence / counterattack;
- joke -> response;
- disagreement;
- interruption;
- correction;
- uncertainty;
- tactical discussion;
- emotional reaction;
- one character changing another's immediate behavior;
- silence that another character notices.

Avoid sequences where characters only deliver isolated exposition to the protagonist.

---

## 4. NPCs talk to each other

The protagonist is not the only valid conversational target.

NPCs may:

- disagree with one another;
- answer one another;
- interrupt;
- argue about the protagonist;
- coordinate;
- react to another NPC's mistake;
- withhold information from one participant while another knows it;
- form their own temporary alliance or disagreement.

Canonical rule:

> **A group scene is not a queue of NPCs waiting to speak to the player.**

---

## 5. Narration role

Narration should provide what dialogue cannot efficiently provide:

- movement;
- spatial change;
- facial/body reaction;
- environmental tension;
- action beats;
- silence;
- observer-safe sensory information.

Narration should not repeatedly substitute for direct interaction such as:

`Kakashi seems annoyed. Anko seems suspicious. Pakkun seems unconvinced.`

when those attitudes could naturally appear through what they say/do to each other.

---

## 6. Distinct voices

Named characters must not sound interchangeable.

Before substantial dialogue, use current canon/SC personality authority to anchor:

- vocabulary;
- sentence rhythm;
- bluntness/politeness;
- humour;
- confidence;
- emotional restraint;
- relationship-specific behavior;
- profanity where appropriate;
- what they would never say.

Do not force a project-wide house voice.

---

## 7. Knowledge discipline

Conversation is constrained by each participant's Knowledge.

A character may:

- be wrong;
- misunderstand;
- ask a question;
- infer;
- suspect;
- refuse to believe;
- learn during the scene.

Do not use omniscient dialogue merely to move the plot.

Preserve:

`World Truth != participant Knowledge != what participant says`.

---

## 8. Player choices

Choices should represent meaningful protagonist intent, not replace normal conversation pacing.

Do not insert a choice after every line.

Use player choice when:
- intent genuinely branches;
- attitude materially matters;
- action changes the state;
- response would reasonably belong to the player rather than authored protagonist continuity.

NPC conversation should continue naturally between meaningful player decisions.

---

## 9. Observation-only scenes

Observation-heavy writing is acceptable when the scene is intentionally:

- surveillance;
- stealth;
- discovery from concealment;
- silent aftermath;
- internal reflection;
- visual reveal;
- non-verbal tension.

But once characters are openly interacting, Writing should not remain stuck in surveillance prose.

---

## 10. Scene liveliness audit

Before a scene is marked Writing-complete, ask:

1. Do the characters speak **to one another**, not only at the protagonist?
2. Can I tell who is speaking without the name tag?
3. Does each line follow from what the previous person actually said/did?
4. Is narration doing physical/environmental work rather than replacing conversation?
5. Does anyone disagree, question, react, interrupt, misunderstand or adapt where natural?
6. Are Knowledge boundaries visible in the conversation?
7. Does the scene feel like people in a room, not notes about people in a room?
8. Is any character present but inexplicably inert?
9. Is dialogue carrying exposition no person would naturally say?
10. Does the scene still accomplish its exact semantic purpose?

If a multi-character scene fails several of these, it is not player-facing Writing complete.

---

## 11. Stephen review model

Stephen does **not** need to write every scene himself.

Preferred model:

```text
Stephen sets scene intent / key beats / any must-have lines
-> Writing produces participant brief
-> Writing drafts natural interaction
-> Stephen reviews the actual scene
-> Writing revises
-> exact approved scene becomes durable
```

If Stephen supplies exact dialogue, preserve it verbatim unless he later approves a change.

If Stephen supplies only the intended meaning, Writing owns producing natural dialogue and should not treat his summary wording as literal dialogue unless he says so.

---

## 12. Free-writing boundary

Free-writing is allowed only inside closed semantic rails.

Writing may improvise:
- conversational rhythm;
- physical beats;
- character-appropriate reactions;
- humour;
- tension;
- line-level phrasing.

Writing may not improvise:
- new factual history;
- hidden Knowledge;
- unapproved participant presence;
- Battle result;
- package/custody state;
- reward;
- Rank/PL/progression;
- new branch outcome;
- canon contradiction.

---

## 13. Full-Origin preflight

Before an Origin implementation package is handed to Coding, perform one whole-Origin read-through specifically for conversational quality.

Check for:
- observation-heavy scenes that should be conversations;
- NPCs who never respond to each other;
- repeated exposition;
- stale earlier drafts;
- voice drift;
- abrupt emotional resets after Battle/kill/defeat;
- silent characters who should logically react;
- dialogue that no longer matches exact route state.

This extends the Academy Origin Writing-to-Coding one-pass benchmark.

---

# Final lock

> **Stephen does not need to author every line. Writing is responsible for turning approved scene intent into believable interaction between autonomous characters. In group scenes, characters should normally converse, react and affect one another; narration supports that interaction instead of replacing it with observation summaries.**
