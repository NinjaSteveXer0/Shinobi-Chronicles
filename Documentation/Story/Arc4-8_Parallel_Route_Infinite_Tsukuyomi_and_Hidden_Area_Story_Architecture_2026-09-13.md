# 2. Infinite Tsukuyomi World — final gameplay purpose

World Map authority already reserves:

`world:S08` — **Infinite Tsukuyomi World**

with **no physical terrain anchor** because it is a non-physical realm.

## 2.1 Binding purpose

> **Infinite Tsukuyomi World is a high-end / endgame challenge realm for testing PL, Skills and Combat capability against extremely strong enemy encounters, with rare-card reward opportunities.**

It is **not** a generic CE dream simulator and must not dynamically invent personalised perfect lives from the player's Chronicle as its primary gameplay function.

The realm should support repeated challenge play after its legitimate unlock/intro has occurred.

## 2.2 Short Story introduction required

Infinite Tsukuyomi still needs a short authored Story introduction so the player understands:

- what this realm is in SC;
- why it remains accessible after the relevant endgame history;
- how the player enters it;
- why dangerous enemy manifestations/challenges can exist inside it;
- why leaving/returning is possible under SC's authored mechanism;
- what the challenge represents in-world.

The intro should be compact. It is not another Main Story Arc.

Exact unlock timing should normally be late Arc 8 / post-Arc-8 territory unless later Story authority establishes a causally valid earlier preview.

## 2.3 System ownership

Writing owns:

- the short introduction;
- the realm's narrative premise;
- Story/world-history conditions required before it can legitimately exist as challenge content.

Combat / Skills / Items / Weapons owns:

- exact enemy encounters;
- encounter legality;
- Battle mechanics;
- Skill interactions;
- difficulty/challenge packages.

PL / Registry / Rank owns:

- exact PL semantics / opponent Base PL where needed;
- no hidden direct PL bonuses merely from entering the realm.

Acquisition / Character Systems owns:

- rare-card obtainability;
- exact reward pools / ownership commits;
- duplicate/provenance/acquisition semantics.

Progression owns any persistent development rewards where separately authorised.

Story must not invent exact PL thresholds or rare-card drop tables.

## 2.4 Canon anchor / deliberate SC use

Official Naruto canon establishes Infinite Tsukuyomi as Madara's world-scale genjutsu/dream plan. SC deliberately repurposes its post-endgame accessible form into a challenge realm after the campaign resolves its main Madara conflict.

This is a deliberate gameplay divergence, not a claim that canon Infinite Tsukuyomi naturally functions as a repeatable arena.

# 4. ROGUE PATH — five-Arc forward spine

Core fantasy:

> **You rejected institutional ownership. Now prove you can live, protect people, build relationships and survive without quietly rebuilding another village around yourself.**

Rogue is a full trajectory, not a temporary exile chapter.

## Arc 4 — OUTSIDE THE WALLS

Founder return destabilises historical legitimacy while the Rogue protagonist experiences the crisis from outside normal Konoha command.

Primary pressures:

- old loyalties attempt to reclaim / recruit / control the protagonist;
- Hashirama/Tobirama's return creates reasons to investigate history without trusting Leaf custody;
- foreign or independent contacts become more important than formal assignment;
- the player must distinguish independence from isolation.

Arc question:

> **If you owe no village obedience, what obligations do you still choose to keep?**

## Arc 5 — NO FLAGS

The Rogue route becomes genuinely international.

Wind, Earth, Lightning and Water must all matter as sovereign systems the Rogue cannot simply ignore.

The player must build or maintain:

- safe movement;
- money/resources;
- intelligence;
- medical support;
- foreign contacts;
- legal/illegal access;
- trusted people who are not automatically subordinates.

Arc question:

> **Can independence survive borders, laws and people who have legitimate reasons not to trust you?**

## Arc 6 — NO CHAINS

The Jinchūriki/Tailed-Beast crisis creates the Rogue route's strongest autonomy test.

The protagonist may become attractive to hosts/beasts seeking refuge or independence, but Rogue does not automatically equal liberation.

Possible pressures:

- harbouring a host;
- refusing extradition;
- refusing a host who would bring catastrophic risk;
- opposing forced sealing;
- recognising a village's legitimate civilian-security concerns without accepting ownership language;
- preventing the protagonist's refuge/network from becoming coercive itself.

Arc question:

> **What does freedom mean when another person's freedom can endanger thousands?**

## Arc 7 — NO MASTER

Madara recognises the Rogue trajectory as ideologically relevant.

He may see the protagonist as somebody who already understands that institutions repeatedly recreate coercion.

The route should support legitimate temporary cooperation / negotiation with Madara where actual history allows, without forcing alliance.

Arc question:

> **Is Madara wrong because his diagnosis is false, or because his answer destroys the very freedom you fought for?**

## Arc 8 — THE FREE WORLD

The Rogue endgame tests whether a world of meaningful freedom can survive catastrophe without submitting to total control.

Potential earned endgame histories may include:

- preserving independent networks;
- refusing both Madara and state ownership;
- cooperating temporarily with governments without rejoining them;
- defending people whose politics the protagonist dislikes;
- dismantling structures the protagonist themselves created if those structures became coercive.

Arc question:

> **If nobody owns you, what do you owe everyone else?**

# 5. LEADER / TAKE-THE-THRONE PATH — five-Arc forward spine

Current route authority defines Take the Throne as concluding current leadership/system is incapable and pursuing replacement/seizure of governing authority.

Stephen's "Leader Path" is treated as the mature forward realization of this family.

Core fantasy:

> **You said you could do better. Now govern.**

Leader does not require the title Hokage. Legitimate realizations may include recognised government, challenger authority, coalition leadership, territorial rule, command of a new institution, or another Chronicle-earned governing structure.

## Arc 4 — THE RIGHT TO RULE

Hashirama and Tobirama's living return creates an immediate legitimacy test.

Questions include:

- does founder status outrank current government?;
- does historical prestige undermine the protagonist's claim?;
- can the leader use founder support without becoming their puppet?;
- what does lawful succession mean after resurrection and civil war?;

Arc question:

> **Why should anybody accept your authority when the founders themselves have returned?**

## Arc 5 — THE WEIGHT OF BORDERS

Leadership becomes international rather than local.

The protagonist must deal with Wind, Earth, Lightning and Water as independent powers with their own interests.

Pressure families:

- treaties;
- sanctions / trade / resources;
- military deterrence;
- appointments / envoys;
- border incidents;
- intelligence sharing;
- responsibility for actions committed by people acting under the protagonist's authority.

Arc question:

> **Can you lead without treating every foreign disagreement as disobedience?**

## Arc 6 — THE WEAPON YOU COMMAND

The Jinchūriki/Tailed-Beast crisis becomes the Leader route's defining hypocrisy test.

A ruler who previously argued that villages do not own hosts may now face advisers saying that national survival requires host deployment/control.

The route must allow hard choices around:

- consent;
- emergency authority;
- strategic deterrence;
- sealing;
- refuge/defection;
- military orders;
- civilian risk.

Arc question:

> **What happens when respecting one person's autonomy makes your people less safe?**

## Arc 7 — A WORLD TO LEAD

Madara's long game threatens the state/order the protagonist has built or seized.

Leader gameplay/story should now consume years of appointments, treaties, enemies, foreign trust and institutional decisions.

Madara becomes a competing ruler/philosopher rather than only a monster.

Arc question:

> **Can the order you built survive without you personally controlling every outcome?**

## Arc 8 — THE LAST AUTHORITY

Madara offers the ultimate authoritarian solution: peace without meaningful disobedience.

The Leader route must prove why its own authority is meaningfully different.

Potential pressures:

- subordinates refusing orders;
- allies demanding concessions;
- civilians rejecting the protagonist's preferred settlement;
- succession;
- limits on emergency powers;
- whether the protagonist can surrender authority after victory.

Arc question:

> **If your peace requires nobody being allowed to say no, how are you different from Madara?**

# 6. NON-CONFORMANCE PATH — five-Arc adaptive spine

Non-Conformance remains special.

It is **not** a universal fifth campaign script.

Current authority requires it to emerge from a Chronicle that has built a structure/ideology/relationship network that does not cleanly fit Reform, Dominion, Rogue or Leader.

Stephen nevertheless requires full Arc 4–8 coverage.

Therefore:

> **Non-Conformance receives a five-Arc adaptive Story spine, not a fixed universal plot.**

Each Arc supplies a required thematic problem and CE resolves the exact causal realization from the Chronicle's unusual existing structure.

## Arc 4 — THE SHAPE THAT DOESN'T FIT

Founder return collides with whatever unusual order/compact/relationship architecture the player has already created.

Required question:

> **Can Konoha's historical categories even describe what you have become?**

The Arc should force other actors to attempt to classify, absorb, regulate, ally with, or destroy the unusual arrangement.

## Arc 5 — A PLACE BETWEEN POWERS

The unusual Chronicle structure is tested internationally.

Wind, Earth, Lightning and Water must respond independently.

One may recognise it.
One may criminalise it.
One may exploit it.
One may attempt to copy it.

Required question:

> **Does your strange arrangement still work when friendly local history is replaced by foreign sovereignty?**

## Arc 6 — PERSON / WEAPON / MEMBER / GUEST

The host/beast crisis forces the Non-Conformance structure to define categories it may previously have avoided.

A compact or network may need to answer whether a Jinchūriki is:

- citizen;
- guest;
- member;
- military asset;
- protected person;
- sovereign individual;
- something else.

Required question:

> **Can your system protect autonomy without becoming incoherent when real danger arrives?**

## Arc 7 — THE THING MADARA DIDN'T EXPECT

Madara and Black-Zetsu-linked actors encounter a political/social structure that does not fit the old village-vs-rogue-vs-ruler assumptions.

Madara may attempt to interpret it as proof of his philosophy, as an unstable anomaly, or as a useful intermediate state depending on actual history.

Required question:

> **Is this genuinely a new answer, or just an old power structure wearing unfamiliar clothes?**

## Arc 8 — PROVE IT WORKS

The Non-Conformance endgame is not about selecting a philosophical label.

The actual accumulated Chronicle becomes the evidence.

The final system should ask:

- can people leave?;
- can they disagree?;
- who holds force?;
- what happens to outsiders?;
- who controls resources?;
- who can say no?;
- does the structure survive without the protagonist personally enforcing it?;
- did it remain coherent under war, foreign pressure and the Tailed-Beast crisis?

Required question:

> **Did you actually build something different, or only refuse to name what you built?**

Non-Conformance eligibility and exact scenes remain Chronicle-relative.

# 7. Hidden-area Story architecture

World Map v2 currently reserves seven physical secret areas:

1. `world:S01` — **Ryūchi Cave**;
2. `world:S02` — **Mount Myōboku**;
3. `world:S03` — **Shikkotsu Forest**;
4. `world:S04` — **Akatsuki Hideout**;
5. `world:S05` — **Jinchūriki Temple**;
6. `world:S06` — **Valley of the End**;
7. `world:S07` — **Tomb of the Sage of Six Paths**.

`world:S08` Infinite Tsukuyomi World is handled separately above as a non-physical challenge realm.

## 7.1 Binding hidden-area principle

> **Each physical high-profile hidden area should receive an authored short Story introduction rather than appearing as a contextless map unlock.**

The introduction must establish enough causal provenance for:

- why the player discovers/reveals the identity;
- why this Chronicle can approach/access it;
- who/what controls entry where relevant;
- what problem, trial, history or relationship makes the place meaningful;
- why the location remains relevant after first discovery.

## 7.2 Preferred two-part mini-arc model

Stephen suggested a two-Arc story for hidden areas.

Current preferred implementation is **two short side-story movements**, not two additional full 12-Mission Main Story Arcs per secret.

### Mini-Arc A — DISCOVERY / INTRODUCTION

Typical functions:

- turn `????` into discovered identity;
- establish historical/contextual reason for discovery;
- establish local guardian/contact/threat where appropriate;
- prove the first access requirement;
- reveal what kind of opportunity the area represents without granting mastery automatically.

### Mini-Arc B — DEEP ACCESS / TRIAL / CONSEQUENCE

Typical functions:

- deeper trial or conflict;
- relationship/guardian approval or opposition;
- capability test;
- choice that changes how the player relates to the place;
- unlock a standing World/Progression/Summon/Combat/Knowledge opportunity where the owning system authorises it;
- preserve lasting consequences.

Exact length may be shorter/longer where content warrants it. The two-part pattern is preferred architecture, not a requirement to manufacture filler.

## 7.3 Area-specific campaign roles — current direction

### Ryūchi Cave

Short Story should introduce dangerous serpent/Sage/summoning/trial identity anchored in canon research.

It may become a standing development/Summon/Knowledge opportunity where Progression/Combat/Acquisition authorise it.

Discovery alone grants none of those automatically.

### Mount Myōboku

Short Story should introduce the toad sanctuary and Sage-training tradition.

Official canon strongly anchors Mount Myōboku as Naruto/Jiraiya's Sage Jutsu training location.

Access/training must still be Chronicle-earned.

### Shikkotsu Forest

Short Story should introduce the third major sanctuary carefully.

Because canon gives substantially less explicit material than Myōboku/Ryūchi, detailed SC content must research first and deliberately author divergence rather than filling blanks from fan assumptions.

### Akatsuki Hideout

Short Story may use organisation history, old infrastructure, remnants, secrets, enemies or surviving networks.

Official canon anchors Akatsuki hideouts as defended operational locations; exact SC hideout history/status requires current continuity authoring.

### Jinchūriki Temple

This is SC-specific forward architecture and should receive deliberate Story/Codex definition.

It is a strong Arc-6-adjacent candidate for host/beast/seal/provenance history, but exact purpose is not yet closed by this document.

### Valley of the End

Short Story should connect the location's Hashirama/Madara history to SC's already-divergent Recorded History.

It is naturally useful during Arc 4 / Arc 7 / Arc 8 but should not automatically become mandatory every time.

### Tomb of the Sage of Six Paths

Short Story should introduce deep Hagoromo / Six-Paths history, provenance and endgame Knowledge.

It must not collapse into a generic legendary-loot dungeon.

Exact tomb existence/history is SC-authored and must remain explicit divergence where canon does not supply a literal tomb.
