## 1. Purpose

This authority consolidates the CE / Codex / Coordination expressive-writing continuity rule with Stephen's subsequently approved Story benchmark additions into **one active player-facing Writing acceptance law**.

It exists to prevent two different failure modes:

1. Story can preserve its facts, branch causality and implementation semantics while losing character voice, emotion, cadence, body language, atmosphere and lived performance.
2. A rewrite can improve prose while accidentally changing closed Chronicle history, Knowledge, resolver outcomes or approved Story facts.

Shinobi Chronicles must satisfy both semantic truth and lived performance.

Canonical principle:

> **The player should feel that people are living through the Chronicle, not that the Chronicle is being reported to them.**

Stephen's locked addition:

> **Facts decide what happened. Diction, Cadence, Personality and Emotion decide whether the player actually lived it with these people.**

## 3. The non-collapse rule

Preserve these distinctions:

> **semantic correctness != expressive completeness**

> **Story facts closed != prose quality frozen**

> **expression repair != Chronicle rewrite**

> **quality instruction != blanket replacement consent**

> **better prose != authority to change resolver outcomes**

> **stronger personality != new factual Knowledge**

> **visual support != prose replacement**

> **detail != bloat**

> **cinematic != overwritten**

A scene may be factually GREEN and still be Writing RED.

A scene may need an expression rewrite without needing a Story redesign.

---

## 4. Binding interactive-presence loop

The default Story loop remains:

`setup -> playable problem -> player action -> owning system/world/story resolution -> visible consequence -> changed next situation`

The player-facing acceptance test is:

> **I'm here. I saw this. I chose this. They reacted. Something changed because of me.**

Reject:

`setup -> choice -> flavour text -> same situation`

Player choice expresses protagonist intent, not guaranteed success. The correct owning resolver commits factual result. Story then performs the resulting state visibly and allows the next situation to inherit what changed.

Player agency requires meaningful decisions, not constant decisions.

Do not turn stronger prose into a wall of text or a button after every sentence.

## 5. Stephen's four mandatory quality dimensions

Every substantial player-facing Story unit must satisfy these four named dimensions.

### 5.1 Diction

The actual words must belong to the speaker:

- age / life stage;
- role and social posture;
- vocabulary;
- confidence;
- relationships;
- stress and emotional state;
- current Chronicle-specific context.

Do not flatten the cast into generic polished dialogue, generic dry sarcasm, generic anime exposition or the current chat's preferred prose voice.

### 5.2 Cadence

Sentence length, rhythm, interruption, hesitation, pauses, silence and timing must belong to the speaker and moment.

Natural spoken dialogue may use:

- contractions;
- fragments;
- false starts;
- unfinished thoughts;
- interruption;
- repetition under stress;
- sharp shortening or lengthening of sentences;
- silence instead of an answer.

Non-contracted or formal wording is valid when it is deliberate for that speaker or moment.

### 5.3 Personality

Named characters must remain recognisable without explanatory labels.

Personality continuity does not mean repeating catchphrases. It means the same person remains recognisable under different conditions while legitimately changing with age, stress, relationships, grief, fear, success, authority, embarrassment, injury and Recorded History.

If dialogue can be swapped between materially different named characters without changing the scene, the scene has failed this dimension.

### 5.4 Emotion

Emotion must normally be performed rather than merely named.

Use character-specific combinations of:

- facial reaction;
- eye-line;
- posture;
- hands;
- breath;
- physical distance;
- movement;
- hesitation;
- interruption;
- silence;
- laughter;
- scoffs;
- sighs;
- inappropriate laughter;
- refusal to answer;
- object/environment interaction;
- changed vocabulary or sentence rhythm.

Stillness may itself be performance.

The same emotion must not look identical on every character.

## 6. Core performed-scene target

Where appropriate, a player-facing scene should combine:

- environmental / temporal narration;
- character movement and body language;
- emotional reaction;
- internal thought or perception when POV legitimately supports it;
- character-specific dialogue;
- natural conversational cadence;
- relationship-sensitive response;
- silence / hesitation / interruption / refusal where appropriate;
- immediate aftermath after meaningful actions;
- connective prose between decisions;
- visible state change after resolution.

Not every beat requires every element.

The target is **performed scene prose**, not maximum word count.

---

## 7. Durable character voice anchors

Every recurring named character should have a stable Writing-owned voice/personality baseline that successor chats consume before substantial dialogue authoring.

Minimum useful anchor:

```text
CHARACTER / REPRESENTATION:
AGE / LIFE STAGE:
BASE TEMPERAMENT:
SOCIAL / AUTHORITY POSTURE:
DEFAULT CADENCE:
VOCABULARY / FORMALITY:
HUMOUR STYLE:
SWEARING / INTENSIFIERS:
CONFIDENCE / HESITATION PATTERN:
EMOTIONAL LEAKAGE:
BODY-LANGUAGE TENDENCIES:
SILENCE / WITHHOLDING STYLE:
WHEN ANGRY:
WHEN AFRAID:
WHEN EMBARRASSED:
WHEN EXCITED:
WHEN HURT / GRIEVING:
WITH TRUSTED PEOPLE:
WITH RIVALS:
WITH AUTHORITY:
DO NOT WRITE THIS CHARACTER AS:
CURRENT CHRONICLE-SPECIFIC MODIFIERS:
```

This is an authoring anchor, not a deterministic personality algorithm.

## 8. Character separation and voice contamination

Where practical, a reader should be able to infer who is speaking from cadence, vocabulary, confidence, rhythm, humour, restraint and emotional behaviour even before seeing the speaker label.

Do not:

- spread one successful character's cadence across the cast;
- make every smart character academic;
- make every funny character quippy;
- make every emotional character explain their emotion;
- make every professional character an exposition terminal;
- use Kakashi's sparse cadence as a generic house style merely because his benchmark scene works.

The benchmark means every character receives equivalent specificity — **not equivalent speech patterns**.

---

## 9. Intelligence, Knowledge and subtext

Preserve:

> **World Truth != observer Knowledge != presentation**

Do not make a clever character explain why they are clever when the player can watch them notice, compare, wait, correct, infer or act.

Do not use dialogue or internal thought to verbalise CE contracts merely to prove semantic compliance.

Do not use expressive prose to leak hidden World Truth.

Silence, mistaken inference, uncertainty and incomplete statements are legitimate when Knowledge-safe and character-appropriate.

For the approved Kakashi benchmark, preserve this principle:

> **Never explain Kakashi's intelligence when the player can watch him use it.**

Internal thought remains observer-relative experience, not omniscient authority.

---

## 10. Relationship-sensitive expression

Characters must not speak to everyone the same way.

Expression should legitimately change with:

- familiarity;
- trust;
- rivalry;
- resentment;
- admiration;
- authority difference;
- fear;
- debt;
- embarrassment;
- shared history;
- recent conflict;
- affection / attraction where authored;
- institutional role.

The semantic meaning may remain the same while the actual line, posture and delivery change substantially.

---

## 11. Scene reaction completeness and NPC autonomy

Meaningful actions should create visible human/world reaction where relevant observers perceive them.

Writing should consider:

- who saw it;
- who heard it;
- who understood it;
- who misunderstood it;
- who reacts openly;
- who hides a reaction;
- who speaks;
- who remains silent;
- who changes physical position;
- whether the emotional temperature changes;
- whether trust, hostility, access, evidence, damage, discovery, custody or environment visibly changes.

Do not force every participant to comment on everything.

Do not let populated scenes behave like the protagonist is surrounded by inert UI furniture.

NPCs remain autonomous people with their own Knowledge, obligations, relationships and personalities. Protagonist intent is not a party command.

---

## 12. Narration, time, atmosphere and transition

Narration must do more than report semantic state.

It may carry:

- weather;
- light;
- time of day;
- ambient village movement;
- crowd pressure;
- silence;
- distance;
- tension;
- changes in pace;
- travel transition;
- aftermath;
- attention;
- perception;
- temporal and emotional movement.

The Scene Board may visually show place, but prose still owns rhythm, attention, implication, emotional timing and what changes between frames.

Do not redundantly narrate every visible object.

> **Do not narrate the entire picture. Do not expect the picture to perform the entire scene.**

---

## 13. Screen-performance units

Player-facing Story should be authored as screen-performance units rather than prose pages or design summaries.

Preserve:

- concise physically specific narration/action;
- actual spoken dialogue as speaker-owned dialogue;
- separate speakers as separate performed turns where presentation benefits;
- meaningful choices at genuine decision points;
- immediate visible/reactive consequence after meaningful choices;
- lightweight ordinary dialogue advance where appropriate.

Do not bundle multiple speakers, multiple actions and multiple reactions into one anonymous narration block merely because the paragraph reads well.

Do not solve the problem by fragmenting the same prose page into many forced click-through pages.

`CONTINUE` may support short cinematic transitions; it must not become the dominant substitute for meaningful play.

---

## 14. Humour and dramatic contrast

Humour is welcome when it belongs to the people and moment.

Do not make the whole cast joke in one register.

Do not flatten serious consequences with mandatory quips.

Do not ban comedy merely because a scene matters.

The Story may legitimately move from tension to absurdity, confidence to embarrassment, affection to danger or gravity to dry reaction when the transition is character-earned.

---

## 15. Unified acceptance tests

Before a substantial Story unit is treated as player-facing Writing-complete, run all of these tests:

1. **Diction test:** do the words belong to the actual speaker?
2. **Cadence test:** do rhythm, pauses, sentence length and interruption belong to speaker + moment?
3. **Personality / speaker-swap test:** if names are swapped, do the lines still work unchanged? If yes, differentiation is weak.
4. **Emotion test:** can the reader feel the emotional state without relying only on emotion labels?
5. **Physicality test:** do important reactions exist in bodies, movement, stillness or silence?
6. **Narration test:** does narration carry time, atmosphere, movement, attention or perception rather than merely restating semantic state?
7. **Relationship test:** does expression reflect who these people are to each other?
8. **Knowledge test:** does any flourish reveal information the speaker/POV cannot know?
9. **Voice-contamination test:** has another character inherited a successful character's cadence because it was convenient?
10. **Reaction test:** do consequential actions receive plausible participant/world reaction where perceived?
11. **Autonomy test:** do NPCs act as themselves rather than as protagonist extensions?
12. **Scene-memory test:** can the player remember a lived exchange/reaction/moment rather than only the branch result?
13. **Presence test:** can the player say, `I'm here. I saw this. I chose this. They reacted. Something changed because of me.`?
14. **Changed-situation test:** does the next beat inherit actual consequence rather than resetting to the same functional state?
15. **Screen-first test:** are prose, speaker ownership and Scene Board complementing one another rather than becoming an on-screen book?
16. **Semantic-safety test:** did the expression pass preserve closed facts and owning-system authority?
17. **Successor-continuity test:** does this still sound like the same game even if another Writing workspace authored it?

If factual correctness is GREEN but several of these are RED, **Writing remains RED**.

## 16. Successor-chat boot requirement

A new Writing chat must not rely on its own default literary style.

Before substantial player-facing authoring, it must consume:

1. current Konoha Writing successor/deep-sweep authority;
2. this consolidated standard;
3. current character-specific voice/personality anchors;
4. current canon-first authority where relevant;
5. exact current Story / branch authority;
6. relevant Recorded History and observer Knowledge;
7. any current approved benchmark/exemplar relevant to the scene.

If a recurring character lacks a sufficiently clear voice anchor, create/confirm one before producing a large volume of dialogue rather than allowing the current chat's generic tendencies to define them accidentally.

Canonical rule:

> **Chat succession may change the tool doing the writing. It must not change who the characters are or how Shinobi Chronicles sounds.**

- Kakashi's intelligence is performed through observation, correction, precision, stillness and clipped response rather than explained;
- Pakkun has an autonomous older/dry cadence rather than functioning as a joke dispenser;

- character separation remains strong without forcing every scene to use Kakashi's exact sparse rhythm.

The lesson is not `write everyone like Kakashi`.

The lesson is:

> **Every character deserves the same degree of specific voice, physical behaviour, subtext and emotional truth that the approved benchmark gives the people in that scene.**

## 18. Retroactive application

Stephen has explicitly locked this benchmark for future Story and warned that prior scenes, stories and arcs may require rewriting against it.

Therefore:

- old semantic closure does not prove expression closure;
- old `Writing complete` labels do not by themselves prove this benchmark is met;
- existing Origins, Arc 1 and later Story may be re-audited for Diction / Cadence / Personality / Emotion and the broader performance tests above;
- weak content may receive a controlled expression/performance retrofit;
- do not restart closed architecture merely because prose is being repaired;
- do not fabricate new systems as part of a prose repair;
- do not claim a retrofit is complete until the actual affected material has been reviewed/re-authored and, where runtime acceptance is required, actually projected/validated.

Canonical boundary:

> **Same committed history where history is closed; better lived realization where expression is weak.**

## 19. Rewrite / approval containment

This standard strengthens **how approved Story is performed**. It does not grant autonomous permission to replace major approved Story units.

For substantial rewrite work, preserve the current approval flow:

`CURRENT AUTHORITY -> PROPOSED EXPRESSION DIFF -> invariant check -> STEPHEN APPROVE / EDIT / REJECT -> final authority`

Where original detailed dialogue survives, recover it before inventing replacement dialogue and calling it recovered.

Where exact original wording is missing, preserve the semantic/story spine and stop at the approval gate for substantive new expression unless Stephen has explicitly authorised autonomous replacement for that unit.

This requirement coexists with the retroactive quality audit:

> **needs rewrite != permission to silently rewrite**

> **benchmark failure != semantic reopening**
