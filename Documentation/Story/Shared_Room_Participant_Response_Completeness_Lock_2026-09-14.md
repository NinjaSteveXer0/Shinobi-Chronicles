# Shared-Room Participant Response Completeness Lock

**Date:** 2026-09-14  
**Owner:** Writing / Story — Konoha  
**Status:** **BINDING STORYWIDE AUTHORING REQUIREMENT**

## Core rule

> **If Story deliberately gathers multiple relevant characters into the same physical scene to review, react to, reveal, debrief, celebrate, condemn, argue about, or otherwise process what happened, every speaking participant who is actually present must get to say their piece before the scene closes.**

A group consequence scene must not reduce most of the room to silent card art while one or two characters carry the entire response.

## Scope

This applies across:

- all Academy Origins;
- Arc 1–3 presentation/reconciliation work;
- future Arcs and route families;
- Hokage / command-room reviews;
- ANBU / Police / authority debriefs;
- team or faction consequence scenes where the relevant people are physically together;
- post-Mission, post-Battle, post-event and Chronicle-consequence group scenes;
- later Canon, Alt, Rogue, Non-Conformance, Leader and other Story routes.

## Presence boundary

This rule does **not** mean the whole game roster, all teammates, or every character connected to the event must be dragged into the scene.

Only characters who are factually and legitimately **present in that physical scene** are covered.

Do not:

- teleport teammates from another location into the room;
- summon unrelated NPCs just so they can comment;
- ignore custody, injury, death, deployment, assignment or location state;
- overwrite CE/world truth to force attendance.

Attendance remains consequence/state-driven.

## Participation requirement

Once a speaking character is intentionally present in a group reaction/debrief/reveal scene, Writing must give that character a meaningful player-facing contribution before the scene resolves.

Normally this means at least one line of dialogue, supported by character-specific expression, timing, body language and reaction where appropriate.

A contribution should reflect that character's own:

- Knowledge;
- relationship to the event;
- relationship to the other people in the room;
- direct experience;
- temperament and voice;
- injuries, losses, victories, humiliation, fear, anger, amusement, relief or confusion;
- Chronicle history created by the player's actions.

Do not give every participant the same generic reaction.

## Cross-participant reaction is part of the payoff

A shared-room review is not only a sequence of people individually reporting to the authority figure. The people in the room should also react **to each other** where their relationships and the committed history make that natural.

This may include:

- teasing;
- laughter;
- disbelief;
- embarrassment;
- annoyance;
- defending another participant;
- challenging another participant's version of events;
- recalling what happened to them personally;
- one participant enjoying another's humiliation;
- another participant pushing back;
- changing tone when a more serious fact is revealed.

These exchanges should make the room feel like a group of real people who just lived through the same event, not witnesses taking turns at a podium.

For example, in the Kakashi Origin evaluation, if Kakashi quickly defeats the Masked Interceptor, later catches the Package Smuggler, and sends the Smuggler to the Uchiha Police Force, surviving participants who are legitimately present in Minato's office may find the outcome funny. The ANBU Marked Target may tease the Masked Interceptor for being beaten quickly; the Masked Interceptor may fire back; both may laugh at the absurdity of the Smuggler ending up with the actual police. Their reactions should arise from their own experience and relationships rather than generic comedy writing.

This does **not** override the lethal-scene emotional-weight lock. If the room is processing a death, attempted murder, severe injury, or another genuinely grave fact, humour must not casually undercut the seriousness unless that specific character response is itself deliberately and credibly authored.

## Silence is not the default substitute

A glance, smirk, frown or silent reaction can strengthen a participant's contribution, but it should not routinely replace their chance to speak in a deliberately assembled group-response scene.

If a capable speaking participant remains silent, that silence must itself be an intentional authored beat with a clear dramatic reason, not an omission caused by Writing focusing only on the main speaker.

## Factual-history sensitivity

The room composition and each participant's response must change with committed history.

Examples:

- a dead participant is absent;
- someone held by the Uchiha Police Force is not silently present in the Hokage Office unless a legitimate transfer occurred;
- a survivor of an attempted kill may speak very differently from someone merely defeated;
- someone Kakashi released may react differently from someone he delivered to ANBU;
- a participant who never witnessed an event cannot speak as though they did unless they legitimately learned it later.

World Truth != observer Knowledge != presentation remains binding.

## Kakashi Origin benchmark

The post-evaluation Hokage Office scene is the immediate benchmark.

Whichever evaluation participants are factually present in Minato's office must each receive their own meaningful response to Kakashi's recorded actions before the scene closes.

They should also be allowed to react to **one another's** outcomes where appropriate. The room should feel socially alive: amusement, teasing, embarrassment, irritation, concern or disbelief can move between participants based on what happened to each of them.

Kakashi himself is not automatically included: if he is elsewhere, he does not appear simply because the player is seeing the review scene.

The player may therefore witness reactions and assessments that Kakashi does not know about.

## Anti-pattern

Reject:

`Minato speaks -> ANBU answers -> everyone else stands silently -> black wipe`

when several relevant surviving participants are deliberately in the room.

Also reject:

`Participant A reports -> Participant B reports -> Participant C reports`

if nobody reacts to anyone else despite obvious shared history, embarrassment, humour, anger or tension.

Prefer:

`Minato response -> participant A says their piece -> participant B reacts to A -> participant C contributes from their own history -> participants respond to each other where natural -> scene resolves`

without turning the scene into repetitive exposition.

## Relationship to expressive-performance authority

This lock extends:

`Documentation/Story/Character_Reaction_and_Expressive_Performance_Authoring_Lock_2026-09-14.md`

Characters should not just speak; they should react. In shared-room consequence scenes, characters should also not merely **stand there** while somebody else reacts for them.

## Final lock

> **Same room, relevant participant, capable of speaking: give them their piece.**
>
> **Let them react to one another as well as to the authority figure.**
>
> **Do not pull in people who are elsewhere. Do not fabricate attendance. But once Story deliberately puts a participant in the room, do not reduce them to silent scenery.**
