# 2. Whole-campaign movement

The approved macro progression is:

> **Arcs 1–3: Konoha breaks.**  
> **Arc 4: its history breaks.**  
> **Arc 5: the world order breaks.**  
> **Arc 6: the weapon/person boundary breaks.**  
> **Arc 7: the old enemy returns.**  
> **Arc 8: decide what the shinobi world becomes.**

Escalation must be primarily **conceptual, political, historical and relational**, not merely `higher PL enemy every Arc`.

The Main Story should increasingly test:

- legitimacy;
- history;
- freedom;
- authority;
- personhood;
- coercion;
- institutional survival;
- whether peace without meaningful agency is still peace.

# 3. ARC 4 — THE FOUNDERS RETURN

## 3.1 Core premise

Arc 3 ends with Konoha having survived genuine civil war without restoring full political unity.

Arc 4 introduces a historical shock greater than either faction expected:

> **Hashirama Senju and Tobirama Senju are genuinely alive.**

Their historical deaths remain real.

Their later restoration to genuine living state remains closed CE/Codex canon.

Their return must be treated as a **political and historical earthquake**, not fan-service and not an automatic solution to the civil-war fracture.

## 3.2 Binding founder-return rule

> **Hashirama and Tobirama return as people, not as automatic rulers.**

Therefore:

- Minato does **not** automatically stop being Fourth Hokage because Hashirama returns;
- Sasuke does **not** automatically abandon his challenge because a founder returns;
- Hashirama's historical title does not automatically recreate present appointment authority;
- Tobirama's historical Second-Hokage office does not automatically override later succession;
- Hiruzen's historical appointment as Third Hokage remains legitimate;
- resurrection does not erase historical succession;
- public discovery that the founders are alive creates new political questions rather than resolving old ones.

## 3.3 Arc-4 campaign question

> **What happens when Konoha's mythology walks back through the front gate?**

The Arc should force Konoha to confront questions such as:

- What else in official history is incomplete, false, concealed or misunderstood?
- Who legitimately knew the founders survived/restored?
- Why did Hashirama remain hidden while later Konoha crises occurred?
- Why did Tobirama remain outside public office?
- Does secret survival undermine institutional legitimacy?
- Does founder status carry moral authority, legal authority, both, or neither?
- What actually happened between Hashirama and Madara?
- Which parts of Konoha's founding mythology are history versus political inheritance?

## 3.4 Founder character boundary

Hashirama and Tobirama must not become one political bloc merely because they are brothers/founders.

Hashirama may understand anti-system grievances without automatically endorsing Sasuke.

Tobirama may understand Minato's institutional-security problem without automatically endorsing every Minato policy.

Both remain autonomous people with their own Knowledge, history, values and disagreements.

Do not reduce:

- Hashirama -> sentimental peace mascot;
- Tobirama -> cartoon authoritarian;
- Minato -> obsolete placeholder;
- Sasuke -> automatic founder opposition.

## 3.5 Arc-4 ending direction

By Arc close:

- founder survival/restoration is no longer safely containable as private myth for the relevant Main Story political field;
- Konoha's public/institutional understanding of its own history has materially changed;
- the founder return has intensified, reframed or complicated legitimacy questions rather than erasing them;
- evidence/history begins creating the more dangerous conclusion that **Madara's historical story may also be incomplete**.

Madara need not personally appear yet.

# 4. ARC 5 — THE WORLD THEY BUILT

## 4.1 Core premise

Arc 4 destabilises Konoha's understanding of its own foundation.

Arc 5 expands that crisis outward:

> **Does the Hidden Village system still deserve to exist in its current form?**

The Arc shifts from Konoha-internal legitimacy to the wider shinobi political order.

## 4.2 International pressure

Other villages, states and non-state actors react to combinations of:

- Konoha's civil war;
- Minato's continued lawful government;
- Sasuke's recognised challenger power where that history exists;
- founder return;
- disclosure of hidden historical truths;
- Konoha's concentration of exceptional shinobi/Jinchūriki/founder-level actors;
- existing Chronicle-specific Kusa/Ame or other foreign relationships.

Their reactions must remain plural and self-interested.

Do not write the rest of the world as one coalition reacting identically to Konoha.

## 4.3 Arc-5 campaign question

> **Does the system the founders created still solve the problem it was built to solve?**

Possible legitimate political responses include:

- reforming inter-village institutions;
- strengthening security blocs;
- strategic deterrence;
- opportunistic territorial/economic pressure;
- alliance-building;
- non-state networks;
- rejection of Great-Village authority;
- protagonist-specific independent arrangements earned from prior history.

## 4.4 Route-family scaling

Arc 5 is where route families may become **geopolitical in consequence** without becoming explicit morality/faction meters.

Examples of trajectory expression, only where earned:

- Reform -> rebuild institutions and negotiated legitimacy;
- Dominion -> enforce durable order/security structures;
- Rogue -> build or rely on non-state relationships and autonomy;
- Take the Throne -> seek authority beyond local Konoha politics;
- Non-Conformance -> produce Chronicle-specific arrangements that do not map cleanly to established categories.

Do not expose these as universal buttons merely because the route families exist.

## 4.5 Arc-5 ending direction

The old shinobi equilibrium fails to restore.

By Arc close:

- the post-war world recognises that a broader order transition is underway;
- multiple powers prepare for what comes next;
- no simple return to pre-Arc-1 normal remains credible;
- foreign actors have their own durable histories with the protagonist/current Chronicle where earned.

# 5. ARC 6 — THE BEASTS IN CHAINS

## 5.1 Core premise

Arc 6 places the Tailed Beast / Jinchūriki question at the center of Main Story.

Universal campaign question:

> **What is a Tailed Beast in the political order?**

The Arc must explicitly confront the collision between:

- personhood;
- military deterrence;
- host autonomy;
- village security;
- seal control;
- coercion;
- cooperation;
- historical distribution of Tailed Beast power.

## 5.2 Non-universal relationship rule

Do not impose one Naruto/Kurama-style relationship template on all hosts/beasts.

Possible legitimate relationships may include:

- hatred;
- fear;
- coercive control;
- mutual dependence;
- negotiation;
- strategic cooperation;
- partial trust;
- genuine friendship;
- total refusal;
- asymmetric exploitation.

Preserve:

- host != beast;
- host identity != automatic cooperation;
- access != consent;
- control != cooperation;
- knowledge != relationship;
- chakra use != ownership;
- Jinchūriki status != government ownership.

## 5.3 Political pressure

Arc 6 should force institutions and protagonists to confront questions such as:

- Are Jinchūriki citizens, strategic weapons, both, or neither?
- Can a village deploy a Jinchūriki against their will?
- Does a Tailed Beast have meaningful political/personhood standing?
- Who owns seal decisions?
- Can coercive Fūinjutsu be legitimate under existential pressure?
- What obligations exist when a host and beast disagree?
- What happens when one village's security depends on another person's imprisonment?

## 5.4 Menma/Naruto boundary

Menma/Naruto/Kurama history may become explosively relevant in the developer Chronicle, but their specific relationship is **not** the universal Arc premise.

Their history should function as one Chronicle realization of the larger personhood/power question.

No automatic new tail state, permanent access or PL increase is created merely because Arc 6 concerns Tailed Beasts.

## 5.5 Founder accountability

Hashirama/Tobirama and other historical actors may be confronted with the consequences of earlier beast-distribution/security decisions.

Historical context does not create automatic guilt or absolution.

## 5.6 Arc-6 ending direction

By Arc close:

- the world has had to confront Tailed Beasts as more than abstract weapons;
- old security arrangements are destabilised;
- actors again begin attempting to collect, control, align or manipulate Tailed Beast power;
- the campaign is positioned for Madara's active return without requiring a carbon copy of canon Fourth-War mechanics.

# 6. ARC 7 — THE LONG SHADOW

## 6.1 Core premise

Arc 7 transitions Madara from historical absence to active Main Story threat.

The Arc should initially reveal **the long game before the man**.

Investigative/story material may include, where legitimately authored:

- Black Zetsu;
- ancient manipulation;
- historical omissions;
- surviving networks;
- actors knowingly serving Madara;
- actors unknowingly advancing overlapping plans;
- post-Valley Madara history;
- ancient plans interacting with modern political fractures.

## 6.2 Responsibility boundary

Madara must remain a responsible ideological actor.

Do not reduce Madara to:

- a puppet with no agency because Black Zetsu manipulated history;
- generic apocalypse villain;
- simple power addict;
- historical cameo waiting for another final boss to replace him.

Black Zetsu may possess its own agenda and may manipulate/withhold/betray.

However:

> **Black Zetsu's agenda does not erase Madara's responsibility for Madara's choices.**

## 6.3 Arc-7 campaign question

> **Can the shinobi world actually fix itself?**

Madara's ideological position must become increasingly understandable even where the player rejects it:

- institutions fail;
- peace collapses;
- generations repeat conflict;
- people abuse freedom;
- power recreates hierarchy;
- coercion repeatedly returns under new names.

Madara's conclusion is that ordinary reality cannot reliably produce lasting peace.

## 6.4 Madara reveal

Late Arc 7:

> **Madara Uchiha returns to active Story as Madara Uchiha.**

Do not substitute:

- Tobi merely pretending to be Madara;
- Edo copy as the primary final Madara;
- historical illusion;
- symbolic successor using his name.

SC's already-closed Valley-of-the-End divergence means his exact route must be authored from SC causal history rather than blindly copying canon war chronology.

## 6.5 Arc-7 ending direction

The conflict scale changes.

The world's prior fights over **who should control the shinobi system** confront Madara's more fundamental claim:

> **the system and ordinary human freedom cannot produce lasting peace.**

Arc 8 begins with that argument no longer theoretical.

# 7. ARC 8 — THE LAST DREAM

## 7.1 Core premise

Arc 8 is the endgame of this campaign movement.

It must not resolve merely as:

`higher PL Madara -> final Battle -> credits`.

Madara is both:

- a major Combat/endgame threat;
- the ideological final antagonist of the campaign.

## 7.2 Arc-8 campaign question

> **Is freedom worth the chaos it creates, or is imposed peace preferable?**

Madara's answer is a peace so absolute that meaningful conflict becomes impossible because meaningful agency itself is constrained, overwritten or escaped through imposed dream/order.

The exact final mechanism may consume canon anchors but need not carbon-copy canonical Fourth-War execution.

The binding thematic invariant is:

> **Madara offers peace without meaningful freedom.**

## 7.3 Campaign thematic convergence

The eight-Arc campaign should retrospectively read as an escalating argument:

- Arc 1 -> who controls information?
- Arc 2 -> who controls truth / interpretation?
- Arc 3 -> who controls authority?
- Arc 4 -> who controls history?
- Arc 5 -> who controls political order?
- Arc 6 -> who controls power and people?
- Arc 7 -> can this world actually fix itself?
- Arc 8 -> is meaningful freedom worth the suffering it permits?

## 7.4 Route-family endgame expression

The five route families must pay off through actual Recorded History rather than final-dialogue alignment buttons.

Illustrative endgame logic, only where legitimately earned:

### Reform
The world is worth repairing even though it can never be perfect.

### Dominion
Madara is right that unmanaged chaos is catastrophic, but absolute surrender of agency is not acceptable; enforceable order must exist without becoming total ownership of people.

### Rogue
No institution, founder, godlike shinobi or imposed peace may own the protagonist's life.

### Take the Throne
Madara's diagnosis may contain truth, but Madara does not get exclusive authority to decide the answer; the protagonist seeks to impose/author a competing order where their Chronicle has actually earned that trajectory.

### Non-Conformance
A Chronicle-specific arrangement earned through prior history may reject the conventional binary altogether.

None of these are guaranteed endings merely because the route family exists.

## 7.5 Madara final-antagonist lock

Current approved campaign direction:

> **Madara remains the final antagonist of the Arc-8 campaign.**

Do **not** repeat the canonical late replacement where Black Zetsu removes Madara so Kaguya becomes the actual final climax.

Kaguya/Ōtsutsuki material may:

- exist historically;
- inform Black Zetsu's agenda;
- appear as lore, evidence, future/post-campaign architecture, or another later threat if separately authored.

But for this campaign:

> **Kaguya does not replace Madara as the Arc-8 final antagonist/climax.**

Black Zetsu may still betray, manipulate or pursue separate goals if later authoring supports it; such action must not erase the campaign's Madara payoff.

## 7.6 Hashirama/Madara final thematic return

Arc 8 should, where causally legitimate, return to the unresolved ideological fracture between Hashirama and Madara.

The player generation must not merely reenact the founders' argument.

The final campaign should create the possibility that the current Chronicle answers a question neither founder successfully solved.

Exact ending, deaths, rulership, settlement and post-game state remain later detailed Story authority.
