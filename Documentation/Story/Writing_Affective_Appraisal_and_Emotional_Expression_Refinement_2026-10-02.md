## Purpose

Shinobi Chronicles already has binding authority for character voice, physical reaction, emotional continuity, relationship-sensitive expression, bounded Knowledge and screen-first consequence.

The remaining Writing gap is the bridge between:

`something happened`

and

`this specific person expressed something believable`.

This refinement makes that bridge explicit without creating a universal Emotion stat, persistent mood store, appraisal score, morality layer, emotion taxonomy or emotion-to-action table.

Canonical Writing stack:

> **TRIGGER -> PERCEPTION -> APPRAISAL -> AFFECTIVE PRESSURE -> REGULATION / DEFENCE -> IMMEDIATE WANT -> EXPRESSION CHANNEL -> CONSEQUENCE**

For social scenes, also ask:

> **WHO IS PRESENT / WHO IS THIS EXPRESSION FOR?**

This is a Writing derivation tool. It does not replace Decision, Behaviour, Communication, Relationship, Memory, Motivation, Disposition or any factual resolver.

## Core non-collapses

Preserve:

- occurrence != appraisal;
- appraisal != emotion;
- emotion != mood;
- emotion != persistent Disposition;
- emotion != Motivation;
- emotion != Goal;
- emotion != Decision;
- emotion != Relationship state;
- emotion != morality;
- experienced emotion != outward expression;
- outward expression != disclosure of private truth;
- same occurrence != same emotion across participants;
- same emotion != same expression across characters;
- emotional intensity != causal importance;
- temporary emotional residue != permanent personality change;
- richer expression != invented history;
- more emotion != louder prose.

A frightened person may advance.

An angry person may remain careful.

A grieving person may joke.

A relieved person may still be resentful.

A person may care deeply and still refuse.

A person may look calm while panicking.

Emotion changes the decision context. It does not own the decision.

# The affective-expression stack

## 1. TRIGGER

Ask:

> **What actually happened?**

Start from committed or currently observable fact, not from the emotion you want to write.

Examples:

- a Battle was lost;
- a mentor criticised a choice;
- a teammate was injured;
- an identity was revealed;
- a rival praised the protagonist;
- an authority refused a request;
- somebody stayed silent;
- a promise was broken;
- a route closed;
- a person unexpectedly returned.

Do not author affect first and backfill history to justify it.

---

## 2. PERCEPTION

Ask:

> **What did this character legitimately perceive, know or believe happened?**

Preserve:

- World Truth != observer Knowledge;
- suspicion != fact;
- testimony != Truth automatically;
- Memory != World Truth;
- public expression != private truth.

A character may react to:

- what they saw;
- what they heard;
- what they inferred;
- what they remembered;
- what they were told;
- what they mistakenly believe.

Writing must know which one is operating.

A character cannot appraise information they do not possess.

---

## 3. APPRAISAL

Ask:

> **What does this occurrence mean to this person, right now?**

Use current:

- Knowledge / Belief;
- Memory;
- Relationship history;
- obligations;
- identity / values;
- expectations;
- goals / motives;
- perceived control;
- threat / opportunity;
- age and life stage;
- institutional role;
- prior similar experience;
- recent pressure.

Do not assign emotion from scene genre alone.

The same criticism from a stranger, a rival, a parent and a respected mentor does not mean the same thing.

The same failure in public and in private does not mean the same thing.

The same injury to a teammate and an enemy does not mean the same thing.

Event Truth may be shared while meaning remains observer-relative.

---

## 4. AFFECTIVE PRESSURE

Ask:

> **What pressures are active now?**

Mixed emotion is normal.

Examples:

- relief + anger;
- pride + embarrassment;
- fear + determination;
- affection + resentment;
- admiration + jealousy;
- grief + duty;
- satisfaction + guilt;
- hope + distrust;
- gratitude + discomfort.

Do not force every beat into one clean emotional label.

Do not require the player-facing prose to label emotion at all.

The important question is whether the pressure is causally legible from the person and situation.

---

## 5. REGULATION / DEFENCE

Ask:

> **What does this person do with the pressure?**

This is one of the strongest characterisation layers.

Possible character-specific regulation can include:

- humour;
- silence;
- bravado;
- ritual / formality;
- anger;
- withdrawal;
- physical action;
- intellectualisation;
- over-explanation;
- clipped professionalism;
- caretaking;
- confrontation;
- denial;
- deflection;
- sudden softness;
- compulsive movement;
- obsessive focus on the task.

This is not a universal taxonomy.

Use the exact character's current voice/personality anchor and canon-first research.

Affective Writing fails when everyone regulates pressure through the same:

- dry joke;
- sarcastic line;
- clenched fist;
- looking away;
- shouting;
- silence;
- therapist-like explanation;
- noble confession.

Character identity often lives in regulation more than in the emotion label.

---

## 6. IMMEDIATE WANT

Ask:

> **What do they want now?**

Immediate want may be:

- leave;
- stay;
- hide weakness;
- protect someone;
- get an answer;
- regain control;
- finish the task;
- avoid humiliation;
- confront;
- reassure;
- punish;
- repair trust;
- maintain authority;
- stop the conversation;
- be understood;
- not be understood.

Emotion may alter urgency, risk tolerance, attention or willingness to disclose.

It must not silently replace current Motivation / Goal / Decision authority.

---

## 7. WHO IS PRESENT / RELATIONSHIP MODIFIER

Ask:

> **Who is this person expressing around?**

The same internal pressure may produce very different outward behaviour around:

- a trusted friend;
- a rival;
- a parent;
- a mentor;
- the Hokage;
- a stranger;
- a subordinate;
- an enemy;
- a crowd.

Relationship may change:

- intensity;
- disclosure threshold;
- defensiveness;
- formality;
- willingness to joke;
- willingness to admit fear;
- physical distance;
- recovery speed;
- whether anger is shown or hidden.

Preserve:

> **Relationship evidence != predetermined emotional consequence.**

---

## 8. EXPRESSION CHANNEL

Choose the channel that fits the person, pressure, relationship and scene.

### Dialogue

Emotion may change:

- sentence length;
- interruption;
- repetition;
- vocabulary;
- register;
- formality;
- bluntness;
- refusal;
- evasion;
- unfinished thoughts;
- saying too much;
- saying too little;
- answering the wrong part of a question;
- humour or profanity where character-appropriate.

### Body / action

Use:

- posture;
- gaze;
- distance;
- stillness;
- movement;
- hand behaviour;
- object handling;
- stepping closer / away;
- protecting a person / object / exit;
- sitting when they usually stand;
- refusing to move;
- leaving;
- staying.

### Attention / salience

Affective pressure may change what the character notices.

Examples:

- fear may make exits salient;
- grief may make an ordinary object suddenly important;
- jealousy may make one interaction difficult to ignore;
- professional training may override emotional salience and return attention to the task.

Preserve:

> **Salience != Truth != causal importance.**

### Silence / omission

Silence can be expression when the scene makes it meaningful.

Do not use generic pause scaffolding:

- `A beat.`
- `A pause.`
- `Silence.`
- `For a moment.`

Use character-specific behaviour or omit the pause.

### Narration

Narration may carry:

- involuntary physical response;
- sensory emphasis;
- changed perception;
- timing;
- observer-safe internal texture;
- what the character cannot bring themselves to say.

Do not turn narration into an emotion-label ledger.

---

## 9. CONSEQUENCE

Expression should normally alter the lived scene when another participant perceives it.

Ask:

- did somebody respond?;
- did the relationship temperature change?;
- did somebody move closer or further away?;
- did a question get dropped?;
- did the character reveal more or less than intended?;
- was the expression misunderstood?;
- did the character regain control?;
- did the immediate task change?;
- did unresolved pressure carry forward?;

Not every emotional beat creates a persistent system fact.

But significant expression should not disappear as disposable flavour when it visibly changes the social situation.

This plugs into the existing Story loop:

`setup -> playable problem -> player action -> factual resolution -> visible consequence -> changed next situation`.

# Nonverbal turn and scene closure

Affective expression does **not** require verbal turn completion.

A laugh, chuckle, look, gesture, breath, posture change, resumed task, touch, departure, stillness or other legible action may legitimately:

- begin an interaction;
- answer;
- acknowledge;
- refuse;
- continue;
- redirect;
- de-escalate;
- close the current conversational beat;
- end the scene.

Preserve:

> **Spoken line != required spoken response.**

> **Last spoken line != required final beat.**

> **Conversation closure != verbal closure.**

> **Nonverbal response may carry the interaction forward or finish it.**

The acceptance question is:

> **Did the interaction naturally complete its current beat?**

not:

> **Did every spoken line receive a spoken reply?**

This matters especially in affective Writing because a character's regulation / defence may make a nonverbal response more truthful than another line.

Examples:

- laughter can acknowledge without explaining;
- resumed work can close a challenge;
- a look can refuse disclosure;
- stepping away can end negotiation;
- touch can answer reassurance;
- a breath and changed posture can show regained control;
- leaving can be the consequence;
- silence can close the scene when the cause is already legible.

Do not append dialogue merely to balance conversational turns.

An extra spoken reply is a Writing defect when it adds no new:

- intent;
- Knowledge;
- Relationship movement;
- action;
- pressure;
- consequence;
- character texture.

The inverse also applies: interaction may begin before the first spoken line through blocking, eye contact, avoidance, offered objects, visible injuries, entering/leaving space or resumed work.

This is **not** a default preference for nonverbal endings. It removes forced conversational bookkeeping.

# Emotional continuity

New scene != emotional reset.

After:

- Battle;
- humiliation;
- death;
- injury;
- betrayal;
- argument;
- escape;
- affection;
- fear;
- revelation;
- public failure;
- reconciliation;

ask what residue survives.

Then ask what changes it:

- time;
- safety;
- new information;
- reassurance;
- exhaustion;
- duty;
- a new threat;
- another relationship;
- successful regulation.

Preserve the opposite guard:

> **Emotional continuity != permanent mood lock.**

Do not keep a character frozen in one affect merely because the previous scene was intense.

# Canon-first emotional research

For canon-derived characters, Canon Research First remains binding.

Research should not stop at adjectives and catchphrases.

Where relevant, seek evidence for behaviour under:

- anger;
- fear / stress;
- shame / embarrassment;
- affection / care;
- grief / loss;
- relief;
- rivalry;
- authority pressure;
- recovery after loss of composure;
- emotional contradiction.

Then derive current expression from:

`canon emotional behaviour`
+
`correct age / era`
+
`current SC divergence`
+
`Relationship history`
+
`current Knowledge / Memory`
+
`current appraisal`
+
`character-specific regulation`.

Explicit SC divergence remains controlling.

Example: Academy Hinata's current Road-to-Ninja-leaning assertiveness must not be overwritten by a generic mainline timid presentation.

# Private emotion != public expression

A character may:

- hide fear;
- perform confidence;
- suppress affection;
- express anger instead of shame;
- sound indifferent while carefully protecting someone;
- threaten because they are frightened;
- joke because vulnerability is intolerable.

Therefore:

> **Expression is evidence of internal state, not automatic proof of internal state.**

Other characters do not gain private truth from expression automatically.

Narration must respect current POV / observer authority.

---

# Anti-melodrama / intensity calibration

More emotion != better Writing.

Intensity should be proportionate to:

- perceived stakes;
- relationship relevance;
- surprise / novelty;
- threat / loss;
- identity pressure;
- perceived control / helplessness;
- public vs private context;
- age;
- exhaustion;
- accumulated stress;
- institutional role.

Do not use:

- shouting;
- tears;
- trembling;
- profanity;
- dramatic narration;
- confessional dialogue;

as generic quality markers.

Quiet emotion can be stronger when it fits the person.

This is optional authoring scaffolding.

It is not a runtime schema and must never appear on the player-facing surface.

# Acceptance test

Before approving an important emotional scene, ask:

1. Is the reaction derived from what the character actually perceived?
2. Is the meaning specific to this person rather than assigned by plot genre?
3. Are mixed pressures allowed where appropriate?
4. Does regulation match the character rather than a project-wide house style?
5. Is the immediate want visible in behaviour even if never stated?
6. Is expression carried through a suitable channel rather than emotion labels alone?
7. Would this person plausibly express the same pressure differently around somebody else?
8. Is private truth protected?
9. Does the scene inherit relevant emotional residue from what happened before?
10. Does the next scene avoid treating the affect as permanently fixed?
11. Has Writing avoided inventing Knowledge, history, motive or Relationship facts?
12. Is intensity proportionate?
13. Could another named character be swapped into this beat without major change? If yes, the expression is probably too generic.
14. Can the player feel the reaction without being told an abstract emotional state?
15. Did Writing avoid forcing a spoken reply merely because the preceding beat was dialogue?
16. If the beat ends nonverbally, does the action naturally complete the current interaction rather than feel abruptly truncated?

If several fail, the affective expression is not Writing-complete.
