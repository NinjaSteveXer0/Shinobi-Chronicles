# Shinobi Chronicles — Story Performance, Character Voice, Emotion, Cadence and Expressive Prose Continuity Standard

**Date:** 2026-09-14  
**Owner:** Writing / Story — Konoha  
**Status:** **STEPHEN-DIRECTED BINDING PLAYER-FACING WRITING QUALITY STANDARD**  
**Primary priority:** **FINISH SHINOBI CHRONICLES ALPHA**

## 1. Purpose

This standard exists to prevent a recurring successor-chat drift: Story structure, branch causality and implementation detail may remain strong while the actual prose loses character personality, emotional performance, linguistic texture, cadence, physical reaction and narrative atmosphere.

Shinobi Chronicles must not read like a semantic contract with dialogue attached.

Visual presentation helps, but visuals do not replace expressive prose.

Canonical principle:

> **The player should feel that people are living through the Chronicle, not that the Chronicle is being reported to them.**

This standard extends, and does not replace:

- `Dialogue_Naturalism_Character_Age_Voice_and_CE_Underlayer_Authority_2026-09-11.md`;
- the Interactive Scene / screen-first authoring doctrine;
- the Structured Story Realisation Package workflow;
- current Knowledge, autonomy, branch-isolation and approval-gate authority.

It creates no new Story facts and does not author resolver outcomes.

---

## 2. Core writing target

A player-facing scene should normally combine, where appropriate:

- environmental / temporal narration;
- character movement and body language;
- emotional reaction;
- internal thought or perception where POV legitimately supports it;
- character-specific dialogue;
- natural conversational cadence;
- relationship-sensitive response;
- silence, hesitation, interruption or refusal where appropriate;
- aftermath / reaction after meaningful actions;
- connective prose between decisions rather than abrupt state-summary jumps.

Not every beat requires every element.

The target is **performed scene prose**, not maximum word count.

Preserve:

> **detail != bloat**

> **cinematic != overwritten**

> **visual support != prose replacement**

> **semantic correctness != expressive completeness**

---

## 3. Character personality must survive across chats

Every recurring named character should have a stable Writing-owned voice/personality baseline that successor chats consume before substantial dialogue authoring.

At minimum, the baseline should capture:

```text
CHARACTER / REPRESENTATION:
AGE / LIFE STAGE:
BASE TEMPERAMENT:
SOCIAL / AUTHORITY POSTURE:
DEFAULT CADENCE:
VOCABULARY / FORMALITY:
HUMOUR STYLE:
SWEARING / INTENSIFIERS:
CONFIDENCE / HESITATION PATTERN:
EMOTIONAL LEAKAGE:
BODY-LANGUAGE TENDENCIES:
SILENCE / WITHHOLDING STYLE:
WHEN ANGRY:
WHEN AFRAID:
WHEN EMBARRASSED:
WHEN EXCITED:
WHEN HURT / GRIEVING:
WITH TRUSTED PEOPLE:
WITH RIVALS:
WITH AUTHORITY:
DO NOT WRITE THIS CHARACTER AS:
CURRENT CHRONICLE-SPECIFIC MODIFIERS:
```

This is a compact authoring anchor, not a deterministic personality algorithm.

A character must remain recognisable while still changing with age, relationships, stress, fear, success, injury, grief, embarrassment, intimacy, authority context and Recorded History.

Canonical rule:

> **Personality continuity does not mean repeating catchphrases. It means the same person remains recognisable under changing circumstances.**

---

## 4. Dialogue must carry identity

Named characters should not sound interchangeable.

Where practical, a reader should be able to infer who is speaking from cadence, vocabulary, confidence, rhythm, humour, restraint and emotional behaviour even before reading the speaker label.

Dialogue may use:

- contractions;
- fragments;
- interruptions;
- false starts;
- blunt answers;
- evasions;
- pauses;
- repetition under stress;
- unfinished thoughts;
- sharp tonal changes;
- profanity where character/situation supports it;
- silence instead of a spoken answer.

Do not force all characters into polished full sentences.

Do not spread one successful character's cadence across the cast.

Do not make every smart character sound academic.

Do not make every funny character quippy.

Do not make every emotional character verbally explain their emotion.

---

## 5. Written intonation and cadence

Player-facing prose should communicate how a line lands, not merely what information it contains.

Use sentence rhythm and punctuation naturally to convey:

- urgency;
- disbelief;
- embarrassment;
- restraint;
- sarcasm;
- anger;
- fear;
- exhaustion;
- excitement;
- uncertainty;
- affection;
- awkwardness;
- deadpan delivery.

Allowed tools include, where they fit the voice:

- short sentence fragments;
- longer breathless sentences;
- dashes / interruptions;
- ellipses for genuine hesitation rather than decoration;
- repetition;
- sudden sentence shortening;
- strategic silence;
- contrast between narration rhythm and dialogue rhythm.

Do not turn punctuation into a gimmick.

Do not flatten every line into neutral subtitle English.

---

## 6. Emotion should be performed, not merely labelled

Prefer showing emotional state through character-specific behaviour and delivery rather than repeatedly writing labels such as `he was angry`, `she was nervous`, `he was happy`.

Emotion may appear through:

- posture;
- eye movement;
- hands;
- breath;
- facial reaction;
- physical distance;
- abrupt movement;
- avoiding eye contact;
- over-talking;
- under-talking;
- jokes used defensively;
- silence;
- impulsive action;
- changed vocabulary;
- changed sentence length;
- inappropriate laughter;
- restrained reaction from disciplined characters.

The same emotion should not look identical on every character.

Example principle:

> **Kakashi being embarrassed should not automatically read like Obito being embarrassed.**

> **Anko being angry should not automatically read like Hinata being angry.**

---

## 7. Narration must perform time, place and transition

Narration should do more than report facts between dialogue windows.

It may establish:

- weather;
- light;
- time of day;
- ambient village movement;
- distance;
- silence;
- crowd pressure;
- room tension;
- changes in pace;
- travel transitions;
- aftermath;
- moments where characters have nothing useful to say.

A transition such as day becoming dusk should feel like part of the scene rather than an internal state label.

The Scene Board can show place visually, but prose should still carry **temporal and emotional movement** when that movement matters.

Do not redundantly describe every visible object merely because it exists in the backdrop.

Use narration to provide what the image cannot fully supply: rhythm, attention, emotion, perception, timing and change.

---

## 8. Internal thought and perception

Internal thought may be used where the current Story perspective legitimately supports it.

Use it to reveal:

- uncertainty;
- private embarrassment;
- suspicion;
- fear;
- temptation;
- tactical thought;
- conflict between what a character says and thinks;
- emotional processing that would not naturally be spoken aloud.

Do not use internal thought to leak World Truth the character does not know.

Do not use thoughts as an exposition dump for CE state.

Preserve:

> **internal thought = observer-relative experience, not omniscient authority**

---

## 9. Relationship-sensitive expression

Characters must not speak to everyone the same way.

Dialogue and behaviour should change legitimately with:

- familiarity;
- trust;
- rivalry;
- resentment;
- admiration;
- authority difference;
- fear;
- debt;
- embarrassment;
- shared history;
- recent conflict;
- attraction / affection where authored;
- institutional role.

The semantic meaning may remain the same while the expression changes dramatically.

A warning to a stranger should not automatically sound like a warning to a friend.

A refusal to a Hokage should not automatically sound like a refusal to a Genin teammate.

---

## 10. Scene reaction completeness

Meaningful actions should create visible human reaction where relevant participants perceive them.

After an important event, Writing should consider:

- who saw it;
- who heard it;
- who understood it;
- who is shocked;
- who hides a reaction;
- who misunderstands it;
- who speaks;
- who stays silent;
- who physically changes position;
- whether the emotional temperature of the room changes.

This must remain Knowledge-safe.

Do not force every participant to comment on every event.

But do not let major actions occur in a populated scene while everyone else behaves like inert UI furniture.

---

## 11. Humour and dramatic contrast

Humour is welcome when it belongs to the characters and moment.

A scene may move from tension to absurdity, affection to danger, confidence to embarrassment, or gravity to dry reaction if the transition is character-earned.

Do not make the entire cast joke in the same register.

Do not flatten serious consequences with mandatory quips.

Do not prohibit comedy merely because a scene is structurally important.

The strongest Story can carry both sincerity and humour without either cancelling the other.

---

## 12. Visuals and prose are complementary

The current coded UI / Interactive Scene Board direction does not reduce the need for expressive writing.

Use visuals for:

- place;
- participant presence;
- speaker focus;
- visible object state;
- obvious staging;
- broad atmosphere.

Use prose for:

- emotional timing;
- subtle physical reaction;
- voice;
- cadence;
- internal perception;
- relational tension;
- temporal movement;
- implication;
- silence;
- what changes between frames.

Canonical:

> **Do not narrate the entire picture. Do not expect the picture to perform the entire scene.**

---

## 13. Anti-drift acceptance test

Before a substantial scene or Story package is treated as player-facing complete, Writing should run this check:

1. **Speaker-swap test:** if two character names can be swapped and most lines still work unchanged, voice differentiation is probably too weak.
2. **Emotion test:** can the reader feel the emotional state without relying only on emotion labels?
3. **Physicality test:** do important reactions exist in bodies / movement / silence, not only speech?
4. **Cadence test:** does spoken language have natural rhythm appropriate to age, personality and current stress?
5. **Narration test:** does narration carry time, atmosphere, movement or perception rather than merely restating semantic state?
6. **Relationship test:** would this character deliver the same line differently to a different person? If yes, has the actual relationship been reflected?
7. **Knowledge test:** does any expressive flourish accidentally reveal facts the speaker/POV cannot know?
8. **Voice-contamination test:** has another character inherited Menma/Anko/Kakashi/etc. cadence because it was convenient?
9. **Reaction test:** do consequential actions receive plausible participant/world reactions where perceived?
10. **Scene-memory test:** after reading, can a player remember a lived moment, reaction or exchange — not merely the branch outcome?

If the scene is semantically excellent but fails several of these, it is **not player-facing Writing complete**.

## 14. Successor-chat boot requirement

A new Writing chat must not rely on its own default literary style.

Before substantial player-facing authoring it should consume:

1. the current Konoha Writing successor deep-sweep audit;
2. this continuity standard;
3. `Dialogue_Naturalism_Character_Age_Voice_and_CE_Underlayer_Authority_2026-09-11.md`;
4. current canon-first/personality authority for materially speaking canon-derived characters;
5. the exact current Story / branch authority being authored;
6. any existing character-specific voice anchor relevant to the scene.

If a required recurring character lacks a sufficiently clear compact voice anchor, Writing should create one before producing a large volume of dialogue rather than allowing the current chat's generic prose tendencies to define that character by accident.

## 15. Relationship to #18

Issue #18 remains the broader future personality/contextual-expression schema for later CE integration.

This standard does **not** activate unrestricted runtime-generated dialogue or make #18 an Alpha architecture blocker.

For Alpha, the immediate requirement is simpler:

> **Writing uses durable voice anchors and expressive-performance acceptance checks so player-facing authored prose remains consistent across successor chats.**

Later CE contextual-expression work may consume these anchors under its own authority.

---

## 16. Approval boundary

This standard strengthens **how approved Story is performed**.

It does not grant Writing permission to wholesale replace approved Story, geography, participants, outcomes or important choreography without the existing approval process.

Preserve:

> **quality standard != blanket replacement consent**

> **expressive rewrite != Chronicle rewrite**

> **stronger personality != new factual Knowledge**

> **better prose != authority to change resolver outcomes**

---

## Final lock

> **Characters must sound, move, react and think like distinct people.**

> **Narration must carry atmosphere, transition, attention and emotional rhythm rather than merely reporting state.**

> **Dialogue must preserve age, personality, relationship, situation and written intonation.**

> **Visuals support the performance; they do not replace it.**

> **If a successor chat can preserve plot detail but loses the voices of the people living through it, Writing continuity has failed.**
