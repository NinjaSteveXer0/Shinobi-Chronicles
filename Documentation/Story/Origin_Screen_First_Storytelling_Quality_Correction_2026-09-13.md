**Status:** **BINDING QUALITY CORRECTION — SUPERSEDES ANY CLAIM THAT THE FIRST #170 MODERNIZATION PASS CLOSED ORIGIN PLAYER-FACING WRITING**

Stephen's installed-browser review of Academy Kakashi proves the first #170 modernization pass did not reach current Shinobi Chronicles Story quality. The prose is mechanically clearer, but several live beats still read as a designer explaining semantic state rather than a scene happening to Kakashi.

Observed abstractions include `confirmed packet`, `apparent carrier`, `reconstruct the operation`, `What does Kakashi think mattered most?`, and a closing character beat reduced to `Recovering the objective.` Those phrases are semantically usable internally; they are not sufficient production storytelling by themselves.

> **Every Origin beat must read as a scene first and a semantic carrier second.**

The expression layer must translate closed semantics through what the character notices, body language and facial reaction, eye movement and posture, environment interaction, interruption and silence, contractions and natural spoken cadence where character-appropriate, relationship-specific dialogue, autonomous NPC action, character-specific interpretation, immediate dramatic choices, and compact screen-sized prose.

Preserve all existing semantic IDs, choice IDs, factual outcomes, source-occurrence/consequence bindings, World Truth, observer Knowledge, Battle callers/returns, PL/Stats/Rank/Progression, ownership/acquisition/rewards, Origin premise/continuity, and fail-closed boundaries. This is a **screen-first expression rewrite**, not an Origin redesign.

Canon Research First #134 remains binding. Named characters must not merely receive more colourful prose; physical reactions, rhythm and decision framing must be recognisably theirs.

> **If the body language and dialogue of two Origin characters can be swapped without making the scene feel wrong, the rewrite has failed.**

Academy Kakashi is the immediate benchmark. His Origin must feel like a young prodigy actually conducting a field-retrieval assessment: he notices tiny inconsistencies before naming conclusions; uses silence deliberately; does not narrate an evaluation rubric to himself; experiences simultaneous pressures as concrete action rather than event labels; debriefs through questions, clipped answers, pauses and physical evidence; and ends in a genuine living-Sakumo relationship scene rather than an abstract reflection survey.
