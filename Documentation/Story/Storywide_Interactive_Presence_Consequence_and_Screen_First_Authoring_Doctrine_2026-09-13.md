# Shinobi Chronicles — Storywide Interactive Presence, Consequence + Screen-First Authoring Doctrine

**Date:** 2026-09-13  
**Owner:** Writing — Konoha  
**Status:** **BINDING STORYWIDE AUTHORING DOCTRINE — ALL CURRENT/FUTURE SC STORY SURFACES**

## 1. Core player-experience rule

Shinobi Chronicles Story must make the player feel that they are **present inside events**, not reading an on-screen book or a design-summary of events.

Canonical acceptance test:

> **The player should feel: “I’m here. I saw this. I chose this. They reacted. Something changed because of me.”**

Reject the experience:

> **“I clicked through paragraphs explaining what my character supposedly did.”**

This doctrine applies project-wide, not only to Origins or Arc 1.

It governs:

- all Academy Origin prologues;
- Arc 1–8 Main Story;
- Canon and Alt route realizations;
- Rogue, Leader / Take-the-Throne and Non-Conformance realizations;
- hidden-area introduction / side stories;
- Infinite Tsukuyomi introduction Story;
- Great-Nation travel Story;
- future Beta village campaigns;
- village Canon + Alt campaigns;
- the future All-Route / Ōtsutsuki convergence;
- any later Shinobi Chronicles Story content unless a more specific authority explicitly supersedes it.

---

## 2. Default interactive Story loop

The preferred causal loop is:

> **setup → playable problem → player action → system/world resolution → visible consequence → changed next situation**

Meaning:

### Setup
Establish the immediate place, people, observable state, tension and bounded Knowledge needed to understand the moment.

### Playable problem
Give the player something concrete to deal with, not merely something to read about.

### Player action
The player chooses/commits **intent** where genuine agency exists.

Player intent != guaranteed success.

### System / World resolution
The correct authority resolves what factually happens:

- Story;
- Battle;
- World / Events;
- Skills / capability;
- Acquisition;
- Progression;
- or another legitimate resolver.

Writing must not narratively pre-win a result that belongs to another resolver.

### Visible consequence
The player must normally see or experience an immediate consequence of the resolved action through the Story surface where reasonably observable.

Examples include:

- somebody reacts;
- dialogue changes;
- an injury or destruction is visible;
- a person escapes;
- evidence is gained/lost;
- a relationship tightens or fractures;
- a route opens/closes;
- a crowd responds;
- an NPC acts independently;
- the player is challenged, thanked, distrusted, contradicted or ignored;
- the physical scene changes.

Do not reduce meaningful consequence to an invisible ledger flag when the scene can legitimately show something.

### Changed next situation
The next beat/Section should inherit the factual result.

The Story should not snap back to the same unchanged state merely because a choice window has closed.

---

## 3. Explicit anti-pattern

Avoid:

> **setup → choice → flavour text → same situation**

A choice is not meaningful merely because four buttons existed.

Different options may sometimes converge on the same later macro event, but the Chronicle should still preserve and, where appropriate, visibly express meaningful differences in:

- Knowledge;
- relationship state;
- evidence;
- trust;
- hostility;
- cost;
- injuries;
- access;
- participant willingness;
- political interpretation;
- future eligibility;
- available methods;
- or other causal state.

Convergence != erasure.

---

## 4. Screen-first scene performance

Story should be **performed**, not explained.

Use, where natural:

- body language;
- facial expression;
- eye-line;
- posture;
- gesture;
- breath;
- pauses;
- laughter;
- scoffs;
- sighs;
- physical distance / movement;
- environmental response;
- interruptions;
- silence;
- subtext;
- reaction beats after player action.

Emotion and attitude should often be conveyed through what characters **do while speaking**, not a narrator naming the emotion.

Example form:

> Mikoto threw her hands up. “I always knew you were insane.”  
> Menma laughed out loud. “It’s the only way to be!”

The physical reaction establishes attitude; the dialogue carries character voice; the exchange has cadence without explanatory narration such as `Mikoto was frustrated but affectionate`.

Do not mechanically attach a gesture to every sentence. Stillness, restraint or formal speech may itself be characterisation.

---

## 5. Spoken-language rule

Dialogue should normally sound like actual speech from that character.

Natural contractions are the default where the character would use them:

- `it’s`;
- `don’t`;
- `can’t`;
- `you’re`;
- `I’ll`;
- etc.

Uncontracted forms such as `it is`, `I will`, `do not` remain valid when they serve:

- factual precision;
- emphasis;
- threat;
- formality;
- ceremonial speech;
- executive/military posture;
- character-specific verbal habit;
- or another deliberate cadence choice.

This is a natural-voice rule, not a mechanical grammar ban.

Also use character-appropriate:

- sentence length;
- vocabulary;
- fragments;
- interruptions;
- profanity;
- humour;
- verbal hesitation;
- directness/indirection;
- intensifiers;
- rhythm.

If lines from several named characters can be swapped without noticeably changing the scene, characterisation has failed.

---

## 6. Intonation without voice acting

Because much of SC is text-presented, prose and dialogue must carry implied intonation through:

- wording;
- punctuation;
- sentence length;
- fragments;
- pauses;
- repetition;
- interruption;
- physical reaction;
- what a character refuses to say;
- and what they do before/after a line.

Do not compensate by writing stage directions for every syllable.

The player should be able to infer whether a line is amused, angry, wary, mocking, frightened, formal, uncertain or affectionate from the scene performance itself.

---

## 7. Player agency requires meaningful decisions, not constant decisions

Preserve the existing doctrine:

> **Player agency requires meaningful decisions, not constant decisions.**

Do not turn every connective sentence into a button.

Non-interactive beats are valid when they:

- establish physical context;
- allow NPCs to act autonomously;
- show the consequence of a prior action;
- communicate legitimately known information;
- move between meaningful decision points;
- or let a major emotional beat breathe.

A scene can remain interactive without presenting choices every card.

---

## 8. NPC autonomy is part of interactivity

The player is not the only actor in the scene.

NPCs should:

- initiate actions;
- interrupt;
- disagree;
- refuse;
- help;
- make mistakes;
- protect their own interests;
- pursue obligations;
- interpret events differently;
- act from their own Knowledge/relationships/personality/capabilities.

Protagonist choice != party command.

An NPC independently changing the situation is not loss of player agency; it is evidence that the world contains other people.

---

## 9. Knowledge and observer boundaries

Presence and immersion must never create omniscience.

Preserve:

- World Truth != observer Knowledge;
- suspicion != fact;
- testimony != Truth automatically;
- NPC Knowledge != protagonist Knowledge;
- parallel occurrence != protagonist occurrence;
- body language may support inference but not magically reveal hidden biography/motive.

Screen-first writing should make bounded Knowledge feel natural rather than explaining the Knowledge model to the player.

---

## 10. Consequence should be dramatized before it is abstracted

Where the player can observe a consequence, Story should usually show it first.

Bad player-facing form:

> `Relationship trust decreased.`

Better Story form:

> Hinata’s hand stopped halfway to Menma’s sleeve. She let it fall.  
> “You could’ve told me.”

The underlying system may still record relationship evidence, but the player experiences the human event.

Likewise:

- do not say `custody changed` when a person can physically take the prisoner away;
- do not say `the route became unavailable` when guards can shut the gate;
- do not say `the crowd became hostile` when the crowd can move, shout or turn away;
- do not say `the NPC disagreed` when the NPC can disagree in their own voice.

---

## 11. Story / resolver seams

When a decision requires another system, preserve the seam rather than turning the Story into a fake resolver.

Preferred form:

> Story setup → player intent → exact resolver → factual result → same Story context resumes → visible aftermath → next changed situation.

Examples:

- Story → Battle → Story aftermath;
- Story → World opportunity/event → Story callback;
- Story → capability check / specialist route → factual consequence;
- Story → Acquisition decision → ownership/custody consequence.

Battle victory != Story conclusion automatically.

Mission completion != Progression automatically.

Player choice != factual success automatically.

---

## 12. Nested loops across Sections and Missions

The interactive loop can repeat several times inside one Section or Mission.

Example:

> setup → suspicious contact → player approach → NPC reaction → new evidence → Battle decision → Battle resolver → visible aftermath → pursuit choice → World resolution → changed relationship → Section close

This is valid.

Section != one choice.

Section != one scene.

Section != one system call.

The 12×12 structure remains a pacing/addressability framework, not a mandate to flatten Story into equal text blocks.

---

## 13. Zoom levels

Not every moment requires identical dramatic density.

### High-detail / cinematic zoom
Use stronger scene performance for:

- major confrontation;
- betrayal;
- reveal;
- death;
- romance/intimacy;
- political showdown;
- founder encounter;
- major Menma/Kurama turn;
- important Origin/teammate beat;
- major Battle return;
- final/endgame decision.

### Compressed connective zoom
Travel, routine investigation, repeated process and connective conversation may be compressed.

But compression must still preserve:

- who is present;
- what happened;
- what changed;
- distinct voices where dialogue matters;
- and the next playable situation.

Compressed != dry report.

---

## 14. Applies to all forward route families

The same Story quality law applies regardless of political route.

- Canon Route does not become a lore novel.
- Alt Route does not become a different set of exposition cards.
- Rogue must feel lived outside institutions.
- Leader / Take-the-Throne must make governing decisions produce visible human/political consequences.
- Non-Conformance must express its unusual Chronicle state through scenes, reactions and changed opportunities rather than a route label.

Route divergence should be **experienced**, not merely announced.

---

## 15. Hidden-area / challenge introductions

Short Story introductions to hidden areas must also feel playable/present.

Discovery should involve a real situation, clue, relationship, risk, choice or consequence where appropriate rather than:

> `You discovered Ryūchi Cave. Continue.`

The Infinite Tsukuyomi World remains a challenge realm rather than CE-generated dream-life Story, but its short introduction must still obey this screen-first doctrine before repeatable challenge gameplay begins.

---

## 16. Beta village campaigns + All-Route

Future village stories must inherit this doctrine from their first playable beat.

Different village campaigns should feel different because:

- people speak differently;
- political assumptions differ;
- environments behave differently;
- the player’s relationships differ;
- consequences alter local/world state;
- NPCs pursue village-specific interests.

The future All-Route / Ōtsutsuki convergence must consume the actual histories created by those campaigns and present consequences through lived scenes, not erase them into one generic world-unity cutscene.

---

## 17. Acceptance checklist

A Story sequence is not approved merely because its facts are correct or its prose is grammatically strong.

Before player-facing approval, ask:

1. **Can the player locate themselves physically/emotionally in the scene?**
2. **Is there a concrete playable problem when agency is appropriate?**
3. **Does the player choose intent rather than pre-authored success?**
4. **Does the correct system/world authority resolve factual outcome?**
5. **Can the player see/feel a consequence where it is reasonably observable?**
6. **Is the next situation actually changed by what happened?**
7. **Do NPCs behave like autonomous people rather than dialogue dispensers?**
8. **Do named characters sound recognisably different?**
9. **Does dialogue sound spoken, including natural contractions/cadence where appropriate?**
10. **Are body language, facial reaction, silence, gesture or spatial behaviour carrying some emotional/subtextual work?**
11. **Are Knowledge limits preserved?**
12. **Is the scene interactive without being choice-spam?**
13. **Has system/contract terminology stayed off the player-facing Story surface?**
14. **Could the player describe what happened as an event they participated in, rather than a paragraph they read?**

If the answer to #14 is no, the Story presentation is not finished.

---

# 19. Final project rule

> **Shinobi Chronicles is not an on-screen book with occasional buttons. It is a Chronicle the player inhabits.**

> **Story should happen around the player, react to the player, resolve through the game’s systems, and leave the world different afterward.**

Design closed != implemented != runtime validated != Golden GREEN.
