# Shinobi Chronicles — Storywide Diction, Cadence, Personality and Emotion Benchmark Lock

**Date:** 2026-09-14  
**Owner:** Stephen / Writing / Story — Konoha  
**Status:** **BINDING STORYWIDE WRITING BENCHMARK / ACCEPTANCE LAW — RETROACTIVE**  
**Primary priority:** **FINISH SHINOBI CHRONICLES ALPHA**

## 1. Purpose

Stephen has approved the Academy Kakashi **Uchiha Police Force** branch expression below as the benchmark for **all future Shinobi Chronicles scenes, stories and arcs**.

This lock exists because semantic correctness, branch causality and factual completeness are necessary but are **not sufficient** for player-facing Writing completion.

A scene can be structurally correct and still fail Shinobi Chronicles if successor-chat drift removes the established authorial voice or flattens characters into functional dialogue delivery.

The mandatory player-facing quality layers are:

1. **Diction** — the actual words belong to the speaker, their age, role, relationships and current state.
2. **Cadence** — sentence length, rhythm, interruption, silence, pauses and timing belong to the speaker and moment.
3. **Personality** — the character remains recognisable without requiring explanatory labels or generic archetype prose.
4. **Emotion** — feeling and subtext are performed through voice, expression, posture, movement, hesitation, silence and reaction rather than merely named.

These four layers operate alongside existing Story causality, Knowledge, autonomy, resolver, branch-isolation and Scene Board authority.

Canonical shorthand:

> **Facts decide what happened. Diction, Cadence, Personality and Emotion decide whether the player actually lived it with these people.**

## 4. Mandatory successor-chat continuity rule

A new Writing chat must not author substantial player-facing Story from its own default prose personality.

Before substantial Story authoring, a successor Writing workspace must recover and consume:

- this benchmark;
- the broader expressive-prose continuity standard;
- the current character-specific voice/personality anchors;
- the exact current branch/Story authority;
- relevant Recorded History and observer Knowledge.

Switching chats must not cause the game to sound as though it changed writers.

The assistant/chat personality is **not** the game's authorial voice.

Canonical rule:

> **Chat succession may change the tool doing the writing. It must not change who the characters are or how Shinobi Chronicles sounds.**

## 5. Character separation requirement

Named characters must remain distinguishable through combinations of:

- vocabulary;
- sentence length;
- rhythm;
- directness or evasiveness;
- humour style;
- emotional leakage;
- silence;
- posture;
- eye-line;
- physical habits;
- confidence;
- social posture;
- relationship-specific phrasing;
- what they refuse to say.

If lines can be swapped between named characters without materially changing the scene, the scene has failed the benchmark.

Examples illustrated by the approved benchmark:

- **Kakashi** does not explain his intelligence; he shows it through observation, correction, precision, stillness and clipped answers.
- **Pakkun** has his own older, dry cadence and autonomy; he is not a generic joke dispenser.
- **ANBU** may use silence as pressure; controlled does not mean inert and professional does not mean exposition-heavy.
- **Minato** remains calm and economical, but his reaction, amusement, thought and concern are visible rather than flattened into a Hokage information terminal.
- institutional characters such as the **Uchiha Police officer** behave as people with their own suspicion, responsibility and professional judgement rather than as exposition interfaces.

## 6. Intelligence, Knowledge and subtext

Preserve:

> **World Truth != observer Knowledge != presentation.**

Do not make a clever character explain why they are clever when the player can watch them notice, correct, wait, compare, infer or act.

Do not have dialogue verbalise CE contracts merely to prove semantic compliance.

Do not use expressive prose to leak hidden World Truth.

Silence, uncertainty, mistaken inference and incomplete statements are allowed when character-appropriate.

For Kakashi specifically, preserve the existing benchmark principle:

> **Never explain Kakashi's intelligence when the player can watch him use it.**

---

## 7. Emotion and physical performance

Consequential scenes must not degrade into:

`Actor A line -> Actor B line -> Actor A line`

with no visible human response.

Use character-specific combinations of:

- eye movement;
- facial change;
- posture;
- breath;
- hands;
- movement toward/away;
- hesitation;
- interruption;
- silence;
- laughter;
- scoffs;
- sighs;
- tension release;
- refusal to respond;
- environmental/object interaction.

This is not a requirement to attach a gesture to every sentence. Stillness may itself be performance.

The player should be able to feel the emotional temperature change without a narrator repeatedly naming emotions.

---

## 9. Writing-complete acceptance gate

A substantial player-facing Story unit is not Writing-complete until it passes all of the following:

1. **Diction:** the words sound like the actual speaker, not generic polished dialogue.
2. **Cadence:** rhythm, sentence length, pauses, interruption and silence fit speaker + moment.
3. **Personality:** named characters remain recognisable and non-swappable.
4. **Emotion:** emotional state/subtext is felt through performance rather than only labelled.
5. **Physicality:** consequential beats include appropriate visible reaction, movement, stillness or spatial change.
6. **Autonomy:** NPCs behave as themselves rather than as extensions of protagonist intent.
7. **Knowledge safety:** no prose flourish invents hidden Knowledge.
8. **Voice-contamination safety:** one successful cadence is not spread across the cast.
9. **Semantic safety:** rewriting does not silently change closed facts/resolver outcomes.
10. **Scene-memory:** the player can remember a lived exchange/reaction/moment, not merely the branch state.
11. **Screen-first fit:** prose and Scene Board complement each other rather than either becoming redundant.
12. **Successor-chat continuity:** the scene still belongs to the same game even if authored in a later workspace.

If factual correctness is GREEN but these expression criteria are RED, **Writing remains RED**.

# 10. Canonical approved benchmark exemplar

The following Academy Kakashi branch expression was explicitly approved by Stephen on 2026-09-14 as **“fucking perfect”** and locked as the benchmark for all future scenes, stories and arcs.

The purpose of preserving it here is not to force every scene to mimic Kakashi's sparse cadence. The benchmark is the **quality of character separation, natural rhythm, performed emotion, physical reaction, silence, subtext and scene flow** while retaining the specific voices of the people present.

## BRING HIM TO UCHIHA POLICE FORCE

For a moment, ANBU Marked Target says nothing.

Then his eyes shift from Kakashi to Pakkun.

Back again.

“...The Police?”

Kakashi gives a small nod.

The man's jaw tightens.

“You don't know what you're involved in.”

Kakashi's visible eye stays on him.

No answer.

He simply shortens his grip on the restraint and starts walking.

Pakkun watches the man stumble into step.

His nose twitches.

Then he pads after them.

---

## UCHIHA POLICE FORCE

The Uchiha Police Force headquarters comes into view at the end of the street.

ANBU Marked Target slows.

Only slightly.

Kakashi notices.

His grip tightens before the man can decide whether that half-step is going to become something else.

It doesn't.

An Uchiha officer at the entrance turns toward them.

His gaze passes over Kakashi first.

Then Pakkun.

Then the restrained man between them.

His posture changes.

“What's this?”

Kakashi reaches into his clothing and produces the sealed mission order.

The officer's eyes settle on the Hokage's seal.

He doesn't reach for it immediately.

“Where did you get that?”

“ANBU.”

That earns Kakashi a longer look.

The officer takes the order, checks the seal, then looks down at Kakashi again.

“You're working for ANBU?”

“No.”

A beat.

“They gave me a job.”

Pakkun's ears twitch.

The officer looks as though he has another question.

He decides against it.

His attention moves to ANBU Marked Target.

“And him?”

Kakashi turns his head slightly toward the prisoner.

“He had the package.”

ANBU Marked Target's eyes cut toward him.

Kakashi catches it.

Corrects himself.

“He had it first.”

The officer notices the correction too.

“First?”

“He gave it to someone else and ran.”

Kakashi taps the package secured against him.

“I got it back.”

The officer's expression hardens.

Now he looks at ANBU Marked Target differently.

“What happened to the other man?”

“Down.”

“And this?”

His eyes flick briefly toward the package.

“Going back to ANBU.”

The officer folds the mission order once.

Carefully.

“And you're bringing **him** here.”

Kakashi nods.

ANBU Marked Target lets out a quiet breath through his nose.

“You really don't know when to leave something alone.”

Kakashi looks at him.

“Probably.”

The answer comes so flatly that, for half a second, the officer isn't quite sure what to do with it.

Pakkun looks up at Kakashi.

Then away again.

The Uchiha officer gestures toward two shinobi inside.

They move forward.

When one reaches for the prisoner, Kakashi doesn't release him immediately.

His eye follows the officer's hands.

The restraint.

The second officer taking position.

Only when the transfer is actually secure does Kakashi let go.

ANBU Marked Target rolls one shoulder where Kakashi had been holding him.

He turns his head as the officers lead him inside.

His eyes remain on Kakashi until the doorway takes him out of sight.

Kakashi watches the empty entrance for another second.

Pakkun sits beside him.

“You done staring?”

Kakashi looks down.

“Yeah.”

Pakkun gets back to his feet.

“Good.”

Kakashi turns toward the street.

The package is still his problem.

---

## RETURN TO THE ANBU CONTACT

The ANBU operative is already waiting when Kakashi returns.

Same mask.

Same stillness.

Almost the same place.

Their head moves a fraction when Pakkun walks into view beside Kakashi.

Pakkun notices.

Of course he notices.

Neither of them says anything.

Kakashi stops.

The operative holds out a hand.

Kakashi places the package into it.

The ANBU operative checks the seal.

Then:

“Report.”

Kakashi doesn't waste time.

“The target met another man.”

The operative goes still again.

“He gave him the package and ran.”

Kakashi's eye narrows slightly as he reconstructs the alley.

“A masked woman came out of the dark and went for the man carrying it.”

A pause.

“I went for the package.”

His gaze drops briefly to the package now resting in the operative's hand.

“They both came at me.”

The ANBU mask gives him nothing back.

“I put them down.”

The operative's fingers stop against the edge of the package.

Only for a moment.

Kakashi notices.

He continues.

“The masked woman was still there when I left. So was the other man.”

Not dead.

Not captured.

Not anything Kakashi didn't actually confirm.

Just there.

“I followed the first one.”

For the first time since the report started, Kakashi looks down.

At Pakkun.

Pakkun looks back.

One ear folds slightly.

Kakashi's voice stays level.

“He'd already found him.”

Pakkun's eyes narrow.

“Found?”

Kakashi pauses.

Pakkun stares at him.

Kakashi gives the smallest shrug.

“Stopped.”

Pakkun huffs.

“Better.”

The ANBU operative's attention moves between them.

No question.

No hint that they know who Pakkun is or why he was there.

Kakashi continues on his own.

“I took the target to the Uchiha Police.”

That does it.

Not much.

Just a slight turn of the ANBU operative's head.

But Kakashi sees it.

Silence stretches between them.

Pakkun scratches once behind his ear.

The operative finally speaks.

“The Police.”

“Mm.”

Another silence.

“Why?”

Kakashi looks at the blank mask.

“You told me to stop the package.”

The ANBU operative says nothing.

Kakashi waits.

“You didn't tell me to bring him back.”

The silence changes.

Barely.

The operative looks down at the recovered package.

Then toward the village.

Then back to Kakashi.

“No.”

A pause.

“We didn't.”

Kakashi doesn't smile.

But something in his posture loosens.

Just a little.

The operative tucks the package away.

“That all?”

Kakashi thinks.

Package Smuggler.

Masked Interceptor.

ANBU Marked Target.

Pakkun.

The Police.

The package.

Nothing missing.

“That's all.”

The operative gives one short nod.

“Go home.”

Kakashi turns.

---

## PAKKUN

Pakkun follows him away from the meeting point.

For half a street, neither speaks.

Then Pakkun stops.

Kakashi takes two more steps before noticing the absence beside him.

He looks back.

Pakkun is sitting in the road.

“So.”

Kakashi waits.

Pakkun studies him for a second.

“You always drag strangers halfway across the village?”

Kakashi glances back in the direction of the Police headquarters.

“He ran.”

Pakkun's mouth pulls slightly at one corner.

“Right.”

Kakashi watches him.

Pakkun gets up, stretches his front legs, then turns toward another street.

“I'm going this way.”

Kakashi gives a small nod.

Pakkun starts off.

Three steps.

Kakashi speaks.

“Thanks.”

Pakkun stops.

One ear turns back.

He doesn't look around.

“You said that already.”

Then he keeps walking.

Kakashi stands there for a moment.

His eye follows the little ninken until he disappears around the corner.

Only then does Kakashi turn for home.

---

## HOKAGE OFFICE

Later.

The Hokage Office is quiet.

Minato sits alone behind his desk.

The ANBU report lies open in front of him.

His eyes move down the page.

Package transferred.

Kakashi intervened.

Package Smuggler defeated.

Masked Interceptor defeated.

Package recovered.

Original target pursued.

Pakkun encountered.

Minato stops.

His eyebrow rises slightly.

“Pakkun?”

For the first time since opening the report, the corner of his mouth lifts.

He keeps reading.

Then reaches the final disposition.

**ANBU MARKED TARGET — TRANSFERRED TO UCHIHA POLICE CUSTODY**

The smile disappears.

Not into anger.

Into thought.

Minato leans back.

His fingers tap the paper once.

Then stop.

A quiet breath escapes him.

“You really did listen.”

His eyes return to the original mission order beside the report.

**Stop the package from falling into the wrong hands.**

Nothing about returning the target.

Nothing about ANBU custody.

Minato closes his eyes for a second.

When they open again, there's the faintest trace of amusement there.

And something more careful underneath it.

“Exactly what I said.”

He closes the report.

A soft, almost helpless sigh.

“That's going to be a problem.”

The expression that follows makes it difficult to tell whether he means that as criticism.

---

## BLACK

The Hokage Office disappears.

For a moment, there is nothing.

Then the Chronicle records what actually happened.

### ACADEMY KAKASHI

Kakashi accepted a limited ANBU assignment under Hokage authority.

He allowed the package exchange to continue long enough to see it change hands.

When the situation broke open, he chose the package.

He defeated Package Smuggler and Masked Interceptor.

He recovered the package.

He pursued ANBU Marked Target.

He met Pakkun.

He took ANBU Marked Target alive.

He chose Uchiha Police custody instead of returning him directly to ANBU.

He returned the package.

He reported everyone he encountered.

And when the mission ended, Kakashi had followed the order he was given—

not the order somebody might have expected him to hear.

**YOUR CHRONICLE BEGINS**

## 11. What this exemplar locks — and what it does not

This exemplar locks the **quality benchmark** and the approved expression of this Kakashi branch.

It does **not** mean future scenes should imitate Kakashi's clipped speech, Pakkun's dryness, ANBU stillness or Minato's exact rhythm. Doing so would itself violate the benchmark through voice contamination.

Future scenes must instead achieve equivalent specificity for the people actually present.

The benchmark therefore means:

> **Kakashi sounds like Kakashi. Pakkun sounds like Pakkun. ANBU behaves like ANBU. Minato reacts like Minato. Every other character deserves the same degree of specificity rather than being rewritten into these voices.**
